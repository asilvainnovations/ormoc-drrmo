# MODE User Manual

*For CDRRMO and EOC staff of the City of Ormoc · A plain-language guide, not for developers · Written for MODE version 1.22.0, screens checked 17 Nov 2026 · Working draft for validation*

## Start here

**MODE** means **M**anual of **O**perations for the **D**RRMO and **E**OC. It is a website and phone app that keeps our Manual of Operations in one place, helps us follow its steps, and records what we do during an emergency.

You do not need to be good with computers. If you can use Facebook or send a text message, you can use MODE. This manual shows every screen in the order you will meet it, and tells you which button to press.

### Five rules to remember

1. **MODE does not issue warnings.** For weather and earthquake warnings, use PAGASA and PHIVOLCS. MODE only gives you the links.
2. **Drafts are not official.** Anything marked *Draft* is still being written. The approved Manual of Operations and EOC Manual remain the legal documents.
3. **Never type the names of affected persons, evacuees, patients or informants.** Type counts only (for example, 12 families, 48 persons). This follows the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173).
4. **It works without internet.** What you type is saved on your device and sent later. A few big decisions (like changing the EOC alert level) need a connection.
5. **The logbook cannot be erased.** If you make a mistake, write a correction. The first entry stays on record.

### How every part of this manual looks

Each part uses the same boxes so you always know where to look:

- **Where:** how to reach the screen.
- **Who:** which roles can do it.
- **Steps:** what to press, in order. Button names are in **bold**, exactly as shown on screen.
- **If stuck:** what to do next.

> **Tip:** Hold your finger (or hover your mouse) over any button, tab or box. A small note appears that says what it does and who may use it.

## Part 1. Get in

### 1.1 Open MODE

Open your web browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari) and go to the MODE address given by the CDRRMO. You will see the **MODE** login page with the City seals and the words *Prepare Before · Respond Together · Recover Stronger · Build Safer Communities*.

### 1.2 Make a new account (first time only)

**Who:** any City staff member. Any email address works, including a personal one.

**Steps**

1. On the login page, tap **New account** (the tab on the right).
2. Fill in the boxes:
   - **Full name**, for example *Maria Santos*.
   - **Position and office** (optional), for example *LDRRMO III, CDRRMO Operations*.
   - **Email**.
   - **Password**: at least 10 characters, with letters and a number.
   - **Password again**: type the same password.
3. Tap **Create account**.
4. Open your email. Tap the confirmation link MODE sent you.

**If stuck:** no email? Check your spam folder. Back on the login page, tap **Have an account**, type your email, and tap **Send the confirmation link again**.

### 1.3 Wait for approval

After you confirm your email, MODE shows **Waiting for approval**. This is normal. Your account sees *no* City records until the system administrator gives you a **role** (see Part 14).

- You can fix your name or position under **Your details**, then **Save details**.
- Tap **Check again** to see if you were approved.
- Tell your administrator you registered. They approve you in **Settings → Users and roles**.

If you see **This account is not active**, ask the system administrator.

### 1.4 Sign in (every other time)

On the login page, stay on **Have an account**. You have two ways:

| Way | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **With a password** | Type your **Email** and **Password**, then tap the sign-in button. |
| **Without a password** | Type your **Email**, leave Password empty, tap **Email me a sign-in link**. Open the email *on the same device* and tap the link. |

### 1.5 Forgot your password

Tap **Forgot your password?**, type your email, tap **Email me a reset link**, open the email and choose a new password.

### 1.6 Put MODE on your phone

MODE is a *progressive web app*. In simple words: it can sit on your home screen like a normal app and still open without signal. If your browser offers **Install** or **Add to Home screen**, accept it. Do this once while you have a good connection, and sign in at least once so your data is saved on the phone.

### 1.7 Sign out

**Where:** **Settings** (bottom of the left menu) → **Sign out and clear this device**.

This erases MODE’s copy of the data on that device. If you still have entries that have **not been sent**, MODE warns you first. Connect and tap **Sync now** (Part 13). Only on a device you are handing over, and only if the entries can never be sent, you may type **DISCARD** to delete them.

> **Shared phones:** always sign out when you hand a phone to someone else.

## Part 5. EOC (Emergency Operations Center)

**Where:** **EOC** in the menu. The EOC has many tabs under the title. Swipe the tab row sideways on a phone to see them all.

The tabs, left to right: **Board · Logbook · SitReps · Requests · Resources · Barangays · Barangay liaison · Risk · Plans and clusters · Roster · Map · Anticipatory action**.

### 5.1 The Board: the EOC at a glance

**Where:** **EOC** → **Board**.

The Board shows the current situation in blocks. Every block has a link to its full screen.

- **Alert level card** (big, top): for example *White level — Normal operations*, with the **Change level** button.
- **Logbook:** type a quick note and tap **Log**. **All entries** opens the full logbook.
- **Barangays:** how many barangays reported a condition in the last 24 hours, and how many families and persons are evacuated. **Report status** opens the barangay list.
- **Open resource requests:** **All requests**.
- **Evacuation centres (ECHO):** **ECHO dashboard**.
- **Latest SitRep:** **SitReps**.
- **Priority barangays (DDRiVE):** five priority barangays and whether they have reported. **Risk**.
- **EOC capability:** **Capability board** (Part 11.2).

> The Board and the barangay figures count **only the current event**. An old typhoon does not mix into today’s numbers.

### 5.2 Change the EOC alert level

**Who:** EOC Manager, CDRRMO Head, system administrator. **Needs a connection.** This big decision is never saved for later. If you are offline, MODE tells you.

**The three levels (as MODE names them)**

| Level | Meaning in MODE |
| --- | --- |
| **White** | Normal operations |
| **Blue** | Partial activation: at least half of resources on standby |
| **Red** | Full activation: all resources available |

**Steps**

1. **EOC** → **Board** → tap **Change level**.
2. Choose the new level. The level you are on now is greyed out and says *Current level*.
3. Type the **Reason**. This is required. It goes into the logbook and shows in the top bar.
4. Optional: choose the **Hazard**, and type the **Authority or memo reference** (for example the Mayor’s order or EOC memo number).
5. Tap the confirm button at the bottom of the panel. The change is logged with your name, the time and the reason, and the top bar changes colour.

> **Important:** MODE records the level. It does not decide it. *When* to go to Blue or Red, and *who may order it*, come from the City’s approved EOC Executive Order and contingency plans. Changing the EOC level also does **not** declare a State of Calamity or a State of Imminent Disaster. Those are separate legal steps (Part 5.13).

### 5.3 Logbook: the official diary

**Where:** **EOC** → **Logbook**, or the quick box on the Board. **Who:** duty officers, EOC staff, EOC Manager, CDRRMO Head, admin write. Others can read.

**Write an entry**

1. Tap **New entry**.
2. Pick a **Category**: *General, Decision, Warning, Request, Resource* or *Report*.
3. Type the **Entry**. Say *what happened, who decided, what was sent*. The time is added for you.
4. Tap the save button.

*Level change* and *Correction* entries are made by MODE itself.

**Fix a mistake**

1. Tap the wrong entry in the list.
2. In **Correct an entry**, type the **Corrected entry**.
3. Save. A new entry is added and linked to the old one. The old one stays visible with a line through it.

You cannot edit or delete entries, and you cannot correct a *Level change* entry. Use **Show** at the top to filter by category.

### 5.4 Barangay status reports

**Where:** **EOC** → **Barangays**, or **Today** → **Barangay status**. **Who:** duty officers, EOC staff and above.

The list shows each barangay’s **latest report** and how long ago it came. Barangays that are late show a **late** tag. Some show reference flood-exposure information from DDRiVE.

**Record a report**

1. Tap the barangay.
2. Choose the **Condition**: *Normal, Monitoring, Affected, Isolated* or *Evacuating*.
3. If evacuating, type **Families evacuated** and **Persons evacuated**. Otherwise leave them blank.
4. Choose **Received by**: *Radio, SMS, Phone call, This app* or *In person*.
5. Add **Remarks** if needed. No names.
6. Tap **Record report**.

> **Blank is not zero.** If the barangay did not give a number, leave the box empty. MODE keeps it as *not reported*. Typing 0 means *they reported zero*.

If the list is empty, the administrator must first load the barangay list (Part 12.5).

### 5.5 Resource requests

**Where:** **EOC** → **Requests**.

A request moves through five steps: **Requested → Approved → Mobilized → Delivered → Demobilized**. A request can also be **Rejected**.

**Make a request** (duty officers, EOC staff and above)

1. Tap **New request**.
2. Fill in **Requested by** (office, barangay or incident commander), **Item**, **Quantity**, **Unit**, and the priority (*Life safety, Urgent* or *Routine*).
3. Save. It appears under **Open**.

**Move a request along**

- Open requests show buttons for the next step (for example **Approved**, **Mobilized**).
- **Only the EOC Manager approves or rejects.** Rejecting asks for a **Reason**.
- Finished requests move to **Closed**.

### 5.6 Resource inventory

**Where:** **EOC** → **Resources**.

A list of what the EOC can use: vehicles, heavy equipment, rescue, medical, relief goods, communications, power and others.

- Find things with **Find a resource**, **Kind**, or **Barangay or place**.
- A yellow notice, for example *2 items not serviceable*, means check them against the generator and vehicle test logs before the next activation.
- Tap an item to see details. The operations team can change **Quantity**, **Unit**, **Location** and whether it is serviceable, then tap **Save**. Saving needs a connection.

### 5.7 SitReps (situation reports)

**Where:** **EOC** → **SitReps**.

A SitRep is the official report sent to OCD VIII and the Province. MODE follows the DILG VIII harmonized template. It moves **Draft → Submitted → Approved**.

**Write and send a SitRep**

1. Tap **New SitRep**. Do this once for each reporting period during an activation.
2. Set the **Reporting period**: **From** and **To**.
3. Under **DILG VIII harmonized fields**, choose the **Report type** (*Pre-disaster, During disaster, Post-disaster, Terminal*) and fill in **Incident type**, **Affected barangays**, **Damaged houses — totally / partially**, **Status of lifelines** (power, water, communications, roads), **Assistance provided** and **Cost of assistance (PHP)**.
4. Under **Situation**, write the **Situation overview**, **Actions taken** and **Needs and gaps**.
5. Under **Figures**, tap **Fill from barangay reports** to copy evacuation numbers from the latest barangay reports, and **Add ECHO camp figures** to add the numbers from the camp reports.
6. Tap **Save**. When ready, tap **Submit**. The SitRep gets its number (*SitRep No. 3*, for example).
7. The EOC Manager, CDRRMO Head or administrator taps **Approve**. An approved SitRep is locked. For new information, make a new SitRep.

**Share it:** tap **Excel** to download it in the DILG VIII layout, or **Print** to print or save as PDF.

### 5.8 Barangay liaison desk

**Where:** **EOC** → **Barangay liaison**.

Shows, by barangay cluster, which barangays have reported and which are **silent** (no report since the EOC was raised). Tap a cluster to see its barangays. Silent barangays are listed first, ranked by DDRiVE priority. Counts only. This is for deciding who to call next, not a trigger.

### 5.9 Risk

**Where:** **EOC** → **Risk**.

The DDRiVE (Data-Driven Risk and Vulnerability Estimation) desk assessment: a **City risk picture** and a **Barangay profile** for each barangay. Use the filter to show: *All barangays, Flood-classified, Rain-induced landslide, Earthquake-induced landslide, Liquefaction, Tsunami, Storm surge, Priority-ranked*, or *In a proposed cluster*. It is aggregate data only.

### 5.10 Plans and clusters

**Where:** **EOC** → **Plans and clusters**.

- **This activation** shows which plan, response clusters and EOC modules are switched on. Each is marked *Lead, Activated, On standby* or *Stood down*.
- **Contingency plans** lists each hazard’s plan with its status: *To prepare, Draft, Adopted, Expired, Retired*.
- **Response clusters** shows the clusters the plans use.
- Opening a plan’s clusters and modules on the current activation is done with the activate button on the plan. **Who:** EOC Manager or CDRRMO Head. **Needs a connection.**

Modules include the Operations Section, Planning Section, PDRA and anticipatory action desk, Logistics Section, Finance and Administration, Cluster desks, Barangay Liaison Desk, and Building assessment and MDM.

### 5.11 Roster

**Where:** **EOC** → **Roster**.

Every EOC position needs a **Primary** and two alternates (**First alternate**, **Second alternate**) so the EOC can run for 72 hours. The **Depth** meter shows how full the roster is.

1. Tap **Propose** under a position. This works offline.
2. The CDRRMO Head taps **Approve** (needs a connection). An approved designation is fixed.
3. **Withdraw** removes a designation, but it stays on record.

A designation names a **position and office**, and the roster follows the City’s approved arrangements.

### 5.12 Map (Geo-COP)

**Where:** **EOC** → **Map**.

The *common operating picture*:

- **City hazard map library:** tap a map (flood, rain-induced landslide, earthquake-induced landslide and others) to open the full-resolution image. The map’s own legend, date, scale and limits are part of the map. These are pictures for reference, not live data.
- **Reference points:** the EOC, alternate EOC, operations centre, incident command post, staging areas and evacuation hubs. Add one with **Add point** (from a KML file or coordinates). New points show *Pending field validation* until someone confirms them on the ground.
- **Layers:** **Add layer** accepts KML, KMZ or GeoJSON files.

### 5.13 Anticipatory action (acting before a disaster)

**Where:** **EOC** → **Anticipatory action**.

This follows RA 12287 (Imminent Disaster Act) and its rules. The screen says *Pending national guidelines*: some national rules are not yet issued, and MODE will be updated.

**Step A. Assess: New PDRA**

**Who:** CDRRMO Head, EOC Manager, EOC staff.

1. Tap **New PDRA** (Pre-Disaster Risk Assessment).
2. **Forecast:** choose the **Hazard**; type the **Forecast source** (issuer and bulletin number); set **Forecast issued** and **Expected impact**. The lead time is counted from now.
3. **Projected impact:** choose *Severe, Moderate, Minor* or *Uncertain*; describe cascading hazards and the exposure summary; tick the barangays.
4. **Decision:** choose one.
   - **Monitor:** keep watching. No anticipatory action yet.
   - **City early action:** act now under the City’s own authority, without a declaration. For flash floods and landslides, also run the flash flood early action protocol (Part 6).
   - **Request a State of Imminent Disaster:** for severe impact with a lead time of at least three (3) days, extendable to five (5) days. You must type the **Basis for the request**.

**Step B. Follow the declaration, in order**

If you request a declaration, MODE shows seven steps with the next deadline:

1. Pre-disaster risk assessment.
2. **Request to RDRRMC VIII.** Tap **Record request sent**. This starts the 24-hour reply clock. (If a Province or Region VIII declaration already covers Ormoc, use the **adopted** option.)
3. **RDRRMC recommendation.** Record *Recommended* or *Not recommended*.
4. **Mayor’s Executive Order.** Type the **Executive Order number** and record it.
5. **Anticipatory actions and funds.** Tap to load the action checklist for the hazard. The CDRRMO Head records releases under **Funds released**. MODE checks the limits.
6. **Lifting.** Tap **Record lifting**. This starts the 45-day clock.
7. **Report to OCD.** Tap **Record report sent** within 45 days.

You can **Withdraw this request** if the forecast changes. Closed declarations stay under **Closed declarations**.

**Funds:** **AA funds** (top of the screen) shows the year’s anticipatory action fund, the 10% cap and the 30%/70% tranches.

> MODE keeps the record and the deadlines. The legal decisions remain with the CDRRMC, RDRRMC and the Mayor. If unsure of a legal reading, ask the City Legal Office.

## Part 6. Protocols (step-by-step procedures)

**Where:** **Protocols** in the menu. The page title is *DRRM Workflows and Protocols*.

Three tabs sit under the title:

| Tab | What it holds | Simple meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Workflows** | Management cycles between offices: CDRRMC, budget, EOC designations, contingency plans, maps | A *workflow* is a bigger job that many offices share |
| **Protocols** | Operational procedures: duty, warning, EOC, anticipatory action, reporting | A *protocol* is a checklist you follow at the moment of action |
| **Forms** | The form that each step is recorded on | Every step ends on a form |

An **SOP** is the controlled text in the Manual. Protocols and workflows turn SOPs into steps you can tick off.

At the top you see counters (for example *21 Protocols · 21 Drafts · 1 Running now*) and a **Find** box. Type a title, an SOP number such as *SOP 4-06*, or an owner. Below are **Running now**, then the protocols grouped by area (for example *Disaster prevention and mitigation*), then **Recent runs**.

> **Draft means not yet for use.** Every protocol and workflow stays a *Draft* until the owning office checks it and the CDRRMO Head approves it. Until then, use them for **practice and drills**, and follow the approved Manual for real operations. A written procedure is not proof that it is in use.

### 6.1 Start a protocol

**Who:** duty officers, EOC staff, EOC Manager, CDRRMO Head, admin.

1. Tap the protocol in the list. A panel opens with the steps, the process owner, and the forms.
2. Check **Procedure source**. It links to the Manual section in the Library. **RACI for (code)** shows who does what (Part 11).
3. If this is an exercise, tick **This is a drill**.
4. Tap **Start protocol** (or **Start drill**).

### 6.2 Work through the steps

The run page shows the title, when it started, and *4 of 9 steps closed*. For each step:

1. Do the action in real life.
2. Fill in the step’s form if it has one. Add a **Note** (some steps require it).
3. Close the step with the done button. MODE records **who** closed it and **when**.
   - **Skip** needs a reason.
   - **Not applicable** means this step does not fit the situation.
   - **Reopen** lets you change a step you already closed.
4. When every critical step is closed, tap **Complete**.
5. To stop early, tap **Stop** and type the **Reason**. It stays in the run history.

A running protocol appears under **Running now** on any of your devices, so you can continue on another phone or computer.

### 6.3 Drills count

A run started as a drill is recorded as a drill. This shows that a procedure has been *tested*, which helps the readiness assessment (Part 7).

### 6.4 Forms

**Where:** **Protocols** → **Forms**.

Forms are listed as **Draft, Approved** or **Retired**. Tap a form to see its fields. Only the CDRRMO Head can **Approve**, **Reopen as draft**, or **Retire** a form, and this needs a connection.

## Part 7. Assess (how ready are we?)

**Where:** **Assess**. Two tabs: **Institutional assessment (IAT)** and **Hazard maps**.

### 7.1 What the IAT is

The **Institutional Assessment Tool (IAT)** is a scorecard for how mature our CDRRMO and EOC are. In version 1.7 it has **140 indicators** (questions):

| Part | About | Indicators |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Part A** | The CDRRMO | 82 |
| **Part B** | The EOC | 43 |
| **Part C** | How the two work together (the interface) | 15 |

Each indicator is scored by maturity level, and the evidence you attach limits how high the score can go.

### 7.2 Assessment cycles

**Where:** **Assess** → **Institutional assessment (IAT)**.

A *cycle* is one round of assessment, for example *2026 Baseline (IAT v1.7)*. Tap a cycle to open it. Assessors and the CDRRMO Head can start a new one with **New cycle** (type a **Name** and optional **Notes**). MODE creates all 140 indicators for you.

### 7.3 Read a cycle

At the top: a progress bar (*140 of 140 answered*) and these buttons:

- **Validation workshop:** the queue of scores to confirm, printable sheets and the results board.
- **Action plan:** every gap, ranked by priority, with owners and dates.
- **EOC capability checklist:** the OCD checklist for the EOC (Part 11.2).
- **Export CSV:** download all scores as a spreadsheet.
- A stage button (for example **Finalize results**) that moves the cycle to its next stage.

**Results** shows four scores: the **Institutional Maturity Index (A+B)**, **Part A, CDRRMO**, **Part B, EOC**, and **Part C, Interface Index** (reported separately). Each has a number out of 4.0, a word such as *Developing* or *Initial*, and *Mandatory met* (the share of must-do items achieved). A red line, such as *32 high-priority gaps*, tells you what to raise with the Mayor and CDRRMC.

**Evidence check** counts how many scores have been *Pending validation, Verified, Partially verified, Unverified*, or have *Missing evidence*. A check does **not** change the score.

Further down are the results by **domain**. Tap a domain, then an indicator.

> At the time these screens were captured, all 140 baseline answers were still **pending validation**. Treat the numbers as draft until the validation workshop is done.

### 7.4 Score one indicator

**Who:** assessors and lead-office focal persons score. Validators confirm. Others can read.

1. Open the indicator.
2. Under **Evidence**, tap **Add evidence**. Choose the **Type of evidence** and **What it shows**, give it a **Title** (for example *EO No. 12 s. 2024 creating the CDRRMO*), the **Document date**, and the **Source office or position**. **Never type an informant’s name.** You may attach a **File** (PDF, Word, Excel or a photo, up to 15 MB). Tap the add button.
3. Under **Score**, choose the **Maturity level**. If the indicator does not apply, say **Why it does not apply**.
4. Add **Gap or remarks** and **Recommended action** (optional).
5. Tap **Save**. Then send the score to a validator.
6. A validator confirms it. If the validator changes the score, a **Reason for the change** is required. Validated scores are locked.

### 7.5 Hazard maps

**Where:** **Assess** → **Hazard maps** (in the side menu it sits just under Assess).

Official hazard sheets (flood, rain-induced landslide, storm surge, liquefaction, earthquake-induced landslide, ground rupture, ground shaking, tsunami) drawn over the barangays. Use the buttons above the map:

| Button | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Identify** | Tap the map to see the barangay and the hazard class of each sheet shown there |
| **Distance** | Tap points to measure a distance along the ground |
| **Area** | Tap three or more points to measure an area |
| **My location** | Shows the hazards where you are now. Uses your device’s location; nothing is saved |
| **Clear** | Removes your measuring points |
| **Export PNG** | Saves the current view as a picture with title, sources and accuracy |
| **Full screen** | Fills the screen. Press **Esc** (or **Exit full screen**) to leave |
| **+ / −** | Zoom in and out |

You can switch each hazard sheet on or off and use **Up** and **Down** to change which is drawn on top. Each sheet has a note about its source and how closely it fits the map. Read it before relying on the map. The sheets are *reference pictures*: they do not forecast and do not trigger anything.

## Part 9. Vault (documents)

**Where:** **Vault**. **Who:** assessors, validators, administrators and CDRRMO staff (CDRRMO Head, EOC Manager, EOC staff, duty officers). If your role is not on the list, MODE says *Not available for your role*. Ask the system administrator.

The Vault is one place for laws, guidelines, City issuances, plans and records. Three tabs: **Documents, Document Review, References**.

### 9.1 Find and open a document

1. Type in the search box (title, number, issuer or indicator), or use **All types** to show one kind of document.

3. **Evidence for** lists the indicators this document supports.

### 9.2 Add a document

1. Tap **Add document**.
2. Fill in the details and do one of: **upload a file**, **add a link** to the official copy, or **say where the paper original is kept**.

### 9.4 Document Review tab

For the assessment team. It lists what to request for each indicator and what the Vault already has. Use **All** or **Still to collect**, and **Download request list** to make a spreadsheet for your request letter to offices.

### 9.5 References tab

A catalogue of the laws and guidelines cited in MODE, each explained in plain language. Tapping a citation such as *RA 10121 Sec. 21* anywhere in MODE opens a short note like this.

### 10.1 ECHO: Evacuation and Camp Handling Operations

**Where:** **Tools** → **ECHO**. Four tabs:

| Tab | Use it to |
| --- | --- |
| **Simulator** | Practise the whole cycle: scenario, evacuation, centres, camp operations, protection and closure. Saved on this device |
| **Evacuation intelligence** | See the hazard sheets, barangay exposure, and screen centres and routes on a map |

### 10.2 Flood intelligence

**Where:** **Tools** → **Flood intelligence** (or from Today).

Shows each barangay’s flood exposure, the City hazard maps and official warning sources. It is **static assessment data, not a live forecast**. A high flood-prone population means more people are exposed, not that water will reach a certain depth, and it is never an evacuation or declaration trigger.

### 10.3 ICS Exercise Simulator

**Where:** **Tools** → **ICS Exercise Simulator**.

Practise the Philippine Incident Command System on Ormoc scenarios. You can run an **individual drill**, a **facilitated tabletop** or a **position drill**.

1. Choose the type, then your **ICS role**, one of six **scenarios**, and the **difficulty**.
2. Enter your name (this is the exercise player, not an affected person).
3. Tap **Start exercise** and read the briefing.
4. For each situation, choose an option and tap **Decide**.
   - **Request intel** asks Planning for more information. It costs some timeliness points.
   - **Hint** (some difficulties) shows a doctrine hint. It costs points.
   - Facilitators can **Pause** and **Resume** the clock. **End** stops without saving.
5. At the end, open the **after-action review (AAR)**. Tap **Print** to print or save as PDF.

Past exercises appear in a list with an **AAR** button. The assessment team can file a saved run as evidence for the readiness assessment, with positions and counts only.

### 10.4 Public Service Continuity Plan (PSCP)

**Where:** **Tools** → **Public Service Continuity Plan**.

A workbench to build the plan that keeps essential services running. Work through the sections in order: **Policy and team, Essential functions (BIA), Risk assessment, Continuity strategies, Activation, Plan document**. Print the finished plan.

### 10.5 Gawad KALASAG Seal Readiness

**Where:** **Tools** → **Gawad KALASAG Seal Readiness**.

Rate the six Seal criteria from 0 to 3 and add notes. MODE calculates a weighted score and rating. **Fill from IAT** can pre-fill ratings from the assessment (check each one). You can **Print**, **download** a copy, or **Clear** everything.

> **Where is the data kept?** The ICS, PSCP, KALASAG and ECHO simulator tools keep your work in your own browser, per account. Use **Print** or the download button to keep a copy.

## Part 11. Two boards that tie everything together

### 11.1 RACI board: who does what

**Where:** **Today** → **RACI board**, or the **RACI for (code)** link inside a protocol.

Type a protocol code (for example *PR-PDRA*), an activity (for example *convene*) or an office in the search box. Each activity shows four kinds of people:

| Letter | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **R** — Responsible | Does the work |
| **A** — Accountable | Answers for it. There should be exactly one |
| **C** — Consulted | Gives advice first |
| **I** — Informed | Is told afterwards |

Activities without exactly one Accountable person are flagged for the CDRRMO. The CDRRMO Head adjusts assignments online; everyone can read them offline. RACI shows *work*. It does not move legal authority.

### 11.2 EOC capability board

**Where:** **EOC** → **Board** → **EOC capability** → **Capability board**, or **Assess** → (a cycle) → **EOC capability checklist**.

The OCD/NDRRMC checklist of **154 items in 15 sections**. Each item shows a status taken from our assessment evidence: **Met** (green), **Partially met** (amber), **Largely unmet** (orange), **Not met** (red), or **No direct IAT indicator** (grey). Open an item to see the evidence, the gap, the recommended action, and links to the SOP, protocol, workflow or form that closes the gap. The assessment team edits online. The CDRRMO Head validates and locks a run.

## Part 13. Working offline and syncing

### 13.1 What the top-right words mean

| You see | It means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Synced 21 seconds ago* | Everything is up to date | Nothing |
| *Syncing* | MODE is sending and receiving now | Wait |
| *Offline* | No signal | Keep working. Entries are saved on the device |
| *Offline, 3 waiting* | 3 entries are saved but not yet sent | They send by themselves when signal returns |
| *Server problem, 2 waiting* | The connection works but the server is not answering | MODE retries. Keep working |
| *2 need attention* | The server refused something or two people changed the same record | Tap it and read Part 13.3 |
| *Not synced yet* | This device has not loaded data yet | Connect once and wait |

Tap the words to open **Sync and offline data**.

### 13.2 What works without internet

**Works offline:** reading anything already loaded, Search, logbook entries, barangay reports, new resource requests, SitRep drafts, running protocols, proposing roster designations, the ICS, PSCP, KALASAG and ECHO simulator tools.

**Needs a connection:** changing the EOC alert level, approving anything (requests, SitReps, roster, forms, manual versions), declaration steps and fund releases, activating a plan, saving resource edits, validating scores, and managing accounts. If you try offline, MODE says so. It never saves these for later.

> **Before a typhoon:** open MODE once while you have signal, check that it says *Synced*, and keep your phone charged.

### 13.3 The Sync and offline data page

**Where:** **Settings** → **Sync and offline data**, or tap the sync words.

- **Sync now** sends waiting changes and fetches the latest data. **Retry now** tries waiting changes again.
- **Refused entries:** the server said no, with its reason in plain words. **Copy** keeps what you typed so you can enter it again. **Dismiss** removes the message.
- **Conflicts:** someone else changed the same record. **Apply my change on top** keeps the other person’s version and adds your changed fields. **Keep theirs** drops yours.
- **Files that could not upload:** **Retry upload**, **Save a copy** to your device, or **Discard** (it asks you to confirm first).

## Part 15. Walk-throughs

These show how the parts of MODE connect on real days.

### 15.1 Start of shift (about 10 minutes)

1. Open **Today**. Read the **Since your last visit** counters.
2. Look at the top bar: what is the EOC level? Is it *Synced*?
3. Open the **Hazard watch** links and read the latest PAGASA and PHIVOLCS information.
4. Check **Data quality** and **Hazard context**. Phone any high-risk barangay that has not reported in 12 hours.
5. Open **EOC → Logbook** and read the last entries.
6. Write a **General** entry: *Shift started, handover read.*

### 15.2 A typhoon or flood, from watch to wrap-up

| Moment | Where | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. A forecast appears** | **Today** → Hazard watch | Read PAGASA. Log a **Warning** entry saying what you read and the source |
| **2. Time to prepare** | **EOC** → **Anticipatory action** → **New PDRA** | Record the forecast, expected impact and decision. Short-lead hazards (flash floods, landslides) normally use **City early action** and the flash flood early action protocol |
| **3. Follow the procedure** | **Protocols** | Tap the right protocol, **Start protocol**, close each step |
| **4. Leadership decides to activate** | **EOC** → **Board** → **Change level** | The EOC Manager or CDRRMO Head sets Blue or Red with the reason and the memo number |
| **5. Switch on the plan** | **EOC** → **Plans and clusters** | Open the hazard’s plan so the clusters and modules switch on |
| **6. Barangays report** | **EOC** → **Barangays** | Record each report. Check **Barangay liaison** for silent barangays and call them |
| **7. People move to centres** | **Tools** → **ECHO** → **Camp management** | Record camp counts. The **EOC dashboard** tab shows the whole picture |
| **8. Help is needed** | **EOC** → **Requests** | **New request**. The EOC Manager approves. Move it to Mobilized, Delivered |
| **9. Everything important** | **EOC** → **Logbook** | Write decisions, warnings and orders as they happen. Correct, never erase |
| **10. Report upward** | **EOC** → **SitReps** | **New SitRep**, **Fill from barangay reports**, **Submit**; the EOC Manager **Approve**s; tap **Excel** to send |
| **11. It is over** | **EOC** → **Board** | The EOC Manager lowers the level with the reason. If a declaration was in force, record **Lifting** and the 45-day **Report to OCD** |
| **12. Learn from it** | **Assess**, **Library** | Hold the after-action review. Note what to fix in the action plan |

### 15.3 Drill day

1. **Protocols** → pick the protocol → tick **This is a drill** → **Start drill**.
2. For team practice, use **Tools** → **ICS Exercise Simulator** (tabletop) and **ECHO** → **Simulator**.
3. Print the **AAR** and keep it. The assessment team can file the drill record as evidence.

### 15.4 Preparing for the readiness assessment

1. **Vault** → **Document Review** → **Still to collect**. Download the request list and send it to the offices.
2. **Vault** → **Add document** as files arrive.
3. **Assess** → open the cycle → open an indicator → **Add evidence** → score → **Save**.
4. Validators confirm. Then open the **Action plan** and plan the fixes.
