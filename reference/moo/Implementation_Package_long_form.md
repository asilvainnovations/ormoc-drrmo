# Ormoc City DRRM and EOC Institutional Implementation Package

Companion instruments to Chapter 1 of the CDRRMO–EOC Manual of Operations

This package translates the Chapter 1 governance architecture into proposed local legislative, executive and operational instruments. It is designed to establish clear institutional authority, define decision rights, formalize the CDRRMO–EOC relationship, standardize disaster declaration procedures and provide the records and accountability mechanisms needed to make the system operational.

The instruments are presented as drafts for legal and technical review, not as already enacted or issued City Government instruments. They should be harmonized with existing Ormoc City ordinances, executive orders, plantilla and staffing arrangements, the approved DRRM plans, the Local Development Investment Program, the CLUP and CDRA, and the applicable national rules.

## Package structure and adoption sequence

| Instrument                     | Proposed form       | Primary institutional purpose                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. CDRRMO institutionalization | City Ordinance      | Establish the CDRRMO as a regular city department, define its mandate, structure, functions, staffing and accountability            |
| 2. CDRRMC governance           | Executive Order     | Update Council membership, committee arrangements, Secretariat functions and governance procedures                                  |
| 3. EOC establishment           | Executive Order     | Establish the City EOC, its activation levels, operating arrangements, functional sections and relationship with ICS                |
| 4. State of Calamity           | SOP                 | Standardize assessment, recommendation, sanggunian action, implementation, monitoring and lifting                                   |
| 5. State of Imminent Disaster  | SOP                 | Standardize risk-based recommendation, LCE declaration by EO, anticipatory action, resource mobilization and post-lifting reporting |
| 6. Accountability instruments  | RACI matrices       | Assign responsibility and decision rights across the CDRRMC, CDRRMO, EOC, LCE, sanggunian, offices and response organizations       |
| 7. Governance templates        | Forms and registers | Ensure decisions, evidence, instructions, resource commitments and corrective actions are documented and auditable                  |

Recommended adoption sequence: initiate the ordinance and legal review first; update the CDRRMC Executive Order; issue the EOC Executive Order; approve the two declaration SOPs and their templates; then integrate the instruments into the MOO, staff orientation, exercises and annual performance review.

A key institutional distinction must be preserved throughout: the CDRRMO is the permanent local government institution, the CDRRMC is the statutory coordinating and policy body, the EOC is the coordination and information-management facility/mechanism, and the Incident Command System (ICS) provides incident-level command and control. Establishing an EOC does not transfer the statutory powers of the LCE or sanggunian, nor does it displace the authority of an Incident Commander under an activated ICS structure.

# Instrument 1. Proposed Ormoc City DRRM Office Institutionalization Ordinance

Draft local legislative instrument · For review by the City Legal Office, City HRMO, City Budget Office and Sangguniang Panlungsod

## Republic of the Philippines

## City of Ormoc

## Office of the Sangguniang Panlungsod

ORDINANCE NO. \_\_\_\_\_\_

AN ORDINANCE INSTITUTIONALIZING THE CITY DISASTER RISK REDUCTION AND MANAGEMENT OFFICE (CDRRMO) AS A REGULAR DEPARTMENT OF THE CITY GOVERNMENT OF ORMOC, DEFINING ITS ORGANIZATIONAL STRUCTURE, FUNCTIONS, STAFFING, COORDINATION MECHANISMS AND ACCOUNTABILITY SYSTEM, PROVIDING APPROPRIATIONS THEREFOR, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES

### Be it ordained by the Sangguniang Panlungsod of Ormoc City, in session assembled:

### Section 1. Title

This Ordinance shall be known as the “Ormoc City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office Institutionalization Ordinance.”

### Section 2. Declaration of Policy

It is hereby declared the policy of the City Government of Ormoc to institutionalize an integrated, risk-informed, inclusive and accountable local disaster risk reduction and management system that protects lives, livelihoods, assets, ecosystems and essential services; reduces disaster risks; strengthens climate resilience; and supports preparedness, response, recovery and sustainable development.

The City shall pursue a whole-of-government and whole-of-society approach, recognizing that disaster risk is shaped by the interaction of hazards, exposure, vulnerability, capacity, land use, infrastructure, ecosystems, governance and socioeconomic conditions.

The City shall mainstream DRRM and climate change adaptation into local development planning, investment programming, budgeting, land use management, infrastructure development, social protection and public service delivery.

### Section 3. Legal Basis

This Ordinance is enacted pursuant to, and shall be interpreted consistently with, the following:

1. Republic Act No. 10121, otherwise known as the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010, and its Implementing Rules and Regulations;
2. Republic Act No. 7160, otherwise known as the Local Government Code of 1991;
3. Republic Act No. 9729, or the Climate Change Act of 2009, as amended by Republic Act No. 10174;
4. Republic Act No. 11038, or the Expanded National Integrated Protected Areas System Act, where applicable to local ecosystem and protected-area concerns;
5. Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012;
6. Republic Act No. 12287, or the law providing for the declaration of a State of Imminent Disaster and the implementation of anticipatory actions, together with its applicable IRR and subsequent issuances;
7. The applicable Department of Budget and Management, Civil Service Commission, Commission on Audit, Office of Civil Defense and other national government rules; and
8. Other relevant national laws, administrative issuances and local ordinances.

Legal drafting note: The City Legal Office shall verify the complete and current titles, provisions, implementing rules and effectivity of the cited laws and issuances before enactment. References to specific funding percentages, lead times, eligibility conditions or documentary requirements under Republic Act No. 12287 and its IRR must be checked against certified official texts and applicable DBM and COA rules.

### Section 4. Establishment and Institutional Status

There is hereby institutionalized the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO) as a regular department of the City Government of Ormoc, subject to the applicable requirements on local government organizational structures, staffing patterns, position classification, compensation, budgeting and personnel administration.

The CDRRMO shall serve as the City's principal technical and administrative office for the coordination, integration, implementation, monitoring and continuous improvement of local DRRM and climate change adaptation programs.

The CDRRMO shall operate under the supervision of the City Mayor, consistent with the Local Government Code, Republic Act No. 10121 and other applicable laws.

Its institutionalization shall not diminish the statutory authority of the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (CDRRMC), the Sangguniang Panlungsod, the City Mayor, national government agencies or other city departments and offices.

### Section 5. Mandate and Objectives

The CDRRMO shall:

a. Develop, implement, coordinate and monitor the City's comprehensive DRRM programs and plans;

b. Support the integration of DRRM and climate change adaptation into local development, land use, investment and budget processes;

c. Maintain and periodically update risk information, hazard and exposure datasets, vulnerability assessments and risk-informed planning inputs in coordination with competent offices and agencies;

d. Strengthen disaster preparedness, early warning, emergency communications, response readiness and recovery planning;

e. Provide technical and Secretariat support to the CDRRMC;

f. Maintain the City's disaster operations and coordination capability, including the EOC, in accordance with approved activation and operating protocols;

g. Facilitate inter-office, inter-barangay, inter-agency, civil society, private sector and community coordination;

h. Support the organization, training, equipping and readiness of local response teams and volunteer groups;

i. Coordinate the conduct of post-disaster needs assessments, damage and loss assessments, and recovery planning with the responsible offices and agencies;

j. Monitor the implementation and performance of local DRRM programs, projects and activities;

k. Maintain institutional records, operational reports, risk information and lessons learned; and

l. Perform other functions assigned by law or validly delegated by the City Mayor, consistent with the mandate of the Office.

### Section 6. Core Functional Divisions

Subject to the approved organizational structure and staffing pattern, the CDRRMO shall be organized around the following functional areas:

1. Administration and Finance – personnel administration, procurement coordination, financial and budget management, property and logistics records, administrative support and office continuity.
2. Research, Planning and Risk Assessment – risk assessment, hazard and exposure information, vulnerability analysis, DRRM and CCA planning, policy development, and mainstreaming into local plans and investments.
3. Operations and Early Warning – preparedness, early warning coordination, EOC operations, incident information management, response coordination, drills and exercises.
4. Training, Public Information and Community Resilience – capacity development, public education, risk communication, community-based DRRM, volunteer coordination and inclusive preparedness.

The functional areas may be implemented through divisions, sections or units, as authorized by the approved organizational structure and staffing pattern. The City shall not create plantilla positions or confer statutory powers solely by virtue of this Ordinance where separate approval or compliance is required.

### Section 7. Head of Office

The CDRRMO shall be headed by a City DRRM Officer, appointed in accordance with applicable civil service laws and regulations, subject to the prescribed qualification standards, salary grade, plantilla authorization and other requirements.

The City DRRM Officer shall:

a. Lead the administration and technical direction of the CDRRMO;

b. Ensure implementation of approved DRRM policies, plans, programs and projects;

c. Advise the City Mayor and CDRRMC on risk, preparedness, response and recovery matters;

d. Serve as the designated CDRRMC Secretariat head or principal Secretariat coordinator, as provided in the applicable CDRRMC issuance;

e. Ensure that the EOC is maintained and operated in accordance with approved protocols;

f. Coordinate with national government agencies, neighboring local government units, barangays and partner institutions;

g. Ensure proper use, documentation and reporting of DRRM resources; and

h. Submit periodic performance, risk and readiness reports to the City Mayor and CDRRMC.

### Section 8. Staffing and Competency Requirements

The City Government shall develop and maintain a staffing pattern appropriate to the City's risk profile, population, geographic conditions, service demands and operational requirements.

The staffing pattern shall be consistent with applicable national guidelines, including the relevant joint issuances on LDRRMO organization and staffing, and shall be submitted for the required review and approval.

The City shall progressively establish competency-based staffing and development arrangements for the following functions:

- Risk assessment and geospatial information management;
- DRRM planning and policy integration;
- EOC operations and information management;
- Early warning and risk communication;
- Emergency logistics and resource tracking;
- Training, exercises and community preparedness;
- Recovery planning and monitoring; and
- Administration, finance and records management.

The City shall ensure that position descriptions, qualifications, competency requirements, reporting relationships and performance indicators are documented and periodically reviewed.

### Section 9. Continuous Operations and Personnel Welfare

The CDRRMO shall maintain arrangements for continuous operational readiness, including an approved duty roster, contact directory, succession and relief arrangements, office continuity procedures and EOC activation support.

The City shall provide appropriate personnel safety and welfare measures, subject to applicable laws and available appropriations. These shall include risk-appropriate personal protective equipment, occupational safety measures, responder insurance or other lawful protection mechanisms, medical support, rest and rotation arrangements, and psychosocial support.

### Section 10. Coordination with the CDRRMC

The CDRRMO shall provide technical and Secretariat support to the CDRRMC, including:

a. Preparation of meeting agendas and technical briefs;

b. Consolidation of office and barangay DRRM reports;

c. Documentation of resolutions, decisions and action points;

d. Monitoring of CDRRMC decisions and commitments;

e. Preparation of policy and investment recommendations; and

f. Maintenance of the official CDRRMC records.

The CDRRMO shall not substitute for the collective deliberative and decision-making authority of the CDRRMC.

### Section 11. Relationship with the Emergency Operations Center

The City EOC shall operate as the City's designated facility and coordination mechanism for monitoring, information consolidation, resource coordination, decision support and operational reporting during emergencies and other authorized activation periods.

The EOC shall be managed and supported by the CDRRMO under the separate EOC Establishment Executive Order and the EOC Manual of Operations.

The EOC shall not be construed as a separate local government department or as an independent incident command authority.

When an Incident Command System is activated, the EOC shall support coordination, information management, resource support and policy-level decision requirements while the Incident Commander retains incident-level command and control within the scope of the activated ICS organization.

### Section 12. Inter-Office and External Coordination

All City Government departments, offices and instrumentalities shall designate DRRM focal persons and provide relevant information and technical support within their respective mandates.

The CDRRMO shall facilitate coordination but shall not assume the statutory, regulatory or technical responsibilities of other offices.

Examples include:

- CPDO for land use, development planning and investment programming;
- City Engineering Office and Office of the Building Official for infrastructure and building safety matters;
- City Health Office and health facilities for public health and medical response;
- CENRO and relevant DENR offices for environmental and ecosystem management;
- City Agriculture and Veterinary Office for agriculture, livestock and fisheries concerns;
- DepEd and other education authorities for school safety and education continuity;
- utilities and service providers for essential service continuity; and
- private sector, business organizations and civil society partners for continuity planning, logistics, volunteer support and community resilience.

### Section 13. Planning, Budgeting and Resource Mobilization

The CDRRMO shall coordinate the preparation, updating and monitoring of the Local DRRM Plan and related investment and preparedness programs.

The City shall ensure that DRRM and CCA priorities are reflected in the appropriate local development plans, annual investment programs, annual budgets and other planning and financing instruments.

The Local DRRM Fund and other legally available resources shall be programmed, obligated, disbursed and accounted for in accordance with Republic Act No. 10121, Republic Act No. 12287 where applicable, and the relevant DBM, COA and other government rules.

Nothing in this Ordinance shall be construed as authorizing expenditure, fund transfer or procurement outside the applicable budget, accounting, auditing and procurement requirements.

### Section 14. Performance Monitoring and Institutional Accountability

The CDRRMO shall establish a performance monitoring and evaluation system based on measurable indicators, verifiable evidence and periodic management review.

At a minimum, the system shall monitor:

a. Institutional readiness and compliance;

b. Implementation of the Local DRRM Plan;

c. EOC operational readiness and activation performance;

d. Early warning coverage, reach and timeliness;

e. Training, drills and response capability;

f. Resource availability, deployment and accountability;

g. Implementation of corrective actions and lessons learned; and

h. Recovery planning and implementation progress.

The CDRRMO shall submit an annual institutional performance report to the City Mayor and CDRRMC, with appropriate public disclosure consistent with applicable transparency and data privacy rules.

### Section 15. Records and Information Management

The CDRRMO shall maintain an organized records management system covering policies, plans, risk assessments, meeting records, EOC logs, warning records, resource inventories, incident reports, assessments, financial and procurement documents, after-action reviews and performance reports.

Sensitive personal information and operationally restricted information shall be handled in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 and applicable records management and disclosure rules.

### Section 16. Transition and Institutional Strengthening

Within one hundred twenty (120) days from the effectivity of this Ordinance, the City Mayor, through the CDRRMO and concerned offices, shall submit to the Sangguniang Panlungsod, as applicable:

1. The proposed organizational structure and staffing pattern;
2. A position description and competency framework;
3. A transition and capacity development plan;
4. A proposed annual work and financial plan;
5. A CDRRMO–EOC operating and coordination framework; and
6. A proposed implementation schedule for the MOO and supporting SOPs.

The City shall prioritize the correction of high-priority institutional gaps identified in the institutional assessment, subject to lawful authority, technical validation and available resources.

### Section 17. Appropriations

The amount necessary for the initial implementation of this Ordinance shall be charged against available appropriations of the City Government, subject to applicable budgeting, accounting and auditing rules.

Thereafter, such sums as may be necessary shall be included in the annual city budget, subject to the usual budgetary process.

### Section 18. Implementing Rules and Regulations

Within ninety (90) days from the effectivity of this Ordinance, the City Mayor, upon recommendation of the CDRRMO and in consultation with the concerned offices, shall issue the necessary implementing rules and administrative guidelines, consistent with this Ordinance and applicable national laws.

### Section 19. Separability Clause

If any provision of this Ordinance is declared invalid or unconstitutional, the remaining provisions not affected thereby shall continue in full force and effect.

### Section 20. Repealing and Amending Clause

All local ordinances, resolutions, executive orders, rules and regulations inconsistent with this Ordinance are hereby repealed, amended or modified accordingly, to the extent of such inconsistency.

### Section 21. Effectivity

This Ordinance shall take effect in accordance with the applicable provisions of the Local Government Code and other laws governing the enactment and effectivity of local ordinances.

ENACTED this \_\_\_\_ day of \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ 2026, in the City of Ormoc, Philippines.

# Instrument 2. Proposed Executive Order Updating the Ormoc CDRRMC

Draft executive issuance · To be reconciled with the current CDRRMC composition and existing executive orders

## Republic of the Philippines

## City of Ormoc

## Office of the City Mayor

EXECUTIVE ORDER NO. \_\_\_\_\
Series of 2026

AN EXECUTIVE ORDER RECONSTITUTING AND UPDATING THE MEMBERSHIP, COMMITTEE STRUCTURE, SECRETARIAT ARRANGEMENTS AND GOVERNANCE PROCEDURES OF THE ORMOC CITY DISASTER RISK REDUCTION AND MANAGEMENT COUNCIL

### WHEREAS

Republic Act No. 10121 mandates the establishment and operation of Local Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Councils to coordinate, integrate, supervise and evaluate local DRRM activities;

WHEREAS, the changing risk landscape of Ormoc City requires stronger institutional coordination, risk-informed planning, inclusive governance, anticipatory action, preparedness, response and recovery arrangements;

WHEREAS, the City Government recognizes the need to update the composition and operational arrangements of the CDRRMC to ensure representation of relevant city offices, national government agencies, civil society organizations, private sector institutions and other stakeholders, consistent with applicable law;

WHEREAS, the City seeks to clarify the relationship between the CDRRMC, CDRRMO, City Emergency Operations Center and Incident Command System;

NOW, THEREFORE, I, the City Mayor of Ormoc, by virtue of the powers vested in me by law, hereby order:

### Section 1. Reconstitution

The Ormoc City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council is hereby reconstituted in accordance with Republic Act No. 10121 and its Implementing Rules and Regulations.

The Council shall serve as the principal local multi-stakeholder policy and coordination body for DRRM, subject to its statutory powers and responsibilities.

### Section 2. Composition

The CDRRMC shall be composed of the following, consistent with the statutory composition prescribed under Republic Act No. 10121:

Chairperson

\- City Mayor

Vice-Chairpersons

\- The heads of the designated city offices or departments responsible for prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, and rehabilitation and recovery, consistent with the applicable statutory and regulatory framework.

Members

- Heads of relevant City Government departments and offices;
- Representatives of concerned national government agencies operating in or serving Ormoc City;
- Liga ng mga Barangay representative;
- Philippine National Police;
- Bureau of Fire Protection;
- Philippine Coast Guard, where applicable;
- Armed Forces of the Philippines, where applicable;
- Relevant health, education, infrastructure, environment, social welfare, agriculture and utilities institutions;
- At least four (4) accredited civil society organization representatives, subject to applicable accreditation and selection requirements;
- Private sector representatives; and
- Other institutions or individuals invited by the Chairperson when their technical expertise or institutional mandate is relevant.

The City Legal Office shall validate the final membership list against the exact statutory composition and applicable IRR. The membership list shall be attached as Annex A to the signed Executive Order.

### Section 3. Functions of the Council

The CDRRMC shall perform the functions prescribed by Republic Act No. 10121 and its IRR, including:

a. Approving, coordinating, integrating, supervising and evaluating the implementation of the Local DRRM Plan;

b. Ensuring DRRM and climate change adaptation are mainstreamed into local development plans, programs and budgets;

c. Recommending the declaration and lifting of a State of Calamity, consistent with law;

d. Providing recommendations and technical inputs for the declaration of a State of Imminent Disaster, where applicable;

e. Coordinating the mobilization of resources and services of concerned offices and partner institutions;

f. Supporting inter-LGU and inter-agency coordination;

g. Ensuring that the needs of vulnerable and at-risk sectors are considered in policies, plans and operational decisions;

h. Reviewing disaster preparedness, response and recovery performance; and

i. Performing other functions mandated by law.

### Section 4. Standing Governance Committees

The CDRRMC shall organize the following functional committees, subject to the statutory cluster structure and the approved Local DRRM Plan:

1. Prevention and Mitigation;
2. Preparedness;
3. Response;
4. Rehabilitation and Recovery;
5. Risk Information, Planning and Mainstreaming; and
6. Governance, Finance and Resource Mobilization.

The committees shall be chaired or co-chaired by the appropriate lead offices based on their statutory mandates and technical responsibilities.

The CDRRMC Secretariat shall maintain a committee directory identifying the lead office, members, focal persons, deliverables, reporting frequency and escalation arrangements.

The committees shall not create parallel command structures or duplicate the statutory responsibilities of existing offices.

### Section 5. CDRRMC Secretariat

The CDRRMO shall serve as the permanent Secretariat of the CDRRMC, consistent with Republic Act No. 10121 and its IRR.

The Secretariat shall:

a. Prepare and circulate meeting notices, agendas and technical documents;

b. Maintain the official membership and focal-person directory;

c. Record minutes, resolutions, attendance and action points;

d. Maintain the CDRRMC decision and action tracker;

e. Consolidate committee reports and technical recommendations;

f. Monitor the implementation of Council decisions;

g. Prepare periodic governance and performance reports; and

h. Maintain the official records and archives of the Council.

The Secretariat shall distinguish between recommendations submitted for Council deliberation and decisions formally adopted by the Council.

### Section 6. Meetings and Quorum

The CDRRMC shall meet at least once every quarter and may convene special meetings when warranted by emerging risks, preparedness requirements, operational developments or other urgent matters.

The Chairperson may call a special meeting upon recommendation of the CDRRMO or upon request of a sufficient number of Council members, subject to the Council's adopted rules.

Quorum, voting and adoption of resolutions shall be governed by applicable law and the Council's duly approved internal rules. The Secretariat shall record attendance, quorum determination, decisions, dissenting views where formally raised, and assigned actions.

### Section 7. Activation for Emergencies

The CDRRMC may convene or activate its coordination arrangements when the situation requires strategic policy guidance, inter-agency coordination, resource mobilization or decisions within its statutory mandate.

Operational coordination shall be supported by the EOC under its separate Establishment Executive Order.

Where an ICS structure is activated, incident-level command shall remain with the designated Incident Commander. The CDRRMC shall provide policy direction and inter-agency support within its lawful authority and shall not issue conflicting tactical instructions to field response personnel.

### Section 8. Civil Society and Private Sector Participation

The Secretariat shall maintain a transparent and documented process for the participation of accredited CSOs and relevant private sector representatives.

The process shall:

a. Verify accreditation or eligibility in accordance with applicable rules;

b. Ensure representation of relevant sectors and communities;

c. Document the selection or designation process;

d. Provide access to non-confidential Council information and meeting schedules; and

e. Establish appropriate safeguards for conflicts of interest, confidentiality and data protection.

### Section 9. Reporting and Performance Review

The CDRRMC shall conduct an annual review of the implementation of the Local DRRM Plan, institutional readiness, risk reduction investments, preparedness and response performance, and recovery progress.

The Secretariat shall maintain a consolidated action tracker and submit periodic implementation reports to the Chairperson.

### Section 10. Initial Compliance Actions

Within sixty (60) days from the effectivity of this Executive Order, the Secretariat shall:

1. Validate and update the CDRRMC membership;
2. Confirm the representation of at least four accredited CSOs and the relevant private sector;
3. Issue the updated directory of Council members and committee focal persons;
4. Prepare the annual meeting calendar;
5. Consolidate existing Council resolutions and outstanding action points;
6. Submit proposed internal rules and Secretariat SOPs; and
7. Establish the CDRRMC decision and action tracker.

### Section 11. Repealing Clause

All previous executive orders or administrative issuances inconsistent with this Executive Order are hereby amended or superseded to the extent of such inconsistency.

### Section 12. Effectivity

This Executive Order shall take effect immediately upon signing and shall be disseminated to all concerned offices and institutions.

DONE in the City of Ormoc, Philippines, this \_\_\_\_ day of \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ 2026.

# Instrument 3. Proposed Executive Order Establishing the Ormoc City Emergency Operations Center

Draft executive issuance · Establishes the EOC as an operational coordination mechanism, not a separate command authority

## Republic of the Philippines

## City of Ormoc

## Office of the City Mayor

EXECUTIVE ORDER NO. \_\_\_\_\
Series of 2026

AN EXECUTIVE ORDER ESTABLISHING THE ORMOC CITY EMERGENCY OPERATIONS CENTER, PROVIDING FOR ITS ORGANIZATION, ACTIVATION LEVELS, OPERATING ARRANGEMENTS, INFORMATION MANAGEMENT, RESOURCE COORDINATION AND INTERFACE WITH THE INCIDENT COMMAND SYSTEM

### Section 1. Establishment

The Ormoc City Emergency Operations Center (EOC) is hereby established as the City's principal facility and coordination mechanism for emergency monitoring, information management, inter-office coordination, resource support, decision support and operational reporting.

The EOC shall be maintained and managed by the CDRRMO under the supervision of the City Mayor and in accordance with the CDRRMC's policies and the Manual of Operations.

### Section 2. Purpose

The EOC shall:

a. Maintain situational awareness of hazards, threats, incidents and emerging risks;

b. Receive, validate, consolidate and disseminate operational information;

c. Support early warning and public information coordination;

d. Facilitate coordination among City Government offices, barangays, national agencies, utilities, CSOs and private sector partners;

e. Track resource availability, requests, allocation and deployment;

f. Support strategic decision-making by the City Mayor and CDRRMC;

g. Maintain a common operating picture;

h. Support the activation and coordination of response arrangements; and

i. Maintain operational records, reports and lessons learned.

### Section 3. Institutional Relationship

The EOC shall be:

1. Operationally managed by the CDRRMO;
2. Available to support the CDRRMC's policy and coordination functions;
3. Activated through the procedures established in the MOO;
4. Linked to barangay-level monitoring and reporting arrangements;
5. Connected to relevant national and regional warning and coordination systems; and
6. Interoperable with the Incident Command System when activated.

The EOC shall not be construed as:

- A separate local government department;
- A substitute for the CDRRMC;
- A substitute for the CDRRMO;
- A field command post unless expressly designated for a specific incident and appropriately organized; or
- An authority empowered to override the Incident Commander or the lawful command structure of responding agencies.

### Section 4. EOC Activation Levels

The EOC shall operate under the following activation levels. The exact criteria and triggers shall be detailed in the EOC MOO and hazard-specific SOPs.

| Level                         | Operating condition                                                                                          | Minimum staffing and function                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Level 0 – Routine Monitoring  | No imminent threat or major incident; routine monitoring                                                     | Duty officer, monitoring and communications personnel, scheduled reporting                                                  |
| Level 1 – Enhanced Monitoring | Elevated threat, weather disturbance, warning advisory or developing incident                                | Expanded monitoring, focal-person notification, situation updates and readiness checks                                      |
| Level 2 – Partial Activation  | Significant incident or credible threat requiring multi-office coordination                                  | Core EOC team, operations and planning functions, resource tracking and regular situation reports                           |
| Level 3 – Full Activation     | Major emergency, multiple affected areas, significant resource demand or City-level coordination requirement | Full EOC staffing, executive coordination, structured information and resource management, continuous operational reporting |

The activation level shall be determined by the authorized official under the approved activation protocol, based on verified information, hazard-specific thresholds and the scale and complexity of the situation.

The EOC activation level shall not automatically determine the activation level of an ICS organization. The EOC and ICS may be activated at different scales depending on incident needs.

### Section 5. Authority to Activate

The City Mayor shall have the authority to direct EOC activation, consistent with law and the City's approved protocols.

The City DRRM Officer or designated duty officer may initiate or recommend activation based on verified hazard information, official warnings, field reports or other credible evidence.

In urgent situations, the designated duty officer may initiate immediate notification and minimum readiness measures under the approved protocol, subject to prompt confirmation by the authorized official.

The activation protocol shall specify who may approve activation, who may act in the absence of the primary authority, and how the decision shall be recorded.

### Section 6. EOC Functional Organization

The EOC shall be organized around the following functions, scaled according to activation level:

1. EOC Management / Coordination – oversees EOC operations, internal coordination and reporting to the authorized city leadership;
2. Operations – consolidates field information, tracks operational activities and coordinates with response agencies and barangays;
3. Planning and Situation Analysis – maintains the common operating picture, situation reports, projections and planning products;
4. Logistics – coordinates resource availability, requests, procurement or deployment referrals, facilities, transport and support services;
5. Administration and Finance – supports documentation of expenditures, personnel time, administrative requirements and financial records;
6. Public Information and Risk Communication – coordinates validated public information, warning messages, media releases and community advisories; and
7. Safety and Liaison – supports personnel safety coordination and liaison with partner agencies, as appropriate to the scale of operations.

The EOC functional organization may be adapted to the incident, but assigned functions and reporting relationships shall be documented.

### Section 7. EOC Manager

The City DRRM Officer shall designate an EOC Manager for each activation, subject to the approval arrangements in the MOO.

The EOC Manager shall:

a. Direct the internal functioning of the EOC;

b. Ensure that assigned functional positions are staffed;

c. Maintain coordination with the City Mayor, CDRRMC and Incident Commander, as appropriate;

d. Ensure that information is validated and properly disseminated;

e. Maintain the common operating picture and action tracker;

f. Coordinate resource requests and inter-office support;

g. Ensure the preparation and submission of situation reports; and

h. Ensure the orderly deactivation and archiving of EOC records.

The EOC Manager is responsible for EOC coordination and administration and does not automatically become the Incident Commander.

### Section 8. Incident Command System Interface

Where an incident requires an ICS structure, the designated Incident Commander shall exercise incident-level command and control in accordance with applicable ICS rules and the activated incident organization.

The EOC shall:

- Receive and consolidate situation reports from the Incident Command Post;
- Facilitate resource requests and inter-agency coordination;
- Provide policy and strategic information to the City Mayor and CDRRMC;
- Maintain citywide situational awareness;
- Coordinate support from offices not directly assigned to the incident organization; and
- Avoid issuing conflicting tactical instructions to field teams.

The Incident Commander shall communicate operational priorities, resource requirements and incident developments through the established reporting channel.

### Section 9. Barangay and Community Reporting Interface

The EOC shall maintain a designated reporting and communications arrangement with all barangays.

Each barangay shall identify authorized focal persons and alternates for:

a. Hazard monitoring and local observations;

b. Receipt and acknowledgment of warnings;

c. Reporting of incidents and affected populations;

d. Evacuation and protective action updates;

e. Requests for assistance and resources; and

f. Confirmation of service restoration and return conditions.

The CDRRMO shall establish backup communication methods for barangays with weak or disrupted telecommunications.

### Section 10. Information Management and Common Operating Picture

The EOC shall maintain a Common Operating Picture (COP) based on verified and time-stamped information.

At a minimum, the COP shall contain:

- Incident and hazard status;
- Geographic location and affected areas;
- Official warning and advisory status;
- Population and facilities potentially or actually affected;
- Evacuation status and displacement information;
- Critical infrastructure and lifeline status;
- Response actions and responsible agencies;
- Resource availability, requests and deployment status;
- Access constraints and urgent needs; and
- Key decisions, outstanding actions and next reporting deadlines.

Information shall be classified as verified, provisional or unverified. Unverified information shall not be presented as confirmed fact in public advisories or executive situation reports.

### Section 11. Communications and Public Information

The EOC shall coordinate with authorized public information offices and relevant technical agencies to ensure that public advisories are accurate, timely, consistent and accessible.

Warnings shall use appropriate languages and formats for affected communities, including Cebuano/Ormocanon and accessible formats where needed.

A warning message shall, at minimum, identify:

1. The hazard and affected location;
2. The expected timing and level of threat;
3. The population or sector at risk;
4. The required protective action;
5. The responsible source or issuing authority;
6. The time of issuance and next update; and
7. Available assistance or reporting channels.

### Section 12. Resource Management

The EOC shall maintain a resource status and tracking system covering personnel, equipment, vehicles, supplies, facilities and other available response resources.

All requests, approvals, dispatches, transfers, receipts and returns shall be documented.

The EOC shall coordinate resource support but shall not bypass the procurement, accounting, property, budget and audit controls applicable to the City Government.

### Section 13. Duty Roster and Continuous Operations

The CDRRMO shall maintain an approved EOC duty roster and alternate roster.

The roster shall specify:

- Primary and alternate personnel;
- Duty schedules;
- Reporting and handover requirements;
- Contact details and escalation channels;
- Minimum staffing per activation level;
- Rest and relief arrangements; and
- Access to EOC facilities and equipment.

### Section 14. Training, Exercises and Evaluation

The City shall conduct periodic EOC orientation, functional exercises, communications tests and full-scale or multi-agency exercises as appropriate.

After each activation or exercise, the CDRRMO shall conduct an After-Action Review and prepare an Improvement Plan identifying corrective actions, owners, deadlines and verification requirements.

### Section 15. Initial Implementation Requirements

Within sixty (60) days from effectivity, the CDRRMO shall submit for approval:

1. The EOC organization chart and functional position descriptions;
2. EOC activation and deactivation SOP;
3. EOC duty roster and succession arrangements;
4. Communications and contact directory;
5. Common Operating Picture and situation report templates;
6. Resource request and tracking forms;
7. EOC–ICS interface protocol;
8. Barangay reporting and last-mile communication protocol; and
9. Minimum equipment, facility and continuity requirements.

### Section 16. Effectivity

# Instrument 4. Standard Operating Procedure for Declaration and Lifting of a State of Calamity

SOP-DRRM-GOV-04 · Draft for legal validation and adoption by the appropriate local authority

## 4.1 Purpose

This SOP establishes a consistent, evidence-based and auditable process for recommending, declaring, implementing and lifting a State of Calamity in Ormoc City.

It is intended to ensure that the decision is based on documented damage assessment, needs analysis and applicable legal criteria, and that the resulting interventions are coordinated, time-bound, properly funded and subject to monitoring.

## 4.2 Legal basis

The principal legal basis is Section 16 of Republic Act No. 10121 and its applicable IRR, together with relevant provisions of the Local Government Code and applicable national and local issuances.

Statutory decision rule: The local sanggunian declares and lifts a State of Calamity upon the recommendation of the LDRRMC, based on the assessment of damage and needs. The SOP must not be interpreted as authorizing the City Mayor, CDRRMO or EOC to independently exercise the sanggunian's statutory declaration or lifting authority.

## 4.3 Scope

This SOP applies to:

- The CDRRMC;
- CDRRMO and EOC;
- City Government departments and offices;
- Barangays;
- Concerned national government agencies;
- Local response organizations; and
- Other institutions requested to support assessment, response and recovery.

## 4.4 Guiding principles

1. Legality: All recommendations and actions shall comply with applicable law.
2. Evidence: Decisions shall be supported by verified damage, needs and impact information.
3. Proportionality: Recommended measures shall be appropriate to the scale, severity and duration of the emergency.
4. Inclusiveness: Assessments shall identify the differentiated needs of women, children, older persons, persons with disabilities, Indigenous Peoples where applicable, low-income households and other at-risk groups.
5. Accountability: Decisions, resource commitments and actions shall be recorded and traceable.
6. Coordination: All offices shall operate through agreed reporting and coordination arrangements.
7. Recovery orientation: Declaration shall support timely response and recovery, not substitute for longer-term risk reduction and reconstruction planning.

## 4.5 Triggers for assessment and consideration

A State of Calamity may be considered when a disaster has occurred and the available evidence indicates that the applicable statutory criteria have been met.

Potential triggers include:

- Significant loss of life or injury;
- Extensive damage to homes, public infrastructure, livelihoods or essential services;
- Large-scale displacement or evacuation;
- Disruption of critical lifelines or basic services;
- Emergency needs exceeding the capacity of affected communities or local response arrangements; and
- Other conditions prescribed by applicable law and regulations.

These are operational screening considerations, not substitutes for the exact statutory criteria. The City Legal Office shall verify the legal threshold and documentary requirements before adoption.

## 4.6 Roles and responsibilities

| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Barangay DRRM Committees                   | Initial incident reporting, local validation, affected-population information and urgent needs reporting |
| CDRRMO                                     | Coordinates assessment process, consolidates evidence and prepares technical recommendation              |

## 4.7 Procedure

### Step 1. Incident reporting and initial validation

The barangay or concerned office shall report the incident to the CDRRMO or EOC through the established reporting channel.

The EOC shall record:

- Date and time of incident;
- Location and affected barangays;
- Hazard type and available official warning information;
- Initial reported casualties and affected households;
- Initial damage and service disruption;
- Immediate needs; and
- Source and verification status of the information.

The EOC shall distinguish between initial reports and validated findings.

### Step 2. Activation of assessment arrangements

The City DRRM Officer shall recommend the activation of the appropriate assessment teams or technical working groups.

The CDRRMO shall coordinate with relevant offices to define:

- Assessment objectives;
- Geographic coverage;
- Sectoral responsibilities;
- Data collection methods;
- Reporting timelines;
- Required technical expertise; and
- Consolidation arrangements.

The assessment shall use applicable national tools and methodologies, including relevant damage assessment, needs analysis and post-disaster assessment protocols.

### Step 3. Conduct of damage and needs assessment

Each responsible office shall assess impacts within its mandate.

The assessment shall, as applicable, document:

1. Human impacts, including deaths, injuries, missing persons and displacement;
2. Housing damage and habitability;
3. Damage to public buildings and infrastructure;
4. Disruption of water, power, telecommunications, transport and other lifelines;
5. Health, sanitation and protection needs;
6. Agriculture, fisheries, livestock and livelihood losses;
7. Education and social service disruption;
8. Environmental impacts and access constraints;
9. Priority needs of vulnerable sectors; and
10. Estimated response and early recovery requirements.

Each report shall identify the assessment date, geographic coverage, methodology, responsible assessor, source records and level of confidence.

### Step 4. Consolidation and technical analysis

The CDRRMO shall consolidate the sectoral reports and prepare a citywide assessment summary.

The EOC shall support consolidation by maintaining:

- Incident chronology;
- Affected-area map;
- COP;
- Resource and assistance tracker;
- Situation reports; and
- Outstanding information requests.

The consolidated report shall identify information gaps and distinguish confirmed figures from estimates.

### Step 5. CDRRMC deliberation and recommendation

The CDRRMC shall review the consolidated assessment and determine whether the evidence supports recommending the declaration of a State of Calamity.

The deliberation shall consider:

- Applicable statutory criteria;
- Scale and severity of impacts;
- Geographic extent;
- Capacity of affected barangays and City Government;
- Urgency and nature of required interventions;
- Expected duration of disruption;
- Resource and financing implications; and
- Risks of further deterioration.

The Council's recommendation shall be documented in a resolution or other legally appropriate instrument, supported by the assessment report.

### Step 6. Submission to the Sangguniang Panlungsod

The CDRRMC Secretariat shall transmit the recommendation, assessment report and supporting documents to the City Mayor and Sangguniang Panlungsod through the appropriate official channels.

The submission shall include:

- CDRRMC resolution or recommendation;
- Consolidated damage and needs assessment;
- Maps and sectoral reports;
- Summary of affected population and facilities;
- Proposed geographic coverage;
- Recommended scope and duration of emergency measures; and
- Other documents required by law or the sanggunian.

### Step 7. Sangguniang Panlungsod action

The Sangguniang Panlungsod shall deliberate and act on the recommendation in accordance with applicable law and its rules of procedure.

If approved, the declaration shall be embodied in the appropriate sanggunian resolution or other legally required instrument.

The resolution shall clearly state:

- The legal basis;
- The affected geographic areas;
- The factual basis and assessment references;
- The date of effectivity;
- The authorized measures and relevant limitations; and
- Any reporting or review requirements.

### Step 8. Dissemination and implementation

Upon declaration, the CDRRMO and EOC shall coordinate dissemination to:

- All City Government offices;
- Barangays;
- Concerned national agencies;
- Utilities and service providers;
- CSOs and private sector partners; and
- Affected communities through appropriate communication channels.

Each responsible office shall implement measures within its legal mandate and approved authority.

The declaration shall not be treated as an automatic exemption from procurement, accounting, auditing, environmental, building safety, data protection or other applicable requirements.

### Step 9. Monitoring and reporting

The CDRRMO shall maintain a consolidated monitoring record covering:

- Response and early recovery actions;
- Resource mobilization and utilization;
- Assistance delivered;
- Unmet needs;
- Service restoration;
- Population movement and displacement;
- Emerging risks; and
- Progress toward recovery.

The EOC shall maintain operational logs and situation reports for the duration of activation.

### Step 10. Review and recommendation for lifting

The CDRRMO shall periodically review whether the conditions that justified the declaration continue to exist.

The review shall consider:

- Restoration of essential services;
- Reduction of immediate life-safety threats;
- Evacuation and displacement status;
- Access to affected communities;
- Remaining urgent humanitarian needs;
- Status of critical infrastructure;
- Capacity of local systems to manage residual impacts; and
- Transition from emergency response to recovery arrangements.

When conditions warrant, the CDRRMC shall deliberate and recommend lifting the State of Calamity to the Sangguniang Panlungsod.

### Step 11. Lifting by the Sangguniang Panlungsod

The Sangguniang Panlungsod shall exercise the statutory authority to lift the declaration, upon recommendation of the CDRRMC and in accordance with applicable law.

The lifting instrument shall specify the geographic scope, effective date and any continuing administrative or recovery arrangements.

Lifting the declaration shall not automatically terminate ongoing recovery, rehabilitation, social protection or reconstruction programs.

### Step 12. Post-incident review and records closure

Within the period prescribed by the MOO or applicable policy, the CDRRMO shall convene an After-Action Review with the participating offices and partners.

The review shall identify:

- What worked;
- What did not work;
- Delays and coordination failures;
- Resource and capability gaps;
- Data and communication issues;
- Good practices;
- Corrective actions; and
- Responsible offices and completion dates.

The Secretariat shall archive the declaration, assessment, resolutions, reports, operational logs and post-incident review.

# Instrument 5. Standard Operating Procedure for Declaration of a State of Imminent Disaster and Implementation of Anticipatory Actions

SOP-DRRM-GOV-05 · Draft for validation against Republic Act No. 12287, its certified IRR and applicable funding rules

## 5.1 Purpose

This SOP establishes the City's procedures for identifying an imminent disaster threat, evaluating credible forecasts and impact information, recommending a State of Imminent Disaster (SID), supporting the City Mayor's declaration by Executive Order, implementing anticipatory actions, managing resources and documenting results.

The purpose is to enable timely action before disaster impacts occur, where credible forecasts, warnings and risk information indicate that a hazard may cause serious impacts within a defined period.

## 5.2 Legal basis and legal safeguards

This SOP shall be implemented in accordance with Republic Act No. 12287, its Implementing Rules and Regulations, and other applicable laws and issuances.

The SOP shall preserve the following distinctions:

1. A State of Imminent Disaster is a legal declaration relating to an impending disaster threat under the statutory framework.
2. A Pre-Disaster Risk Assessment (PDRA) is a technical process for assessing the potential impacts of a forecast or anticipated hazard.
3. Anticipatory actions are time-bound actions implemented before the anticipated impacts to reduce expected loss and harm.
4. The declaration and anticipatory actions shall be based on the applicable statutory recommendation, evidence and procedural requirements.
5. The City Mayor's authority to declare by Executive Order shall be exercised only upon the recommendation required by law. The exact role and sequence of the Regional DRRM Council and other competent authorities must be followed.
6. The declaration does not dispense with applicable budget, procurement, accounting, auditing, data privacy or other legal controls.

Important validation requirement: Before this SOP is adopted, the City Legal Office and CDRRMO must verify the certified text of Republic Act No. 12287 and its IRR, particularly the provisions on the recommending authority, declaration requirements, anticipatory action protocols, fund eligibility, lead times, social amelioration, data sharing and post-lifting reporting. The assessment workbook records specific requirements, including a 45-day post-lifting report and certain fund-use and tranche provisions. Those details must be checked against the certified IRR and applicable DBM and COA rules before they are incorporated into binding city procedures.

## 5.3 Scope

This SOP applies to the City Mayor, CDRRMC, CDRRMO, EOC, City Government departments, barangays, relevant national government agencies, utilities, CSOs and other partners involved in anticipatory action.

It applies to hazards for which credible forecasts, warnings or other recognized technical information can provide sufficient lead time for protective action.

## 5.4 Operational principles

- Forecast-informed: Use authoritative forecasts and warnings from competent technical agencies.
- Impact-based: Assess who and what may be affected, where, when and how severely.
- Action-oriented: Link every trigger to a defined protective action, responsible office, resource requirement and deadline.
- Time-critical: Establish decision and implementation deadlines based on the available lead time.
- Inclusive: Address the needs of at-risk households and sectors, including people who may face barriers to receiving warnings or evacuating.
- Accountable: Record the evidence, recommendation, declaration, funding, implementation and results.
- Proportionate: Match actions and resource commitments to the forecast risk and applicable legal authority.

## 5.5 Hazard-specific anticipatory action protocols

The CDRRMO shall coordinate the development and maintenance of hazard-specific protocols. These shall be based on the City's risk profile and the official warning and forecasting systems of competent agencies.

At a minimum, protocols should cover:

| Hazard                                                                          | Technical information to monitor                                                                              | Potential anticipatory actions                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tropical cyclone and extreme rainfall                                           | PAGASA bulletins, rainfall forecasts, wind signals, track and impact forecasts                                | Prepositioning, evacuation readiness, suspension recommendations, protection of facilities and lifelines                  |
| Riverine flooding, including relevant river systems such as Anilao and Malbasag | Rainfall forecasts, river levels, upstream conditions, local gauges and flood models where available          | Targeted warnings, pre-emptive evacuation where justified, deployment of rescue assets, protection of critical facilities |
| Rain-induced landslide                                                          | PAGASA rainfall information, MGB susceptibility information, field observations and local rainfall thresholds | Targeted warnings, access restrictions, evacuation and relocation of people in threatened areas                           |
| Tsunami affecting Ormoc Bay and coastal areas                                   | PHIVOLCS tsunami bulletins, earthquake information, sea-level observations and official advisories            | Immediate warning dissemination, evacuation to designated safe areas, closure of exposed coastal access points            |
| Other locally relevant hazards                                                  | Authoritative agency warnings and validated local monitoring                                                  | Actions defined in the relevant hazard-specific protocol                                                                  |

The City shall not invent or independently substitute technical warning thresholds for those issued by competent national agencies. Locally developed thresholds may supplement official warnings only when technically validated, documented and approved through the appropriate process.

## 5.7 Procedure

### Step 1. Continuous monitoring and threat identification

The EOC shall monitor official forecasts, warnings, bulletins and relevant field information.

The duty officer shall record:

- Source agency;
- Bulletin or advisory number;
- Date and time of issuance;
- Hazard type;
- Forecast location and affected areas;
- Expected timing;
- Severity or intensity information;
- Forecast confidence or uncertainty, where provided;
- Potentially exposed population and assets; and
- Recommended monitoring or readiness actions.

### Step 2. Initial threat screening

The CDRRMO shall conduct an initial screening to determine whether the threat requires:

- Routine monitoring;
- Enhanced monitoring;
- Activation of the PDRA process;
- Pre-positioning or readiness actions under existing authority; or
- Consideration of a State of Imminent Disaster.

The City shall not delay urgent protective actions that are already authorized and warranted merely because the SID declaration process is being considered.

### Step 3. Convening the PDRA process

Where the threat warrants formal assessment, the CDRRMO shall convene the relevant technical offices and agencies.

The PDRA shall identify:

1. Hazard characteristics and forecast scenario;
2. Geographic areas potentially affected;
3. Exposed population and vulnerable groups;
4. Potential impacts on housing, infrastructure, lifelines and essential services;
5. Potential livelihood and economic impacts;
6. Evacuation and access constraints;
7. Critical facilities and essential services at risk;
8. Potential secondary or cascading impacts;
9. Existing response and coping capacities;
10. Priority anticipatory actions; and
11. Required resources, lead times and responsible offices.

The PDRA shall clearly identify assumptions, uncertainties, source information and the time at which the assessment was prepared.

### Step 4. Development of the Anticipatory Action Matrix

The CDRRMO shall coordinate the preparation of an action matrix that links each identified risk to a concrete action.

The matrix shall include:

| Required field         | Description                                                     |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hazard / scenario      | The forecast hazard and relevant scenario                       |
| Trigger                | Official warning, forecast threshold or validated local trigger |
| Geographic coverage    | Barangays, communities, facilities or sectors concerned         |
| Anticipatory action    | Specific action to be implemented before impact                 |
| Responsible office     | Office with operational responsibility                          |
| Supporting offices     | Offices or partners providing support                           |
| Target completion      | Deadline based on lead time                                     |
| Resource requirement   | Personnel, equipment, supplies, transport and funding           |
| Approval authority     | Official or body authorized to approve the action               |
| Evidence of completion | Log, acknowledgment, dispatch record, photograph or other proof |
| Status                 | Not started, ongoing, completed, delayed or cancelled           |

Actions may include:

- Pre-positioning of food, water, medicines, rescue assets and other essential supplies;
- Activation of evacuation centers and transport arrangements;
- Pre-emptive evacuation where justified by the risk and authorized procedure;
- Protection of critical infrastructure and essential services;
- Public warnings and targeted advisories;
- Assistance to people requiring mobility or medical support;
- Protection of livestock, agricultural assets and livelihoods where feasible;
- Continuity measures for health, education, water and other essential services; and
- Social amelioration or other assistance in accordance with applicable law and eligibility rules.

### Step 5. Review and recommendation

The CDRRMO shall submit the PDRA and proposed Anticipatory Action Matrix to the CDRRMC or the appropriate authority for review and recommendation, following the statutory process.

The review shall establish:

- Whether the threat meets the legal basis for consideration of an SID;
- Whether the forecast and impact evidence is sufficiently credible;
- Whether the anticipated impacts justify the proposed declaration;
- Whether proposed actions can be completed within the available lead time;
- Whether funding and resources are legally available;
- Whether affected populations can be reached through warning and assistance systems; and
- Whether the recommended actions are within the authority of the responsible offices.

The recommendation shall be formally documented and transmitted through the legally required channel.

### Step 6. Declaration by the City Mayor

Upon receipt of the recommendation required by law, the City Mayor may issue an Executive Order declaring a State of Imminent Disaster, consistent with Republic Act No. 12287 and its IRR.

The EO shall identify:

- Legal basis;
- Hazard and forecast scenario;
- Geographic coverage;
- Date and time of effectivity;
- Technical basis and recommendation reference;
- Authorized anticipatory action arrangements;
- Responsible offices and coordination mechanisms;
- Resource and reporting arrangements; and
- Review, extension or lifting arrangements, as permitted by law.

The declaration shall not be issued solely on the basis of an unsupported forecast or an unverified social media report.

### Step 7. Activation and dissemination

Upon issuance of the EO:

1. The EOC shall record the declaration and disseminate it to concerned offices and barangays.
2. The CDRRMO shall activate the relevant hazard-specific protocol.
3. Responsible offices shall implement their assigned actions.
4. Barangays shall acknowledge receipt of warnings and instructions.
5. The EOC shall maintain a live action tracker and COP.
6. Public information shall be issued through authorized channels.

### Step 8. Implementation of anticipatory actions

Each responsible office shall implement the actions assigned in the approved matrix.

The EOC shall track:

- Action;
- Responsible office;
- Target deadline;
- Actual completion time;
- Geographic coverage;
- Number and type of people reached;
- Resources deployed;
- Outstanding constraints; and
- Evidence of completion.

If a planned action cannot be completed, the responsible office shall report the reason, risk implications and alternative measures.

### Step 9. Anticipatory social amelioration

Where social amelioration is included in the approved action package, the responsible social welfare office shall coordinate beneficiary identification, eligibility validation, assistance delivery and reporting.

The process shall:

- Use the applicable beneficiary registry or authorized data source;
- Verify eligibility and avoid duplication;
- Prioritize households facing heightened exposure and barriers to self-protection;
- Maintain clear records of assistance;
- Apply data minimization and access controls; and
- Ensure lawful data sharing in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 and applicable rules.

The CDRRMO and EOC shall coordinate information and operational requirements but shall not independently determine social welfare eligibility outside the authority of the responsible office.

### Step 10. Monitoring and adjustment

The EOC shall monitor changes in the forecast, official warnings and actual field conditions.

The CDRRMO shall recommend adjustments to the action matrix when:

- The forecast changes materially;
- The hazard's timing or geographic coverage changes;
- New areas become exposed;
- A protective action becomes unsafe or impracticable;
- Additional resources become necessary; or
- The threat diminishes and planned actions should be modified or stood down.

Changes shall be recorded with the time, decision-maker, rationale and offices notified.

### Step 11. Lifting or termination of the SID

The SID shall be lifted or otherwise terminated in accordance with the applicable statutory requirements and the authority prescribed by Republic Act No. 12287 and its IRR.

The CDRRMO shall prepare a technical recommendation based on:

- Updated official forecasts and warnings;
- Actual hazard development;
- Residual risk;
- Completion or cancellation of anticipatory actions;
- Continuing safety requirements; and
- The legal requirements for lifting.

The City Mayor shall issue the appropriate instrument where authorized and required by law.

### Step 12. Post-lifting report and review

The CDRRMO shall coordinate the preparation and submission of the required post-lifting report within the period prescribed by law and the IRR. The assessment workbook identifies a 45-day post-lifting reporting requirement; the City Legal Office must confirm the precise trigger, recipient, required contents and computation of the reporting period against the certified IRR before this is adopted as a binding deadline.

The report should document:

- Forecast and warning chronology;
- Basis for the PDRA and declaration;
- Anticipatory actions planned and implemented;
- Offices and partners involved;
- Population and sectors reached;
- Resources and funds used;
- Actions not completed and reasons;
- Actual hazard impacts compared with the forecast scenario;
- Lessons learned;
- Recommendations; and
- Corrective actions.

# Instrument 6. Integrated RACI Accountability Framework

The following matrices translate the governance instruments into assigned responsibilities. They should be adopted as controlled annexes to the MOO and reviewed whenever the City's organizational structure, statutory mandates or operating arrangements change.

RACI definitions

- R – Responsible: Performs or coordinates the work.
- A – Accountable: Holds final responsibility for the task or decision. There should normally be one accountable role for each activity.
- C – Consulted: Provides technical advice or is consulted before action.
- I – Informed: Receives information on the action or decision.

The RACI matrix does not create legal authority. Where law assigns a decision to a specific official or body, that statutory authority prevails over any administrative assignment in the matrix.

## 9.1 Important legal drafting safeguards

Before final adoption, the City Legal Office should ensure that:

1. The CDRRMO ordinance does not create positions or organizational arrangements inconsistent with approved staffing and budget rules.
2. The CDRRMC EO does not alter statutory membership or decision-making powers.
3. The EOC EO does not create a parallel incident command structure.
4. The State of Calamity SOP preserves the statutory role of the Sangguniang Panlungsod.
5. The SID SOP follows the exact recommendation and declaration sequence prescribed by Republic Act No. 12287 and its IRR.

# 11. Recommended Immediate Management Decisions

The institutional assessment indicates that Ormoc should prioritize the following decisions to move from developing institutional maturity toward established and consistently implemented DRRM governance.

1\. Authorize the preparation of the CDRRMO Ordinance. This addresses the institutionalization gap and provides the legal foundation for a permanent, properly structured and adequately staffed office.

2\. Issue an updated CDRRMC Executive Order. This formalizes statutory representation, CSO and private sector participation, committee arrangements and Secretariat accountability.

3\. Issue the EOC Establishment Executive Order. This clarifies the EOC's legal and operational purpose, activation arrangements, functional organization and interface with ICS.

4\. Adopt the two declaration SOPs. These are essential for ensuring that the City can lawfully and consistently manage both post-impact disaster declarations and anticipatory action before an expected disaster.

5\. Approve the RACI and governance templates. These convert institutional roles into documented assignments, decisions, reports, resource records and measurable accountability.

6\. Conduct an EOC and SID functional exercise. The City should test the instruments before relying on them during an actual emergency.

7\. Establish a corrective action monitoring system. The CDRRMO should track each high-priority institutional gap until it is resolved and independently verified.

## 12. Closing institutional recommendation

The assessment results provide a clear reason to treat these instruments as an integrated governance reform package rather than as isolated administrative documents. The combined institutional maturity score of 2.18/4.00, the EOC maturity score of 1.66/4.00, the separately assessed institutional interface score of 1.29/4.00, and the 30 high-priority gaps indicate that the City's next step should be to institutionalize decision rights, operating procedures, coordination arrangements and accountability mechanisms.

The CDRRMO ordinance should establish the permanent institutional foundation. The CDRRMC Executive Order should formalize the City's multi-stakeholder governance and coordination body. The EOC Executive Order should establish the operational coordination mechanism. The two declaration SOPs should provide the legally compliant pathways for managing disasters that have occurred and threats that are imminent. The RACI matrices and templates should ensure that the responsibilities established in those instruments are translated into routine, traceable practice.

The central implementation principle is that every mandate must have an accountable institutional owner, every decision must have a lawful authority, every operational process must have a documented procedure, and every critical action must produce evidence that can be monitored, reviewed and improved.

This is the foundation for moving Ormoc's DRRM governance system from an institutionally developing arrangement toward a more integrated, anticipatory, accountable and resilient city system.
