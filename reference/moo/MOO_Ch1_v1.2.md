# 1. Introduction

Disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) in Ormoc City requires an integrated institutional system that connects executive leadership, policy direction, technical management, operational coordination and community-level action. Its effectiveness depends not only on the existence of the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (CDRRMC), the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO) and the Emergency Operations Center (EOC), but also on the clarity of their respective mandates, decision-making authority, functional relationships, accountability arrangements and operational interfaces.

This chapter establishes the legal, governance and institutional accountability framework for the City's CDRRMO and EOC. It defines how the City Government organizes, directs, coordinates, monitors and continuously improves its DRRM functions across normal operations, preparedness, anticipatory action, emergency response and recovery.

The chapter adopts a systems-thinking perspective: disaster risk is understood as the consequence of interconnected hazards, exposure, vulnerability, institutional capacities and dependencies across social, economic, environmental and physical systems. Accordingly, the City's DRRM governance arrangements must operate as an integrated system rather than as a collection of independent offices, committees and emergency procedures.

The chapter also applies Total Quality Management (TQM) principles to institutional operations. These include process ownership, evidence-based decision-making, stakeholder participation, standardization, performance measurement, corrective action and continuous improvement.

The institutional provisions are informed by the **Ormoc CDRRMO–EOC Institutional Assessment Tool v1.7 (workbook v1-7, 11 November 2026)**, particularly its nine assessment sheets.

The assessment is treated as a diagnostic baseline, not as a substitute for legal compliance verification or formal institutional approval.

## 1.2 Interpretation of institutional Maturity and Compliance

The assessment methodology distinguishes institutional maturity from mandatory compliance. A domain may achieve a relatively high maturity index while remaining legally non-compliant because one or more mandatory requirements have not attained the required rating.

This distinction is particularly important for Ormoc:

- CDRRMO Legal Mandate and DRRM Governance (A1) has a maturity index of 2.47/4.00, but only 66.7% mandatory compliance.

- EOC Legal Basis and Concept of Operations (B1) has a maturity index of 2.23/4.00, but only 20% mandatory compliance.

- The overall mandatory compliance rate is 32.9%, against a combined maturity index of 1.98.

The implication is that institutional improvements must address both capability and compliance. A procedure that is practiced informally cannot automatically be treated as an established institutional control. Similarly, the existence of an executive order, organizational chart or operational facility does not establish compliance unless the relevant requirements are documented, approved, implemented and supported by evidence.

The assessment uses the following evidence-based maturity logic:

| **Rating** | **Maturity classification**  | **Institutional interpretation**                                                             |
|------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0          | Absent                       | No established arrangement.                                                                  |
| 1          | Initial / Ad hoc             | Practice depends on individuals and is not adequately documented.                            |
| 2          | Developing                   | Arrangement exists but is incomplete, outdated, unapproved or inconsistently implemented.    |
| 3          | Compliant / Established      | Applicable standards are met through approved documentation and demonstrated implementation. |
| 4          | Advanced / Institutionalized | Compliance is sustained, tested, reviewed and improved through documented learning.          |

For purposes of this MOO, a procedure shall not be considered fully institutionalized merely because it appears in the Manual. Its implementation must be demonstrated through records, operational outputs, verification and periodic review.

# 2. Purpose

This chapter establishes the legal and institutional governance arrangements governing the CDRRMO, CDRRMC and EOC.

It aims to:

1.  Establish a clear and legally grounded division of authority among the Local Chief Executive, CDRRMC, CDRRMO and EOC.

2.  Define the institutional responsibilities, decision rights and accountability of participating City Government offices.

3.  Institutionalize transparent, inclusive and evidence-based DRRM governance.

4.  Establish procedures for CDRRMC meetings, resolutions, recommendations and action monitoring.

5.  Standardize the City's procedures for State of Calamity and State of Imminent Disaster declarations.

6.  Strengthen vertical and horizontal coordination among the City Government, barangays, national government agencies, neighbouring LGUs, CSOs, private-sector partners and humanitarian organizations.

7.  Establish process ownership, responsibility assignments and decision-making controls using the RACI framework.

8.  Embed risk-informed governance, quality assurance, performance monitoring and continuous improvement into institutional operations.

# 3. Scope and Application

This chapter applies to the following institutional actors:

- Office of the City Mayor;

- CDRRMC and its Secretariat;

- CDRRMO Head and functional divisions;

- EOC Manager and EOC functional sections;

- Sangguniang Panlungsod;

- City Government departments, offices and units;

- Barangay DRRMCs in all 85 barangays;

- National government agencies and regional DRRM institutions;

- Accredited CSOs, ACDVs, private-sector organizations and other partners.

Its provisions apply under three operating conditions:

| **Operating condition**                       | **Institutional focus**                                                                                                                                | **Primary governance mechanism**                                                  |
|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Normal operations                             | Risk reduction, institutional planning, preparedness, capacity development, monitoring and continuity.                                                 | CDRRMC oversight and CDRRMO management.                                           |
| Heightened preparedness / anticipatory action | Pre-Disaster Risk Assessment, warning-action decisions, resource readiness, anticipatory measures and possible State of Imminent Disaster declaration. | CDRRMC coordination, Mayor's executive authority and CDRRMO technical leadership. |
| Emergency operations                          | Incident coordination, situational awareness, resource mobilization, policy support and operational reporting.                                         | Activated EOC, Incident Management Team and designated response clusters.         |

The Manual applies to all hazards and emergencies within the City's jurisdiction, including hydrometeorological, geological, climate-related, technological and human-induced hazards, subject to applicable institutional mandates.

It shall be read together with the City's approved DRRM plans, contingency plans, Public Service Continuity Plan, climate and disaster risk assessment, development plans, land-use plans and other applicable local policies.

## 4.4 Policy Hierarchy and Conflict Resolution

In implementing this chapter, the following order shall apply:

1.  Philippine Constitution and applicable statutes;

2.  Valid implementing rules and regulations;

3.  National administrative and operational issuances;

4.  Provincial and regional coordination requirements, where applicable;

5.  City ordinances and valid executive issuances;

6.  Approved plans, policies and institutional procedures;

7.  MOO protocols, checklists and supporting forms.

In case of inconsistency, the higher-ranking legal or regulatory instrument shall prevail. The CDRRMO shall refer material legal ambiguities to the City Legal Office and seek technical clarification from OCD Region VIII or the appropriate national agency.

The MOO shall maintain a legal and policy register, with an identified custodian and review date for each applicable instrument.

# 5. Systems-Thinking Framework for DRRM Governance

## 5.1 Institutional System Architecture

Ormoc City's DRRM governance shall operate as a connected system comprising five interdependent layers:

1.  **Executive governance**: Political leadership, statutory authority and executive decision-making.

2.  **Policy and oversigh**t: CDRRMC deliberation, policy direction, recommendations and monitoring.

3.  **Institutional management**: CDRRMO planning, technical management, administration and implementation.

4.  **Operational coordination**: EOC, ICS, response clusters and incident management arrangements.

5.  **Community and external interface:** BDRRMCs, CSOs, private-sector institutions, utilities, national agencies and neighbouring LGUs.

**Conceptual relationship**. This is not a substitute for the statutory chain of authority or the approved ICS organizational chart.

The architecture recognizes that information, authority and resources move through different pathways.

- Executive authority flows through legally established government structures.

- Technical information flows from monitoring institutions, technical offices and communities toward decision-makers.

- Operational instructions flow through the approved command and coordination arrangements.

- Resources flow through authorized mobilization and accountability mechanisms.

- Feedback and performance information flow back into planning, policy and institutional improvement.

The CDRRMO shall ensure that these pathways are connected but not conflated.

# 7. Institutional Governance Processes and Workflows

The MOO shall define governance as a series of connected, controlled and measurable processes. Each process shall have:

- A defined trigger or initiating condition;

- A designated process owner;

- Responsible actors;

- A legally authorized decision-maker;

- Required inputs and evidence;

- Defined outputs and records;

- Escalation and exception procedures; and

- Performance and compliance indicators.

This approach ensures that governance decisions can be traced from initiation to approval, implementation, reporting and review.

## 7.1 Process 1: CDRRMC Governance and Policy Decision-making

**Purpose**: Ensure that DRRM policies, plans, priorities and institutional decisions are deliberated, approved or recommended by the appropriate authority and translated into accountable actions.

![Figure 8: 7.1 Process 1: CDRRMC Governance and Policy Decision-making](/library/ch1v12/fig-08.webp)

**Workflow GOV-01: CDRRMC Governance**

1.  **Initiation and agenda development**  
    > CDRRMO identifies policy issues, assessment findings, risk developments and matters requiring Council action.

2.  **Technical preparation**  
    > Concerned offices submit technical papers, evidence, financial implications and recommendations.

3.  **Secretariat processing  
    > **CDRRMO consolidates agenda items, issues meeting notices and circulates supporting documents.

4.  **Council deliberation  
    > **CDRRMC members review evidence, assess implications and deliberate on proposed actions.

5.  **Decision and documentation**  
    > The Council approves matters within its authority or adopts recommendations for submission to the appropriate approving authority.

6.  **Implementation and assignment  
    > **The Secretariat records decisions, assigns action owners and establishes target dates.

7.  **Monitoring and feedback  
    > **CDRRMO tracks implementation and reports unresolved actions at succeeding meetings.

**Required Output**

Approved minutes, resolutions or recommendations, decision register and action-monitoring report.

**Operating Requirements**

| **Control point**    | **Required standard**                                                                                                     |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Meeting frequency    | At least once every three months and as necessary.                                                                        |
| Agenda               | Include standing items on risk developments, LDRRMP implementation, funding, assessment gaps and institutional readiness. |
| Supporting documents | Technical papers, risk information, financial implications and draft resolutions, as applicable.                          |
| Documentation        | Minutes, attendance, decisions, dissenting views where material, assigned owners and deadlines.                           |
| Follow-through       | Action register reviewed at every succeeding meeting.                                                                     |
| Review               | Annual assessment of Council functionality and effectiveness.                                                             |

The assessment records a score of 4 for A1.3. This established practice should be retained and strengthened through systematic action tracking, particularly for the 30 high-priority institutional gaps.

## 7.2 Process 2: CDRRMC Secretariat Management

**Purpose**: Ensure that Council proceedings, decisions and institutional records are complete, traceable and accessible to authorized stakeholders.

![Figure 9: 7.2 Process 2: CDRRMC Secretariat Management](/library/ch1v12/fig-09.webp)

**Workflow GOV-02: Secretariat Operations**

1.  Receive agenda requests and supporting documents.

2.  Prepare agenda, meeting notice and document package.

3.  Coordinate with the Chairperson on scheduling and meeting requirements.

4.  Record attendance, proceedings, resolutions and decisions.

5.  Prepare draft minutes and resolutions for review and approval.

6.  Distribute approved records to authorized members.

7.  Enter decisions into the action register and assign responsible offices.

8.  Track implementation and preserve the official records.

**Required records**: Meeting calendar, agenda, attendance, minutes, resolutions, action register and document distribution log.

The CDRRMO shall develop a Secretariat SOP and use standardized templates. This responds to A1.4, which is already rated 4, while addressing the need to document the existing practice more systematically.

## 7.3 Process 3: Institutional Policy and Ordinance Development

**Purpose**: Ensure that local DRRM policies are legally sound, risk-informed, coordinated and implementable.

The process shall be used for the proposed CDRRMO enabling ordinance, updated CDRRMC Executive Order, EOC establishment issuance, ICS institutionalization and amendments to existing DRRM ordinances.

![Figure 10: 7.3 Process 3: Institutional Policy and Ordinance Development](/library/ch1v12/fig-10.webp)

**Workflow GOV-03: Policy Development and Approval**

1.  **Identify the policy gap.** CDRRMO documents the legal, institutional or operational deficiency.

2.  **Develop the technical proposal**. Prepare the proposed policy, institutional implications and implementation requirements.

3.  **Conduct consultation.** Engage affected City offices, CDRRMC members, CSOs and relevant partners.

4.  **Legal review.** The City Legal Office reviews legality, authority, consistency and enforceability.

5.  **Resource review**. HRMO and Budget Office assess staffing, funding and administrative implications.

6.  **Approva**l. Submit the proposal to the Mayor or Sangguniang Panlungsod, depending on the nature of the instrument.

7.  **Publication and dissemination**. Circulate the approved policy to responsible offices and affected stakeholders.

8.  **Implementation and evaluation**. Monitor compliance, assess effectiveness and recommend revisions.

**Decision rule**: The appropriate approving authority shall be determined by the legal nature of the proposed instrument. An ordinance shall not be substituted by an Executive Order where legislative action is required.

## 7.4 Process 4: State of Calamity Declaration

The City shall maintain a documented protocol consistent with Section 16 of RA 10121 and its IRR.

**Practice to date (IAT v1.7, A1.6).** The SP adopted a State of Calamity for Typhoon Odette (SP Res. No. 2021-366, December 2021). The CDRRMC recommended a State of Calamity for the 2024 dengue outbreak for a period not exceeding one year, liftable earlier on the Council's recommendation (CDRRMC Res. No. 06 s.2024). These cases show the process in use. They also show the gaps this protocol closes: no written criteria, no standard recommendation package, and Quick Response Fund resolutions that do not cite the declaration relied on.

A State of Calamity declaration is based on the results of damage assessment and needs analysis. It shall be distinguished from the anticipatory declaration mechanism under RA 12287.

**Workflow GOV-04: State of Calamity**

1.  **Incident assessment**  
    > BDRRMCs, response agencies and the CDRRMO report casualties, affected populations, infrastructure damage, livelihood disruption and service interruptions.

2.  **RDANA consolidation**  
    > CDRRMO coordinates the validation and consolidation of damage and needs information.

3.  **Technical recommendation  
    > **CDRRMO prepares the assessment and submits it to the CDRRMC.

4.  **CDRRMC deliberation**  
    > The Council evaluates the evidence and adopts a recommendation where the legal criteria are met.

5.  **Legislative declaration  
    > **The Sangguniang Panlungsod considers and issues the declaration or lifting through the appropriate resolution.

6.  **Implementation  
    > **The City Government implements applicable remedial measures, resource arrangements, assistance and reporting requirements.

7.  **Monitoring and lifting  
    > **The CDRRMO monitors continuing impacts and prepares the technical basis for recommending lifting when appropriate.

**Required Outputs**

RDANA, CDRRMC recommendation, Sangguniang Panlungsod resolution, implementation records and lifting documentation.

The City shall maintain the following supporting records:

- Technical assessment and recommendation;

- RDANA and other supporting reports;

- CDRRMC resolution;

- Sangguniang Panlungsod resolution;

- Post-declaration action register;

- Resource utilization and accountability records; and

- Lifting recommendation and corresponding resolution.

The protocol shall also address coordination where national or regional declarations, infrastructure closures or emergency restrictions affect Ormoc City without automatically constituting a local State of Calamity declaration.

Every resolution that uses the Quick Response Fund shall cite the declaration relied on: the SP resolution number and date, or the Proclamation number and date for a national or regional declaration. The reading of the QRF trigger shall be validated with the City Legal Office and COA.

## 7.5 Process 5: State of Imminent Disaster Declaration and Anticipatory Action

**New statutory Governance Process**

RA 12287 establishes a mechanism for declaring a State of Imminent Disaster before a disaster occurs, based on the applicable forecast, pre-disaster risk assessment and legal criteria.

The IRR released on 29 September 2026 provides the implementing framework for this mechanism.

The assessment identifies A1.8 as a mandatory requirement with a score of 1. The City has no localized procedure, request template or Mayor's Executive Order template documented in the evidence reviewed.

The following workflow shall therefore be institutionalized.

![Figure 11: 7.5 Process 5: State of Imminent Disaster Declaration and Anticipatory Action](/library/ch1v12/fig-11.webp)

**Workflow GOV-05: State of Imminent Disaster**

1.  **Hazard forecast and trigger monitoring  
    > **CDRRMO Operations and Warning monitors official forecasts, advisories and hazard-specific triggers from PAGASA, PHIVOLCS and other competent agencies.

2.  **Pre-Disaster Risk Assessment  
    > **CDRRMO coordinates the assessment of exposure, vulnerability, projected severity, likely impacts, affected sectors, lead time and available response capacity.

3.  **Technical recommendation**  
    > CDRRMO prepares the PDRA findings and proposed anticipatory measures for CDRRMC consideration.

4.  **Regional coordination and recommendation  
    > **The City coordinates with RDRRMC VIII through OCD Region VIII and submits the prescribed request and supporting documentation.

5.  **Regional decision and response**  
    > RDRRMC VIII undertakes the action required under the Act and IRR. The request, acknowledgment and prescribed response timeline are documented.

6.  **Local declaration**  
    > Upon the applicable recommendation, the City Mayor may declare a State of Imminent Disaster through an Executive Order covering the jurisdiction forecast to be affected.

7.  **Activation of anticipatory measures**  
    > Authorized offices implement approved early actions, including pre-positioning, evacuation, protection of critical infrastructure and targeted social assistance.

8.  **Funding and accountability  
    > **Budget, Accounting, Procurement and implementing offices document commitments, disbursements, utilization and outputs in accordance with the IRR and applicable DBM-COA rules.

9.  **Lifting and post-declaration reporting  
    > **The City observes the applicable lifting procedures and submits the required post-lifting report to OCD within the prescribed 45-day period.

The process shall observe the following safeguards:

1.  A forecast alone shall not automatically trigger a declaration. The applicable legal criteria and PDRA requirements must be met.

2.  The CDRRMO shall provide technical recommendations, not independently exercise the declaration authority.

3.  The local declaration shall follow the recommendation and coordination mechanism prescribed by RA 12287 and its IRR.

4.  Anticipatory actions shall be proportionate to the projected impacts, hazard lead time, available resources and applicable legal authority.

5.  Measures shall observe inclusion, protection, do-no-harm and no-regrets principles.

6.  The use of funds shall follow the statutory funding limits, tranche arrangements and applicable financial regulations.

7.  Decisions, approvals, actions and expenditures shall be documented for audit and evaluation.

**Funding control**: As reflected in the assessment workbook, the IRR provides for an anticipatory-action allocation of up to 10% of the 70% LDRRMF component and a 30%/70% tranche arrangement associated with the prescribed three-day lead-time rule. The precise timing, eligibility, disbursement and documentation requirements must be implemented using the certified IRR and applicable DBM-COA guidance. The MOO shall not treat this allocation as an additional fund outside the statutory LDRRMF framework.

**Independent Component City clarification:** Ormoc's status as an Independent Component City must be considered when establishing its vertical coordination arrangements. The City shall obtain written confirmation from OCD Region VIII on the application of the IRR's regional and provincial coordination provisions, particularly whether any provincial declaration has legal applicability to Ormoc. The City's ICC status shall not be used as a reason to bypass the RDRRMC recommendation mechanism expressly established by RA 12287.

## 7.6 Process 6: Institutional Escalation and Inter-agency Coordination

Institutional escalation shall occur when:

- The scale of an incident exceeds the capacity of a barangay;

- Multiple barangays require coordinated intervention;

- City resources are insufficient;

- Specialized national or regional assistance is required;

- Critical infrastructure or essential services are disrupted; or

- A statutory declaration or executive decision is necessary.

RA 10121, Section 15, provides the general coordination framework: barangay-level leadership where a barangay is affected, city/municipal leadership where two or more barangays are affected, and progressively higher coordination levels when impacts exceed the relevant territorial scale. Higher councils and institutions support the LGU's primary response responsibility.

![Figure 12: 7.6 Process 6: Institutional Escalation and Inter-agency Coordination](/library/ch1v12/fig-12.webp)

The operational workflow shall be:

1.  Incident or risk information is reported to the appropriate local authority.

2.  The affected BDRRMC undertakes initial action and reports to the City.

3.  CDRRMO validates the information and assesses the level of support required.

4.  The CDRRMC and EOC coordinate City-wide institutional action, as appropriate.

5.  The City requests external assistance through the prescribed channels when required.

6.  The receiving institution acknowledges and assigns the request.

7.  The CDRRMO monitors the response and maintains the institutional record.

8.  Unresolved requirements are escalated through the appropriate higher-level coordination mechanism.

## 7.7 Process 7: Governance Review and Corrective Action

All governance processes shall be subject to periodic review.

The CDRRMO shall:

1.  Consolidate compliance and performance information;

2.  Compare actual results against approved targets;

3.  Identify gaps, recurring problems and systemic causes;

4.  Assign corrective actions to accountable offices;

5.  Establish completion dates and required resources;

6.  Verify implementation;

7.  Report unresolved issues to the CDRRMC; and

8.  Update policies and procedures when necessary.

This process shall link the Institutional Assessment Tool, Action Plan, CDRRMC action register, annual LDRRMP monitoring and the MOO revision mechanism.

## 8.5 RACI Compliance Rules

The following rules shall govern all RACI matrices in the MOO:

1.  Each activity shall have one clearly designated Accountable role.

2.  Multiple Responsible roles may be assigned where work is genuinely shared.

3.  Consulted roles shall be limited to those whose technical or institutional input is necessary.

4.  Informed roles shall receive the relevant outputs without becoming unnecessary approval points.

5.  RACI assignments shall be reviewed whenever the organizational structure, legal mandate or delegation changes.

6.  Where a statutory body exercises collective authority, the Council or Sanggunian shall be identified as the accountable decision-making body, with the designated Chairperson responsible for convening and presiding over proceedings.

7.  Each RACI matrix shall identify the approving authority for the final output.

The detailed job action sheets and position-level RACI matrices shall be developed under the organizational and EOC modules.

# 9. Total Quality Management and Institutional Performance

## 9.1 Quality Management Approach

The CDRRMO shall apply Total Quality Management principles to the design, implementation, monitoring and improvement of its governance processes.

TQM shall be treated as an institutional management approach rather than a separate administrative activity. Its purpose is to ensure that DRRM services are reliable, timely, accountable, evidence-based and responsive to the needs of affected communities.

![Figure 14: 9.1 Quality Management Approach](/library/ch1v12/fig-14.webp)

The following principles shall guide implementation:

|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Customer and stakeholder focus | Decisions and services shall respond to the needs of communities, affected populations, responders and institutional partners. |
| Leadership and accountability  | Each process shall have a designated owner and authorized decision-maker.                                                      |
| Process orientation            | Activities shall be standardized through workflows, SOPs, checklists and defined outputs.                                      |
| Systems approach               | Institutional processes shall be managed as interconnected functions rather than isolated activities.                          |
| Evidence-based decisions       | Decisions shall be supported by verified risk information, assessment results, operational reports and documented evidence.    |
| People engagement              | Personnel, barangays, CSOs and partners shall participate in process design, implementation and improvement.                   |
| Continuous improvement         | Findings from exercises, incidents, audits and assessments shall result in corrective and preventive action.                   |

## 9.5 Corrective and Preventive Action

The CDRRMO shall maintain a Corrective and Preventive Action Register (CAPA) linked to:

- Institutional Assessment Tool findings;

- CDRRMC resolutions;

- Internal and external audit findings;

- After-action reviews;

- Exercise evaluation reports;

- Incident and response reports;

- Stakeholder feedback; and

- Applicable changes in laws and policies.

Each corrective action shall identify:

| **CAPA field**       | **Required information**                                                                   |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Reference            | Source of the finding or non-conformity                                                    |
| Problem statement    | Specific deficiency or process failure                                                     |
| Root cause           | Underlying organizational, procedural, technical or resource-related cause                 |
| Risk consequence     | Potential effect on life safety, service delivery, compliance or institutional performance |
| Corrective action    | Specific intervention to address the deficiency                                            |
| Accountable owner    | Single office or official responsible for completion                                       |
| Responsible actors   | Personnel or offices undertaking the work                                                  |
| Target date          | Approved completion deadline                                                               |
| Required resources   | Budget, personnel, equipment or technical assistance                                       |
| Verification         | Evidence that the action was implemented                                                   |
| Effectiveness review | Evidence that the original problem has been resolved and has not recurred                  |

A finding shall not be closed merely because an action has been initiated. Closure requires evidence of completion and verification of effectiveness.

# 16. Concluding Notes

This chapter establishes the legal, institutional and governance foundation for the integrated operation of Ormoc City's CDRRMC, CDRRMO and EOC.

It recognizes that urban resilience is not produced by a single office or emergency facility. It emerges from the effective interaction of executive leadership, institutional capacity, policy coordination, risk information, financial governance, community participation and operational systems.

The institutional assessment demonstrates that Ormoc has established important DRRM governance practices, particularly regular CDRRMC meetings, Secretariat functions, DRRM representation in development and financial bodies, and existing planning and partnership mechanisms. However, gaps in legal institutionalization, EOC authority, anticipatory governance and cross-office coordination continue to constrain the effectiveness of the wider system.

The provisions of this chapter respond to these issues by establishing:

- Clear institutional mandates and decision rights;

- Defined process ownership and responsibility assignments;

- Standardized governance workflows;

- Legally differentiated declaration procedures;

- Formalized institutional and inter-agency interfaces;

- RACI-based accountability;

- TQM and PDCA-based quality assurance; and

- Evidence-based monitoring and continuous improvement.

The ultimate objective is to move Ormoc City's DRRM governance from arrangements that are partly dependent on individual initiative toward a legally grounded, process-driven, integrated and continuously improving institutional system.

This foundation shall support the implementation of the succeeding modules, particularly organizational management, preparedness, EOC operations, information management, resource mobilization, recovery and performance improvement.
