## 1. Introduction and Assessment-Informed Rationale

Chapters 1 to 9 set what the City does to reduce, prepare for, respond to and recover from disasters, how it pays for it, how it learns, whom it works with and how it builds capacity. This chapter asks a different question: how do these gains last? A good Manual can be shelved by the next administration; a trained team can leave; a platform can lapse when an account is not renewed; an award can be won one year and lost the next. Sustainability is the work of making the system outlast the people who built it.

The chapter has three parts. **Institutional sustainability** (sections 5 to 7) keeps the legal, human, financial and digital foundations in place. **Recognition** (section 8) treats Gawad KALASAG and the SGLG as independent evidence of progress, not as goals in themselves. **Deep resilience** (section 9) sets the path beyond compliance: fewer losses, faster recovery and adaptation to a changing climate, measured against the Sendai Framework and the Ten Essentials that the City's own plan adopted.

The baseline shows where sustainability is weakest (Figure 10.1). Three of the gaps that carry the most weight in the whole assessment are about permanence: the legal basis of the CDRRMO as a regular office with its JMC 2014-1 divisions (A1.1, High), the filling of the minimum plantilla (A2.3, High) and the alternate EOC (B2.7, score 0, High). Continuity of public services (A3.4) and digital continuity and security (B3.3, B3.5) are still developing. The CDRRMO Head is permanent (A2.2), the EOC has its own budget line (B7.5), and the City takes part in recognition systems (A10.7), but the succession order, the readiness self-check (B7.6) and the follow-up of recognition gaps are not yet documented (Table 10.1).

### 1.1 What the Records Show

- **A legal basis exists, but not the full structure.** Ordinance No. 008, s. 2014 (26 June 2014), amended by Ordinance No. 011, s. 2015, creates the CDRRMO under the Office of the City Mayor with a Head (SG-23) and three LDRRMO I posts. Chapter 1 recasts the A1.1 finding as an amendment of that ordinance to provide the three JMC 2014-1 divisions and their staffing (Annex 1-A), and proposes an EO establishing the EOC (Annex 1-C).

- **People.** The CDRRMO Head holds a permanent position. The FGD reported about 47 staff, of whom four are permanent; most are on job-order or casual terms. The CDP 2024–2030 programmes the creation of regular positions (PPAs 1.1.1–1.1.2) and the 10-Year Plan lists 4 + 4 + 2 plantilla items. SP Res. No. 2023-347 records the CDRRMO's lack of response manpower.

- **Recognition goals.** The CDP sets the goal of achieving the Gawad KALASAG award every year and full compliance with the SGLG disaster preparedness indicators. The 10-Year Plan cites the Gawad KALASAG Seal and the SGLG and observes that the City's performance has been assessed mainly through such national systems.

- **Networks for deeper resilience.** The City has an MCR2030 commitment, the NRC Resilient LGU partnership (SP Res. No. 2021-036) and the EDC Adopt-a-City programme, and is the model LGU of the DENR–EDC Project TRANSFORM (EO 2023-0026). The 10-Year Plan bases its monitoring on the UNDRR Ten Essentials.

- **The platform.** MODE runs in a Supabase project under an organisation named for the City Government of Ormoc, in Tokyo (ap-northeast-1), and on a Vercel team, both on free tiers during development (ADR-026). The architecture decisions require City ownership, time-boxed developer access removed at turnover, portability to any Postgres host, and a decision by the City on paid, City-owned hosting with backups before MODE is used for real operations. Weekly backup and quarterly restore runbooks exist in the repository.

### 1.2 Design Considerations

1.  **Institutions, not individuals.** Every critical role has an alternate and a succession order; every critical document has an owner office and a version; every agreement names positions.

2.  **Law before budget, budget before people.** An office that exists in ordinance and has a line in the budget survives changes of leadership better than one that exists by practice.

3.  **Continuity is tested, not assumed.** The alternate EOC, the PSCP, the backups and the offline mode are proven once a year.

4.  **Recognition is evidence.** Awards confirm progress measured independently; chasing scores for their own sake drifts toward paperwork.

5.  **The City owns its system.** Data, code, accounts and keys belong to the City and can be run by a local IT contractor without the developer.

6.  **Beyond compliance.** Compliance is the floor. The aim is fewer deaths, smaller losses and faster recovery, measured over years.

## 2. Purpose and Intended Results

This chapter sets how the City keeps its DRRM institutions, people, funds, systems and knowledge in place over time; how it uses recognition systems; and how it plans its progress from compliance toward deeper resilience.

Intended results:

1.  The CDRRMO has a full legal basis with its JMC 2014-1 divisions, and the EOC is established by issuance (A1.1, B1.1).

2.  Every critical position has an alternate and a documented succession order, and leadership transitions follow a handover protocol (A2.2).

3.  The PSCP, the alternate EOC and the EOC readiness self-check are tested every year (A3.4, B2.7, B7.6).

4.  MODE runs on City-owned, paid hosting with backups, restore tests, an access review and an information-security policy before it is used for real operations (B3.3, B3.5; MR-10).

5.  The City takes part in the Gawad KALASAG Seal and the SGLG each year, and every gap found is entered in the CAPA register and acted on (A10.7).

6.  A sustainability review each year checks that the legal, human, financial, digital and knowledge foundations are still in place, and updates the resilience roadmap.

7.  At the end of the engagement, the Manual and MODE are handed over to City custody with no dependence on the developer.

## 5. Institutional Sustainability

Five foundations keep the City's DRRM system standing (Figure 10.2). If any one is missing, the others carry more weight than they can bear: a well-funded office without legal permanence can be dissolved; a well-written Manual without trained people is not used; a platform without backups can lose years of records in a day.

### 5.1 Legal and Institutional Permanence

The CDRRMO already exists in ordinance. Permanence now means amending Ordinance No. 008, s. 2014 so that it provides the JMC 2014-1 divisions (Administration and Training, Research and Planning, Operations and Warning) and their functions and staffing; establishing the EOC by issuance with its levels, activation authority and roster; keeping the CDRRMC constituted with all its seats; and keeping the Manual approved and versioned. The sustainability review tracks each instrument (SUS-F01): drafted, endorsed, enacted, implemented.

### 5.2 People and Succession

The Head, the division chiefs, the EOC Manager and the duty officers each have a named alternate and a written order of succession (Chapter 2, section 11.2). When a holder leaves, is on leave or is unavailable in an emergency, the alternate acts without waiting. After each local election, or when the CDRRMO Head changes, a transition protocol (SOP 10-02) briefs the incoming Mayor and CDRRMC on the City's risks, the Manual, open CAPA items, the budget lines and any declaration in force, and confirms the designations that the new administration must reissue. Critical knowledge held by one person is written into the Manual, a protocol or the Vault.

### 5.3 Financial Sustainability

Recurring costs that keep the system working are budgeted as lines, not found event by event: EOC operations and maintenance (B7.5), equipment preventive maintenance and replacement (A8.3, B7.6), stock replenishment (Chapter 6), training and exercises (Chapter 9), insurance (A4.7), and the hosting and support of MODE once the City adopts it for real operations. The sustainability review checks that each line is in the LDRRMFIP and in use (Chapter 6, FIN-F03).

## 6. Continuity of Operations

The City must keep its essential DRRM functions running when the EOC building, its power, its network or its people are affected by the same disaster. The PSCP (Chapter 3) names the essential functions and how they continue; the alternate EOC (Chapter 2, section 2.8) gives a second site; the EOC readiness self-check (B7.6) confirms both are ready. This chapter sets one annual continuity check (SOP 10-04) that brings the three together: a relocation drill to the alternate EOC, a PSCP exercise for the CDRRMO's essential functions, and the EOC Capability Assessment Checklist for both sites. Findings go to the CAPA register.

## 7. Digital Continuity and Information Security

MODE now holds the Manual, the assessment, the protocols, the logbook, the Vault and the EOC's operating records. Its continuity is part of the City's continuity. The architecture already favours it: the data are plain Postgres and SQL migrations that can move to any Postgres host; the app works offline for routine tasks and keeps authority actions online-only; the City owns the project; the developer's access is time-boxed. What remains is to put the operating controls in place and test them (Figure 10.3).

## 8. Recognition Systems

The LGU self-assesses with the prescribed LDRRMCO Assessment Tool, attaches its means of verification and a notarised Omnibus Sworn Statement, and submits to OCD Region VIII, whose Regional Validation Committee runs a desk review and may validate in the field. Results for CY 2026 are due not later than 30 November 2026. Special Awards are suspended for CY 2026 pending a supplemental guideline.

### 8.2 Seal of Good Local Governance

The SGLG under RA 11292 includes disaster preparedness among its assessment areas. Its criteria are set each year by the DILG and change from year to year, so this Manual does not fix them. Each year the CDRRMO obtains the current criteria from the DILG City Office, maps them to the IAT and the Manual, and prepares the evidence through the Vault (SOP 10-05).

### 8.3 Using Recognition as Evidence

The IAT and the recognition systems measure overlapping things in different ways. Annex 10-B maps the Gawad KALASAG criteria to IAT domains so that one body of evidence in the Vault serves both. Results and assessor comments are entered in the CAPA register like any other finding. The City does not create documents only to satisfy an assessor; every document filed as evidence must also be in use. The MODE Gawad KALASAG self-check tool is to be updated to the CY 2026 tiers before it is used (Coherence Analysis, open issue II.2-2).

## 9. Deep Resilience: Beyond Compliance

Compliance with the IAT Mandatory items and the Gawad KALASAG minimum requirements is the floor. Deep resilience is measured by outcomes: fewer deaths and missing persons, fewer people affected, smaller economic losses, less disruption of basic services, and faster recovery (Sendai Targets A to D), and by the City's position on the UNDRR Ten Essentials, which the 10-Year Plan adopted as the base of its monitoring.

### 9.1 The Resilience Roadmap

**Figure 10.5 proposes three horizons aligned with the LDRRMP 2023–2032 and the Sendai Framework. The first institutionalises what the Manual sets out; the second consolidates it through practice and the plan's mid-term evaluation; the third deepens it toward Sendai 2030 and the next LDRRMP. Within each horizon the order of work follows this priority order: legal constitution first, then the operating systems that carry whole domains, then high-leverage compliance items. Targets for the IMI, Mandatory compliance and the Interface Index are set by the CDRRMC; this chapter sets none.**

### 9.2 Deepening Areas

- **Anticipatory action maturity.** From the first funded AA line and the first declaration under RA 12287 toward impact-based triggers, pre-agreed actions by sector and evaluated results (Chapters 4, 6 and 7).

- **Climate adaptation and nature-based solutions.** Linking the LDRRMP with the LCCAP 2019–2030 and Project TRANSFORM: watershed protection in the Anilao–Malbasag catchment, mangrove restoration and climate-smart agriculture (Chapter 8, Part C).

- **Risk-informed development.** Bringing CDRA scenarios into the CLUP and CDP and screening investments (Part C, C1), so that new risk is not created.

- **Social protection and inclusion.** Connecting relief and recovery with social protection and livelihood programmes so that the poorest recover first (Chapter 5).

- **Learning city.** Peer learning through MCR2030 and the NRC, and contributing Ormoc's own lessons to other LGUs (Chapter 7).

### 9.3 Innovation Pipeline

New ideas (a sensor network, a community feedback tool, a new financing instrument) are tried as pilots with a stated purpose, a measure of success, a time limit and a data-protection check; evaluated; and then adopted into the Manual, scaled or dropped (SOP 10-06). A pilot is not a programme until it has been evaluated. Adopted innovations are shared with peers through Chapter 7.

## 10. Stewardship and Handover of the Manual and MODE

The Manual and MODE were developed with the City under a fourteen-week engagement. Their value lasts only if the City can keep, revise and run them alone. At the end of the engagement, and again whenever a custodian changes, the stewardship protocol (SOP 10-07) confirms that the City holds the approved Manual and its source files, the Library versions, the repository, the hosting accounts and keys, the runbooks, the training materials and the handover record; that the developer's access has been removed; and that a named City custodian and a local IT contractor can maintain the platform. The Manual is revised under Chapter 7 (SOP 7-08) and approved only by the CDRRMO Head.

## 17. Concluding Notes for Chapter 10 and the Manual

Ormoc has learned at great cost what disasters do: the 1991 flash flood, Yolanda, the 2017 earthquake and the typhoons since. It has built a Council, an Office, a fund, plans, partnerships and a record of helping others. This Manual gathers those pieces into one system and adds the controls that let the City see whether the system works and fix it when it does not.

The test of the Manual is not its approval but its use: a warning turned into action in time, an anticipatory action funded before impact, a family that finds a safe evacuation centre, a barangay that counts its people within hours, a recovery that builds back safer, a lesson that changes a procedure. This last chapter asks the City to protect that system from the slow forces that erode institutions, and to keep raising its standard year after year.

Writing these procedures does not make the system last. Until the CDRRMO's full legal basis is enacted, the alternate EOC has been drilled, MODE runs on City-owned hosting with a tested restore, the first sustainability review has been held and the stewardship handover is complete, this chapter remains a controlled working draft.
