# How to Use This Manual

This Manual is one document for two bodies: the permanent City DRRM Office (CDRRMO) and the Emergency Operations Center (EOC) that the City activates when needed. Start with the Introduction. It explains the purpose, the legal basis, the assessment and the conventions used in every chapter.

- **To find a procedure**, use the Master Procedure Register (Annex A). It lists every SOP with its chapter, owner, quality stage and key record.

- **To act in an emergency**, go to Chapter 4. Its SOPs run in the order of a real event, from warning receipt to deactivation.

- **To check a legal point**, read the legal framework section (section 4) of the chapter, then the official text. The official text always governs.

- **To see what is still unconfirmed**, read the validation register at the end of each chapter.

Each chapter follows the same pattern: an at-a-glance panel, the assessment findings, purpose and scope, the legal framework, the processes, the SOPs, the RACI, performance measures, the implementation priorities and the references. Tables and figures are numbered by chapter (Table 4.3 is the third table in Chapter 4). Captions follow APA style: number in bold, title in italics, and a note below.

## Quality Management Approach

The Manual applies Total Quality Management (TQM). In practice this means three things. Every process has an owner and a record. Every process is measured. Every gap found becomes a corrective action that is closed only on proof that it worked. The cycle is Plan–Do–Check–Act (Figure FM.1).

# Executive Summary
Ormoc City CDRRMO–EOC Manual of Operations

**Institutionalising Risk-Informed Governance, Operational Readiness, and Deep Resilience**

## 1. Strategic Context and Institutional Transformation

The Ormoc City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO)–Emergency Operations Center (EOC) Manual of Operations (MOO) establishes an integrated institutional and operational framework for strengthening disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) across the City Government of Ormoc and its 85 Philippine Standard Geographic Code (PSGC) barangays.

The Manual addresses a fundamental institutional challenge: translating existing disaster management mandates, operational experience, and inter-agency coordination practices into a coherent, legally anchored, documented, and verifiable system of governance. It connects routine institutional responsibilities with preparedness, anticipatory action, emergency response, recovery, and continuous improvement.

Rather than treating disaster management primarily as an event-driven response function, the Manual positions DRRM as a continuing responsibility of local governance. It establishes institutional arrangements, decision rights, operational procedures, accountability mechanisms, and information systems intended to embed DRRM in planning, budgeting, service delivery, and development processes.

The Manual is assessment-informed and operational by design. Its provisions respond to identified institutional and operational gaps through standard operating procedures (SOPs), responsibility assignments, prescribed records, performance measures, and corrective actions. The MODE (Manual of Operations for the DRRMO and EOC) digital platform is proposed as an enabling environment for controlled documentation, operational monitoring, institutional records, and performance assurance.

### 1.1 Institutional Assessment and Diagnostic Baseline

The Manual draws on the Ormoc CDRRMO–EOC Institutional Assessment Tool (IAT), Version 1.7, dated 11 November 2026. The instrument comprises 140 indicators across 22 domains: Part A covers the CDRRMO (82 indicators in 10 domains); Part B covers the EOC (43 indicators in seven domains); and Part C covers institutional interfaces with other City offices and partners (15 indicators in five domains).

The Institutional Maturity Index (IMI) combines Parts A and B. Part C is reported separately as an Institutional Interface Index and is not added to the IMI.

The baseline indicates that operational experience and existing practices have not consistently translated into approved institutional arrangements, documented controls, demonstrated use, and verifiable evidence of compliance.

### 1.2 Systems Diagnosis and Institutional Leverage Points

Beyond identifying individual compliance gaps, the assessment examines institutional structures and recurring patterns that sustain operational weaknesses. The systems diagnosis highlights three principal archetypes:

- Shifting the burden. Immediate operational demands are repeatedly addressed through temporary staffing, event-specific arrangements, and informal problem-solving. The reported workforce of approximately 47 personnel includes four permanent plantilla employees; this reported figure requires validation against HRMO records.

- Growth and underinvestment. Expanding responsibilities in DRRM, anticipatory action, emergency coordination, and institutional performance have not been matched consistently by investments in permanent staffing, EOC infrastructure, information management, and operational redundancy.

- Drifting goals. Institutional performance risks being measured by emergency response effort rather than demonstrable compliance, preparedness, procedural effectiveness, and sustained improvements in resilience.

The central transformation is from fragmented, event-driven arrangements toward an integrated, continuously functioning, and accountable DRRM operating system.

### 2.2 Principal Institutional Contributions

- **Governance and legal institutionalisation.** Clarifies the responsibilities of the City Mayor, Sangguniang Panlungsod, CDRRMC, CDRRMO, and EOC; identifies the need to formalise EOC authority and distinguish declaration processes.

- **End-to-end operational procedures**. Covers multi-hazard early warning, preparedness, anticipatory action, EOC activation and deactivation, incident coordination, resource mobilisation, reporting, and transition to recovery. Proposed EOC alert postures are White (normal), Blue (partial activation), and Red (full activation), aligned with the National Disaster Response Plan resource postures (White, normal operations; Blue, at least 50% of resources on standby; Red, all resources available).

- **Risk-informed planning and spatial integration.** Connects CDRA findings with CLUP zoning, CDP investment decisions, contingency planning, and Public Service Continuity Planning.

- **Multi-sectoral coordination and accountability.** Establishes responsibility arrangements and institutional interfaces involving barangays, City departments, regional and national agencies, utilities, civil society, academic institutions, and private-sector partners.

- **Digital continuity and quality assurance.** MODE is designed to support controlled records, role-based access, operational logbooks, documented decisions, annual assessments, and corrective action tracking.

## 3. Strategic Priorities and Actionable Institutional Improvements

### 3.1 Governance, Organisational Capacity, and Institutional Authority

The immediate priority is to establish the institutional foundations required for a functioning CDRRMO–EOC system.

- Advance the proposed CDRRMO departmental ordinance amendment through the Sangguniang Panlungsod, consistent with applicable national organisational guidelines.

- Issue an appropriate EOC establishment executive order defining authority, relationship with the CDRRMC and OpCen, activation arrangements, and coordination boundaries with ICS.

- Formalise the three functional divisions: Administration and Training; Research and Planning; and Operations and Warning.

- Develop a permanent staffing and workforce plan, including competency requirements, duty rosters, succession arrangements, and personnel welfare safeguards.

- Designate an Anticipatory Action focal person and formalise institutional responsibilities.

### 3.2 Risk Intelligence, Preparedness, and Operational Readiness

- Establish geospatial information governance and verification procedures for authoritative hazard information from PAGASA, PHIVOLCS, and MGB.

- Mainstream CDRA findings into land-use planning, development investment screening, contingency planning, and public service continuity.

- Develop a City Public Service Continuity Plan supported by Business Impact Analyses and essential-function inventories.

- Establish systematic quality assurance of Barangay DRRM Plans across all 85 barangays.

- Strengthen EOC activation, early warning, reporting, resource coordination, and incident management procedures.

- Conduct exercises to test warning thresholds, operational rosters, inter-office coordination, and the ICS–EOC interface.

### 3.3 Recovery, Rehabilitation, and Build Back Better

- Formulate a Pre-Disaster Recovery Framework identifying sectoral responsibilities, recovery priorities, financing options, and implementation arrangements.

- Designate and develop RDANA and PDNA teams.

- Establish a controlled transition mechanism transferring unresolved response requirements into recovery processes.

- Apply hazard exposure, climate resilience, environmental safeguards, and social inclusion criteria to reconstruction investments.

- Strengthen monitoring and accountability for recovery programmes until completion.

### 3.5 Monitoring, Evaluation, and Institutional Learning

The Manual establishes a continuous improvement mechanism through the Plan–Do–Check–Act (PDCA) cycle.

- Annual IAT assessment and evidence-based validation.

# Introduction to the Manual

## 1. Purpose and Application of the Manual

This Manual of Operations (MOO) provides one integrated operating reference for the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO) and the Emergency Operations Center (EOC) of the City Government of Ormoc. It explains how the City's disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) functions are organised and carried out across regular operations, risk assessment, prevention and mitigation, preparedness, early warning, anticipatory action, emergency response, recovery and continuous improvement.

The Manual translates applicable laws, local ordinances, executive and administrative issuances, approved plans, standards and operational guidance into a coherent set of institutional arrangements, procedures, decision pathways, responsibilities and records. It is intended to help personnel understand who is responsible, what action is required, when and under what conditions it is taken, what authority governs the action, what evidence must be recorded, and where an issue must be referred.

The Manual is designed for use by the CDRRMO, the activated EOC, the City offices and service units that contribute to DRRM, barangays, response and recovery partners, and other institutions with defined roles. It is also a reference for orientation, training, exercises, performance review and institutional learning.

Three rules apply throughout the Manual:

- **The Manual does not create legal authority**. It operationalises authority established by law and valid City instruments. Where an additional ordinance, executive order, designation or other instrument is needed, the Manual identifies the requirement for action by the competent authority.

- **A written procedure is not, by itself, proof of institutional capability**. A procedure becomes an established practice when it is appropriately approved, assigned, resourced, used, recorded, reviewed and improved.

- **The Manual and its platform content remain controlled working material until validated and approved through the City's applicable processe**s. Drafting, configuration or platform seeding does not constitute approval.

## 2. Ormoc City and Its Disaster Risk Context

Ormoc City is an independent component city in the Province of Leyte, Eastern Visayas (Region VIII). The City comprises 85 barangays. This is the confirmed current geographic reference for the Manual. Older plans, datasets and historical records may use a previous barangay configuration; where those sources are used, the relevant historical crosswalk must be made explicit so that trends, service coverage and event records are not compared on inconsistent geographic units.

The City's risk context includes multiple interacting natural, biological, technological and climate-related hazards. These include tropical cyclones and associated storm surge; riverine and flash flooding, including areas influenced by the Anilao and Malbasag river systems; rain-induced and earthquake-induced landslides; earthquakes and liquefaction; tsunami; drought and El Niño conditions; epidemics such as dengue; and industrial or geothermal-related incidents. Exposure, vulnerability and service dependencies vary across communities, infrastructure, livelihoods and ecosystems.

Ormoc's disaster experience, including the 1991 flood, Super Typhoon Yolanda in 2013, the magnitude 6.5 Leyte earthquake in 2017 and Typhoon Odette in 2021, reinforces the need for a system that connects risk knowledge to land-use and development decisions, preparedness, warning, coordinated response, recovery and long-term resilience. These events are part of the City's institutional learning context; they do not replace current hazard information or official technical advisories.

The Manual therefore treats risk as a dynamic interaction among hazards, exposure, vulnerability, capacities and dependencies. Hazard information must be drawn from competent and authoritative sources, including PAGASA, PHIVOLCS and MGB, and from City-approved plans and assessments. The Manual does not create independent forecasting products or substitute for official warnings.

## 3. Legal and Policy Basis

The Manual is grounded in the laws, local issuances, guidelines, standards, memorandum circulars, approved plans and other authoritative instruments applicable to the City Government of Ormoc. Its purpose is to harmonise these requirements into a consistent institutional and operational framework so that provisions that are distributed across different instruments can be applied coherently in day-to-day work and during emergencies.

The Manual's principal national legal anchors include Republic Act No. 10121, or the Philippine Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act of 2010; Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012; and Republic Act No. 12287, or the State of Imminent Disaster and Early Action Act, together with its implementing rules and regulations and applicable subsequent issuances. The relevant local ordinances, executive issuances, administrative guidelines and sectoral rules are identified in the appropriate chapters and controlled legal and policy registers.

The CDRRMO leads the technical and institutional harmonisation of the Manual and coordinates the resolution of operational inconsistencies. This leadership does not displace the statutory or delegated powers of the City Mayor, Sangguniang Panlungsod, CDRRMC, other City offices, national government agencies or other competent authorities. The City Legal Office is engaged where a legal dispute, conflicting legal interpretation or matter requiring formal legal advice arises. OCD Region VIII is consulted or informed, as appropriate to the issue and its mandate.

Where provisions appear inconsistent, the applicable hierarchy of law and valid issuances governs. The Manual must not be interpreted as amending, repealing or overriding an enactment or valid City issuance. References to laws and policies are guides to the governing instruments; the official text governs.

## 6. Design Principles

The Manual applies five design principles across all chapters and supporting platform functions:

- Compliant. Each requirement is linked to its governing legal, policy or administrative basis. Mandatory obligations are distinguished from recommended or good-practice measures.

- Context-specific. Procedures reflect Ormoc's hazards, confirmed 85-barangay geography, institutional arrangements, service dependencies and local operating conditions.

- Efficient. Procedures are practical and proportionate. Steps, forms and records are designed to support action without unnecessary duplication or administrative burden.

- Evidence-based. Decisions, assessment findings and operational records identify their sources, dates, assumptions, limitations and validation status.

- Actionable. Findings are translated into assigned responsibilities, SOPs, workflows, forms, implementation priorities, performance measures and follow-up actions.

## 7. Systems Thinking, Quality Management and Coherence

Disaster risk is produced and changed through interactions among hazards, exposure, vulnerability, capacities, institutions, infrastructure, ecosystems and social and economic systems. The Manual therefore treats the CDRRMO, EOC, City offices, barangays and external partners as connected parts of one local DRRM system. A gap in one function may affect other functions through information flows, authority relationships, resource dependencies and timing.

Systems thinking is used to examine not only visible events and immediate operational problems, but also recurring patterns, institutional structures, feedback loops and assumptions that influence performance. It helps identify leverage points where clearer mandates, better information, stronger coordination, adequate resources or improved feedback can produce benefits across multiple functions.

Quality management provides the discipline for maintaining and improving this system. Processes should have clear owners, inputs, outputs, decision rules, records and performance measures. The Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle supports implementation, monitoring, review and corrective action. Lessons from exercises, incidents, assessments and audits should inform controlled changes to the Manual and its supporting tools.

Coherence is maintained across five connected layers:

- Legal and policy requirements: the authoritative instruments that establish obligations and decision rights.

- Institutional architecture: the offices, councils, positions and partners that hold mandates and responsibilities.

- Operational processes: the SOPs, workflows, triggers, decision points and escalation routes that translate mandates into action.

- Information and records: the evidence, forms, registers, logs and reports needed to support decisions and accountability.

- Performance and learning: indicators, review, after-action learning and corrective action that strengthen the system over time.

## 8. Institutional Invariants and Authority Boundaries

The following principles apply throughout the Manual. Chapter-specific procedures may provide additional detail but must not contradict these boundaries:

- The CDRRMC is the City's statutory policy, coordination and oversight body under the applicable legal framework. The CDRRMO is the permanent technical and operational office and serves as the CDRRMC secretariat in accordance with its mandate.

- The EOC is an activated coordination mechanism. It is not a fourth CDRRMO division and is not a substitute for incident command. The Incident Commander and Incident Management Team retain field-level incident command; the EOC supports coordination, information management, resource coordination and executive decision support.

- EOC activation, a State of Calamity under Republic Act No. 10121, and a State of Imminent Disaster under Republic Act No. 12287 are distinct processes. One must not be treated as automatically creating or replacing another.

- City offices, national agencies, utilities, barangays and partners retain their own legal mandates. A RACI matrix clarifies responsibility and coordination; it does not transfer statutory authority or create a power that an office does not otherwise hold.

- The Manual does not independently authorise evacuation orders, no-build zones, declarations, expenditure, procurement or other coercive or financial decisions. These actions must follow the authority, process and safeguards established by applicable law and valid City instruments.

- Hazard thresholds and technical triggers must be sourced from competent agencies and approved City plans. The Manual must not invent rainfall, wind, flood, tsunami, earthquake or landslide thresholds.

- Responsibilities are assigned to offices and positions rather than dependent on the continued presence of named individuals. Designations, alternates and succession arrangements must be maintained through appropriate City processes.

- Records must be proportionate and privacy-conscious. Personal information is collected and shared only for a lawful, specified purpose and with appropriate safeguards. A blank or missing report means 'not reported' or 'not available'; it must never be interpreted as zero.

## 9. Review, Validation and Institutional Decision-Making

The Manual is developed through a structured process of technical review, evidence validation, institutional consultation and formal decision-making. These activities are related but distinct. A technical comment may improve clarity or feasibility; evidence validation confirms whether a statement is supported; a recommendation proposes a course of action; and a formal decision is made only by the office or authority empowered to make it.

The CDRRMO leads and coordinates the Manual's technical and institutional review. It identifies the appropriate reviewers and validators, consolidates comments, maintains the issue and decision records, routes matters to the competent office, and tracks resolution. This coordinating role reflects the CDRRMO's ownership of the Manual's operational coherence; it does not replace the decision rights of the Mayor, Sangguniang Panlungsod, CDRRMC or other competent authorities.

OCD Region VIII is consulted or informed where regional technical guidance, coordination or national-to-local alignment is relevant. The City Legal Office is engaged when a legal dispute, conflicting interpretation or need for formal legal advice arises. Subject-matter offices validate matters within their mandates, including organisational and staffing arrangements, planning and risk information, finance and procurement, public information, data protection, response operations and recovery.

The Review and Validation Tracker is a working management instrument. It records review areas, evidence needs, responsible offices, open issues and decisions. Its proposed assignments support coordination and do not, by themselves, constitute formal designations or approvals. The CDRRMO maintains the authoritative status of each issue and ensures that decisions are reflected in the controlled Manual and relevant MODE records.

### 9.1 Validation and Closure Rules

- Every material comment or issue is logged, assigned to an appropriate office or decision owner, and given a clear disposition.

- Evidence is checked against the relevant requirement and its source. Conflicting evidence is recorded and referred for resolution.

- Proposed procedures, role assignments, triggers, thresholds and forms remain marked as proposals until validated and approved through the applicable process.

- A matter is closed only when its disposition, supporting evidence or decision, responsible office and resulting document or platform change are recorded.

- Where a matter remains unresolved, the Manual states the limitation and identifies the required decision or validation action rather than presenting an assumption as settled fact.

## 11. Behavioural Design and Stakeholder Action: Applying the B = MAP Framework

The Manual and MODE are designed not only to make information available but to help the right people take the right institutional action at the right time. This design uses B. J. Fogg's Behavior Model, expressed as Behaviour = Motivation + Ability + Prompt (B = MAP). The expression is a practical model, not literal arithmetic: the intended behaviour is more likely when motivation, ability and a prompt converge at the same moment.

**Table I.4**

*Applying B = MAP to Manual Review and Institutional Action*

| **Element** | **Meaning in This Project**                                                  | **Design Response**                                                                                              |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Behaviour   | The specific action expected from a stakeholder or office.                   | Define observable actions such as review, validate, recommend, decide, provide evidence or close an issue.       |
| Motivation  | The reason the action matters to the institution and the person responsible. | Connect the task to mandate, public safety, institutional responsibility, relevance and accountability.          |
| Ability     | The practical capacity to complete the action.                               | Provide accessible text, clear evidence, manageable tasks, guidance, tools, role clarity and a workable process. |
| Prompt      | A timely cue that calls for a defined action.                                | Issue a specific assignment, notification, instruction, deadline or follow-up with an expected response.         |

*Note. A prompt alone is insufficient when the responsible person lacks the motivation, authority, information, time or practical ability to act.*

A useful prompt should identify who is expected to act; why the action is required; what exact section, evidence or decision is involved; how the action should be completed and against what criteria; when a response is due; what response is expected; and what happens next.

The framework distinguishes stakeholder behaviours from the CDRRMO's coordination behaviours. Stakeholders review, validate, recommend, provide evidence or decide within their mandates. The CDRRMO assigns and clarifies tasks, coordinates review, receives and consolidates inputs, resolves or routes issues, monitors progress and records closure. MODE should be assessed by whether it helps these actions occur with evidence and proper authority—not simply by logins, uploaded documents or feature usage.

## 12. Document Conventions and Instructions for Use

The Manual uses consistent headings, numbering, tables, figures, SOP identifiers, forms, workflows and annex references so that users can find and apply information reliably. The following conventions apply:

- Headings use a consistent hierarchy. Chapter titles are distinct from numbered sections and subsections. The Introduction is front matter and is not an additional chapter.

- Tables are numbered by chapter or front-matter sequence, have a concise Title Case title above the table, and include a note or source statement below where needed. Column headings use Title Case.

- Figures have a sequential number, a concise caption and a source or explanatory note. Conceptual diagrams are identified as explanatory models and are not presented as measured results.

- SOPs use a consistent identifier and control structure. Each procedure should state its purpose, owner, trigger, authority, inputs, steps, decision rules, records, timing, escalation and related forms.

- Forms, workflows, registers and annexes use unique identifiers and cross-references. The same identifier must not be assigned to different items, and an identifier must not be changed in one location without updating all references.

- Responsibilities are expressed by office or position. Personal names may appear in orientation or contact material where appropriate, but operational accountability must not depend on a named individual.

- Status labels distinguish draft, for review, for validation, validated, approved, effective, superseded and archived content. Only the competent authority can approve or adopt a provision.

Before platform seeding, the Consultant and MODE Systems Architect and Developer are responsible for temporarily reconciling the chapter, SOP, workflow, form and annex identifiers across the Manual and its supporting registers. The reconciliation must be recorded and checked before the identifiers are treated as stable platform references.

Users should begin with the chapter relevant to their function, then follow its linked SOPs, forms, registers and annexes. Where a procedure refers to another chapter, the cross-reference should be followed to understand the related authority, resource, information or reporting requirement. Where a conflict or uncertainty is found, users should refer it to the CDRRMO rather than independently rewriting or bypassing a controlled procedure.

## 13. National Issuances, Regulatory Updates and Change Control

Disaster risk reduction, early action, emergency management, financing, procurement, privacy and local governance requirements may be clarified or amended through subsequent laws, implementing rules, administrative issuances and technical guidance. The Manual must therefore be maintained as a controlled and reviewable instrument rather than treated as a static publication.
