# 1. Purpose of this Manual

This Manual of Operations (MOO) tells the City Government of Ormoc **who does what, when, on what authority, and to what standard** in disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM), before, during and after emergencies. It is the single controlled reference for the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO) and the Emergency Operations Center (EOC).

The manual exists to make the CDRRMO–EOC institution:

- **Compliant** — every procedure traces to a legal or policy authority (RA 10121 and its IRR, RA 12287 and its IRR, NDRRMC issuances, and City ordinances and executive orders), and mandatory (M) indicators are separated from good-practice (GP) indicators so the City always knows what it *must* do and what it *should* do.
- **Context-specific** — written for Ormoc's actual hazards (typhoon, riverine and flash flood along the Anilao–Malbasag corridor, rain-induced landslide, storm surge, earthquake) and its **85 barangays** (PSA PSGC figure adopted by plebiscite on 8 October 2022; the City master list is being reconciled with CPDO, the City Secretary and the DILG City LGOO before the number is treated as final).
- **Efficient** — built for a small office: the full institutional assessment takes about **five field days with a two-to-three person team**, and a short priority set of indicators carries most of the readiness gain (see §4.4).
- **Evidence-based** — nothing is claimed without proof (see §4).
- **Actionable** — every gap found by the assessment converts into a costed, owned, dated action in the manual's action plan.

# 3. Document Control and Citation Policy

- The **approved manual is the legal document**. The MODE platform makes it easy to read, search and use; where the platform and the approved manual differ, the approved manual governs.
- Versions move through **draft → in review → approved**, and only the **CDRRMO Head** approves a version for use. Approved versions are immutable; changes come as new versions.
- Every SOP cites its authority in a control block. Where a rule is read from a **scanned or unofficial copy** of an issuance (for example, NDRRMC Memorandum No. 131, s. 2018), the reading is **provisional** until verified with OCD Region VIII and the City Legal Office, and the manual says so on the page. Parameters the law leaves open are stored as adjustable rules, not hard-coded guesses.
- Citations on the platform render with the note: *the official text governs.*

# 4. How We Assess Readiness: the Institutional Assessment Tool (IAT)

**This section is stated once, here. No chapter repeats it; chapters apply it.**

## 4.1 What the IAT measures

The **Institutional Assessment Tool (IAT) v1.7** measures the capacity of the CDRRMO (Part A), the EOC (Part B), and the CDRRMO–EOC interface (Part C):

- **140 indicators** in **22 domains** — Part A: 82 indicators in 10 domains; Part B: 43 indicators in 7 domains; Part C: 15 interface indicators in 5 domains.
- Each indicator is typed **M (mandatory)** or **GP (good practice)** and weighted 1–3 by criticality. Part C indicators have no M/GP type: they score the quality of the hand-off between the office and the center.
- Every indicator maps to at least one **master requirement** (MR-01 to MR-18) in the Traceability and Data Dictionary, and to the manual sections, SOPs and forms that close its gap.

## 4.2 Scoring rules (evidence-gated)

Each indicator scores **0–4**: 0 = not present; 1 = initial; 2 = developing; 3 = established; 4 = advanced.

- A score of **3 or 4 requires documentary proof** — the plan, EO, ordinance, MOA, log, report or form must exist and be shown.
- **Interviews and workshops alone can never score above 2.** What people say is a lead, not evidence.
- Blank barangay counts stay blank; a count that is not reported is not zero.

## 4.3 From scores to action

Assessment results feed, in one pass:

- the **maturity index** per domain and part;
- the **Institutional Maturity Index (IMI)** for the CDRRMO and EOC;
- the **Interface Index** (Part C) for the CDRRMO–EOC hand-off;
- **compliance rates** for M indicators (what the law requires) and GP indicators (what good practice expects);
- the **gap priority** ranking; and
- the **action plan** — each gap becomes an action with an owner office, a target quarter and a status, reviewed every cycle.

## 4.4 The priority set (Pareto)

A small set of indicators carries most of the readiness gain. The platform's **Today dashboard** tracks this set live:

- **Tier 1:** A1.1, A1.2, A2.2, A2.3, A4.1, A4.2 — legal mandate, council reconstitution, staffing and fund-access basics.
- **Tier 2:** B1.1, B1.2, B1.3, A3.3, A6.1, A6.2, A6.3, B4.1, B4.2, A8.6, A8.8 — EOC establishment, alert levels, contingency planning, warning thresholds and anticipatory action.
- **Tier 3:** A4.6, A10.4, A10.6, A10.8, A7.3, A1.8 — audit, M&E, recovery procedures and the State of Imminent Disaster workflow.

A Tier 1 indicator is never left at 2 if the City can raise it to 3 with one ordinance, one executive order or one funded staffing item.

# 5. The Manual and the MODE Platform

**MODE** — the *Manual of Operations for the DRRMO and EOC* — is the mobile-first, offline-capable platform that hosts this manual and runs the institution around it:

- **Assess** (IAT) — score the 140 indicators with the evidence gates of §4.2; results roll up to maturity indices and the action plan.
- **Library** — this manual: chapters, annexes, SOPs, workflows and forms, with citations and the RefText legal catalogue.
- **Protocols and Workflows** (DRRM) — the point-of-action checklists and multi-office cycles that carry out the SOPs during events and duty shifts.
- **EOC** — activation levels, the EOC board, situational reports and anticipatory action under RA 12287.
- **Tools** — the ICS simulator, the continuity (PSCP) workbench and the Gawad KALASAG self-check; all keep their data in the browser.

The platform follows **privacy by schema**: it never stores the names of evacuees, affected persons or informants — barangay reports are counts, consistent with the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173). Ten platform roles, enforced in the database, mirror the accountabilities in this manual.
