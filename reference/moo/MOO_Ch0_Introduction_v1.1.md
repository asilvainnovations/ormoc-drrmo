# 1. Purpose of the Manual

This Manual of Operations (MOO) is one integrated manual for the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO) and the Emergency Operations Center (EOC) of the City Government of Ormoc. It sets out who decides, who acts, in what order, with which records and under which law, across normal operations, preparedness, early warning, anticipatory action, emergency response, recovery and continuous improvement.

The Manual is produced through a fourteen-week co-creation between the City Government of Ormoc and its consultant, Alvin M. Silva (ASilva Innovations). It is assessment-informed: each chapter answers gaps found by the Institutional Assessment Tool (IAT) and names the indicators it addresses. It is also operational: each standard operating procedure (SOP) is written so that it can be run as a checklist in the MODE platform and tested in an exercise.

Three limits apply to the whole Manual:

-   **It is not a source of legal power.** The Manual implements authority that already exists in law, ordinance and valid executive issuance. Where an authority is missing, the Manual proposes the instrument (for example, an EOC establishment EO) and labels it as a proposal.

-   **Writing a procedure is not evidence of using it.** A procedure counts as institutionalised only when it is approved, used, recorded and reviewed. The IAT scores reflect this rule.

-   **Every chapter remains a controlled working draft** until the owning office validates it and the CDRRMO Head approves it. Seeded content in MODE is never marked approved by the drafting team.

# 2. The City and Its Risk Context

Ormoc is an independent component city in the Province of Leyte, Eastern Visayas (Region VIII). Under the Philippine Standard Geographic Code (PSGC) the City (code 0803738000) has **85 barangays as of 31 July 2025**. The count follows City Ordinance No. 052, s. 2021 and the plebiscite of 8 October 2022, which merged the 28 numbered Poblacion districts into Barangays South (14), East (7) and West (7) and renamed District 29 as North (110 − 28 + 3 = 85). Plans prepared before the merger, including the CLUP 2017–2027 and parts of the CDRA, still use 110 barangays; chapters apply a 110-to-85 crosswalk to those sources. The CPDO, the City Secretary and the DILG City LGOO have still to sign off the City master list, so 85 is the working figure in controlled records.

The City is exposed to tropical cyclones and storm surge, riverine floods and flash floods (notably along the Anilao and Malbasag rivers), rain-induced and earthquake-induced landslides, earthquakes and liquefaction, tsunami, drought and El Niño, epidemics (including dengue), and industrial and geothermal incidents. Short-lead hazards such as flash floods and landslides usually fall below the lead time needed for a State of Imminent Disaster and are handled through city-level early action (Chapter 4). The 1991 flash flood remains the deadliest event on record; Super Typhoon Yolanda (2013), the magnitude 6.5 Leyte earthquake (2017) and Typhoon Odette (2021) shaped the current arrangements (Chapter 5, Table 5.3).

# 5. Assessment-informed Method and Systems Diagnosis

## 5.1 The Institutional Assessment Tool

The Manual rests on the Ormoc CDRRMO–EOC Institutional Assessment Tool (IAT), version 1.7 (workbook v1-7, 11 November 2026). It has 140 indicators in 22 domains: Part A, CDRRMO, 82 indicators in 10 domains; Part B, EOC, 43 indicators in 7 domains; and Part C, interface with other City offices and partners, 15 indicators in 5 domains. Parts A and B have 79 Mandatory (M) and 46 Good Practice (GP) indicators and form the Institutional Maturity Index (IMI). Part C has no Mandatory items, names a lead office other than the CDRRMO, and is reported as a separate Interface Index that is never added to the IMI.

**Table I.6**

*Scoring Rubric, Evidence Rule and Index Bands*

| **Element**                         | **Rule**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Rubric**                          | 0 Absent; 1 Initial (verbal only); 2 Developing (draft or partial); 3 Compliant (approved document and proof of use); 4 Advanced (level 3 plus a test, exercise or after-action review showing improvement); NA with written justification. |
| **Evidence rule (Methodology 5.2)** | A score resting only on focus group discussion or interviews is capped at 2. Until validation, the lower score stands.                                                                                                                      |
| **Index**                           | Sum of score × weight divided by the sum of weights of scored items. Weights: 3 life-safety or legal; 2 effectiveness; 1 supportive.                                                                                                        |
| **Bands**                           | Below 0.5 Absent; 0.5–1.49 Initial; 1.5–2.49 Developing; 2.5–3.49 Compliant; 3.5 and above Advanced. The unrounded value is banded.                                                                                                         |
| **Mandatory compliance**            | Scored Mandatory items at 3 or 4, divided by scored Mandatory items. A domain is "Compliant" only at 100%.                                                                                                                                  |
| **Gap priority**                    | (4 − score) × weight × 1.5 for Mandatory items, × 1.0 for Good Practice and all Part C items. High 9 and above; Medium 4 to 8.9; Low below 4.                                                                                               |

### The Iceberg Model

The iceberg model separates what people see during an event from the patterns, structures and mental models below it (Figure I.3). The visible events are familiar: activation decided case by case, Council resolutions adopted for each event, staff stretched across OpCen, field and EOC duties, and late or uneven situation reports. Below the waterline, the evidence points to four structures.

-   The CDRRMO is an office under the Office of the City Mayor, created by Ordinance No. 008, s. 2014 (amended by Ordinance No. 011, s. 2015) with a Head (SG-23) and three LDRRMO I posts. Most of its workforce (about 47 staff, of whom four are permanent, as reported at the FGD) is on job-order or casual terms (A1.1 and A2.3, each scored 2).

-   No executive order establishes the EOC, its levels, its activation authority or its staffing (B1.1, scored 2).

-   The boundary between incident command (ICS) and the EOC is not written down, so the same people play both roles (B6.1, scored 1).

-   Standing rules exist, such as Ordinance No. 003, s. 2013 on forced evacuation and Ordinance No. 135, s. 2023, yet decisions are still taken through event-by-event resolutions rather than through procedures that cite them.

The mental model underneath is that disaster management is response: capability is shown by how hard the office works during an event, not by the rules, rosters and records it keeps between events.

### Causal Loop: Shifting the Burden

Figure I.4 shows the dominant dynamic. Operational pressure from each event is relieved quickly by ad-hoc fixes: borrowed staff, a new resolution, an improvised EOC set-up (balancing loop B1). Fundamental solutions, namely plantilla positions, an EOC executive order and approved SOPs, also relieve the pressure but only after a delay (balancing loop B2). Each successful ad-hoc fix increases reliance on ad-hoc fixes and reduces the attention and resources given to the fundamental solutions (reinforcing loop R1). Over time the office becomes better at improvising and no better at institutionalising.

### Causal Loop: ICS–EOC Mandate Conflation

Figure I.5 shows a second, reinforcing loop (R2). Without a codified boundary, the EOC is drawn into field command; the more it commands, the less it coordinates information and resources; incidents then need more direct intervention, which pulls the EOC further into command. The loop is broken by writing the boundary down: the Incident Commander keeps incident-level command, and the EOC supports coordination, information, resources and executive decisions (SOP 4-05; indicator B6.1).

# 7. Systems Thinking, Quality Management and Coherence

The Manual treats disaster risk as the result of interconnected hazards, exposure, vulnerability, capacities and dependencies. Its offices, councils and procedures are therefore written as one system with feedback loops rather than as separate units. Total Quality Management supplies the operating discipline: every process has an owner; decisions rest on evidence; procedures are standardised and measured; and corrective action closes the Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle. Each chapter states only the systems and quality principles that are specific to its subject; the general approach is set here once.

| **Versioned** | Approved versions are never edited; corrections are made through a superseding version or assessment cycle.                                                                 |

# 8. Institutional Invariants

The following rules hold in every chapter. A chapter may add detail but may not contradict them.

1.  The CDRRMC is the statutory policy, coordination and oversight body; the CDRRMO is the permanent technical and operational office and the CDRRMC secretariat.

2.  The EOC is an activated coordination mechanism. It is not a fourth CDRRMO division and not a field command. The Incident Commander and the incident management team keep incident-level command; the EOC supports coordination, information, resources and executive decisions.

3.  EOC activation does not declare a State of Calamity (RA 10121, by the Sanggunian on CDRRMC recommendation) or a State of Imminent Disaster (RA 12287, by the Mayor's EO on the prescribed recommendation). The three paths stay distinct.

4.  Sector offices, agencies, utilities and barangays keep their own mandates. A RACI allocates work; it does not transfer legal authority.

5.  The Manual creates no independent power to order evacuation, declare no-build zones, spend funds or procure. It follows the authority in law and valid City issuances.

6.  Rainfall, wind, flood, tsunami and landslide thresholds are never invented. They come from the official products of PAGASA, PHIVOLCS and MGB and from City-approved contingency plans, with their uncertainty recorded.

7.  Duties are assigned to positions, never to persons.

8.  No general-purpose record holds the names of evacuees, patients or informants. Counts are aggregates by barangay, a blank means "not reported", never zero, and counts are scoped to the current event.

# 10. The Manual and the MODE Platform

MODE, the Manual of Operations for the DRRMO and EOC Development Platform, supports the Manual but is not the Manual. The approved Manual remains the legal document; MODE holds its current text, version history and the evidence that it is used. MODE does four jobs: it **assesses** the office and the EOC with the IAT; it **writes and traces** the Manual, linking every section to its indicators and master requirements; it **runs** SOPs as protocols and workflows with owners, timings and an audit trail; and it **operates** the EOC and the RA 12287 anticipatory-action process.

**Table I.14**

*MODE Design Rules That the Manual Relies On*

| **Rule**                          | **What it means for users of the Manual**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **City ownership**                | The City owns the code and the data. The production database is a City Supabase project in Tokyo (ap-northeast-1); the privacy policy names the transfer.                                                                                                                     |
| **Local-first**                   | Field staff can capture logbook entries, forms and barangay figures without a connection; they sync when the device is back online.                                                                                                                                           |
| **Authority actions online only** | Changing the EOC level, approvals, declaration steps, disbursements and role grants need a connection and are never queued.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Ten roles**                     | CDRRMO Head, EOC Manager, EOC staff, duty officer, assessor, validator, lead office focal person, author, viewer and admin, enforced in the database. Only the CDRRMO Head approves, retires or reopens Manual versions and protocols; a draft protocol runs only as a drill. |
| **Immutability**                  | Approved versions, validated responses, approved SitReps, decided resource requests, declarations and disbursements are locked. The logbook is append-only; corrections are new entries. Vault documents are superseded, never deleted.                                       |

# 11. Document Conventions

## 11.1 Standard Chapter Template

Every chapter follows the same order so that readers find the same things in the same place: cover; document control; revision and approval record; introduction and assessment findings for the chapter; purpose and intended results; scope, application and institutional boundaries; legal, policy and operational framework (chapter-specific instruments only; the general framework is here and in Chapter 1); chapter-specific systems and quality principles; the operational sections; standard operating procedures with control blocks and step tables; RACI; performance monitoring and corrective action; implementation priorities; forms, registers and annexes; relationship with other chapters; conclusion; references; validation and source control register; annexes.

## 11.2 Numbering, Captions and Notes

**Table I.15**

*Numbering and Presentation Conventions*

## 11.3 Typography and Colour

The chapters use the three type families of the MODE design system, with Roboto added for table text: chapter titles in Montserrat Bold (the standard, "classic" cut; there is no separate font named Montserrat Classic); subtitles and section titles in Poppins Bold; subsection titles in Roboto Bold; body text in Roboto Condensed with single line spacing; table text in Roboto 10 pt. Colours follow the design-system tokens: navy (#0F3D66) for titles and figure outlines, light blue (#EEF5FB) and gray-blue (#E3EBF2) for fills, and orange (#D66812) only to mark what is active or to draw attention, never to show a status. The White, Blue and Red level colours are used only where an EOC level is meant.
