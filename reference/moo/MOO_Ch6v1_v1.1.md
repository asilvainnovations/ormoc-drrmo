# 1. Introduction and Assessment-informed Rationale

Chapter 4 turns warnings into protective action, and Chapter 5 turns the damage that remains into recovery. Both depend on money, supplies and a lawful way to buy them. A warning that cannot be matched by funds, stock and a ready supplier does not become action; a recovery program without a fund source stays on paper. This chapter is where the City keeps those means ready and accounts for them.

RA 10121 gives the City a dedicated Local DRRM Fund (LDRRMF). RA 12287 now allows part of it to be spent before a hazard strikes. Both depend on plans approved in advance, procedures that the Budget, Accounting, Treasury, BAC and General Services offices recognise, stocks that are counted and rotated, and records that stand up to audit. The chapter applies the systems-thinking and Total Quality Management approach set out in the Introduction (section 7): one chain from plan to proof, with feedback from utilisation, stock counts and audit into the next plan.

The IAT v1.7 baseline shows a sound base and a few critical gaps (Figure 6.1). Domain A4 (DRRM Financing and Fund Management) has an index of **2.16 (Developing)**, with 3 of 6 Mandatory items compliant. The 5% LDRRMF, the 70/30 split and the Special Trust Fund are in place and approved (A4.1 to A4.3 at 3). Gaps sit where speed and evidence matter most: the anticipatory action funding pathway (A4.6, score 0, High), pre-arranged procurement (A4.5) and supplier commitments (B6.6), fund adequacy (A4.9), and an inventory that reaches beyond City stocks (B6.3) (Table 6.3).

## 1.2 Design Considerations

1.  **Two clocks.** Normal budgeting runs on an annual calendar; emergency spending and issue of stock run in hours. Both must lead to the same records.

2.  **Three funding windows.** Before impact (anticipatory action funds under RA 12287), during and after impact (the QRF under a State of Calamity), and the rest of the year (the 70% component and the Special Trust Fund). Each window has its own legal trigger, and this chapter keeps them apart (Figure 6.3).

3.  **Pre-arrangement is the speed.** Emergency purchases and stock issues are fastest when the stock level, supplier, framework agreement, specification and price reference exist before the event.

4.  **One item, one record.** A food pack, a generator or a rescue boat has one record from purchase to disposal, shared by the property books, the warehouse stock card and the EOC resource list in MODE.

5.  **Evidence by design.** Every SOP ends in a form that an auditor can follow from appropriation to delivery and use.

6.  **No invented thresholds.** Where national guidelines are still pending (NDRRMC operational guidelines on AA triggers; DBM–COA guidelines on AA funds), the chapter states the gap and holds the parameter in a rule that can change without rewriting the procedure.

# 2. Purpose and Intended Results

This chapter sets the City's procedures for planning, releasing, spending, recording and reporting DRRM funds; for buying what DRRM needs in normal times and in emergencies; for keeping, rotating and issuing prepositioned stocks; for knowing what resources the City, its barangays and its partners can mobilise; and for accounting for money, goods, property and donations entrusted to DRRM.

Intended results:

1.  The LDRRMF is computed on the correct base, split 70/30, and programmed through an LDRRMF Investment Plan (LDRRMFIP) consistent with the LDRRMP, the CDP and the AIP.

2.  An anticipatory action (AA) line of up to 10% of the 70% component is programmed, with Budget, Accounting, Treasury and BAC steps that allow immediate release under a State of Imminent Disaster and observe the tranche rule.

3.  The QRF is used only within its legal trigger and purpose, and its use is reported.

4.  Unexpended balances are tracked by year of origin and used within five years.

5.  Emergency procurement is pre-arranged, and every emergency purchase can show its legal ground.

6.  Prepositioned relief stocks meet levels set from the contingency plan scenarios, are rotated before expiry and are replenished at defined reorder points.

7.  The EOC can see City, barangay, partner and supplier resources in one inventory, with the commitments behind them.

8.  Donations and external assistance are received, recorded, kept, used and disclosed as the donor and the law require.

9.  Utilisation is reported quarterly to the CDRRMC and posted under the Full Disclosure Policy; COA observations on DRRM funds and property are answered and closed.

10. The City knows how far its funds and stocks cover a severe credible event, and what it would draw on beyond them.

# 5. Systems Thinking and TQM Principles for DRRM Finance and Resources

The Introduction (section 5.4) found that ad-hoc fixes during events (borrowed supplies, event-by-event resolutions) relieve pressure faster than fundamental solutions and so keep the office improvising. In finance and logistics the fundamental solutions are known: a plan line before the event, a supplier agreement before the season, a stock level before the forecast. This chapter puts them in place and keeps them visible.

-   **One chain from plan to proof** (Figure 6.2). LDRRMP target → LDRRMFIP line → AIP and budget → PPMP and APP → obligation → procurement → delivery → use → report → audit. A break anywhere shows up as unspent money or unsupported spending.

-   **Speed through preparation.** The fastest emergency purchase is the one whose supplier, specification and price were agreed before the event; the fastest relief is the stock already in a safe warehouse.

-   **Separation of duties.** The office that requests is not the office that certifies, pays, awards or holds the property. MODE records each step by position.

-   **Feedback loops.** Quarterly utilisation, the STF ageing ledger, stock counts and COA observations feed the next LDRRMFIP and the next stock plan.

-   **Prevention over correction.** The pre-audit checklist (Annex 6-B) prevents the most common COA findings: non-DRRM charges, QRF use without a declaration, missing utilisation reports, unliquidated advances, expired stocks and incomplete supporting documents.

# 6. LDRRMF Planning and Allocation

## 6.1 Computing the Fund

The LDRRMF is not less than 5% of **estimated revenue from regular sources** (RA 10121 Sec. 21). For a city this means the National Tax Allotment share plus regular local revenue (local taxes, fees and charges, and other recurring income), as defined by the Budget Office under DBM rules. It excludes one-time receipts, grants and borrowings.

The CY 2026 computation used the National Tax Allotment alone (Res. No. 05, s. 2025). The City Budget Office confirms the base each year on form FIN-F01 and keeps the computation with the budget. If the base was understated, the difference is appropriated through a supplemental budget or corrected in the next budget, as the Budget Office and the SP decide.

The CY 2026 plan has no AA line, so these figures apply only once a plan includes one.

## 6.3 The LDRRMF Investment Plan

The LDRRMFIP is the annual plan that turns the LDRRMP into funded lines. Each line carries the LDRRMP and CDP PPA code; the thematic area (prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, rehabilitation and recovery); the fund source (70%, QRF, STF by year of origin, other); the amount; the implementing office; the output and target; the procurement mode; and the quarter of implementation. Four lines are always shown on their own: the **AA line** (A4.6), the **EOC operations and maintenance line** (B7.5), **stock replenishment** for prepositioned relief items (A7.5), and **insurance premiums** for DRRM assets (A4.7).

# 8. Special Trust Fund Management

Unexpended LDRRMF accrues to the STF and may be used only for DRRM within five years from the year it accrued; after that it reverts to the general fund (RA 10121 Sec. 21). The STF ledger (FIN-F04) keeps each balance by **year of origin** and shows its deadline, so the oldest money is programmed first. STF balances are also a lawful AA source under RA 12287 IRR Rule V Sec. 1(b), and they are not subject to the 30%/70% graduation.

Programming STF balances follows the same approvals as the 2024 and 2025 Trust Fund plans: CDRRMC approval, then SP adoption or concurrence on the City Accountant's advice (Res. No. 08, s. 2025). A bill to shorten the five-year period has been filed in Congress but is not law and is not applied.

# 9. Quick Response Fund

The QRF is a standby fund for relief and recovery in areas struck by a disaster. Under JMC 2013-1 it is used when the City is under a **State of Calamity** declared by the SP (Chapter 1, SOP GOV-04). COA has found LGUs using the QRF without that declaration; this chapter does not allow it. A State of Imminent Disaster under RA 12287 does not open the QRF: anticipatory action is funded from the AA line and other lawful sources (A4.6).

Unused QRF at year-end accrues to the STF. The QRF balance is reported in every quarterly report and after each use (FIN-F05). When QRF purchases replenish relief stocks, as in Res. No. 06, s. 2025, the items enter the stock card under SOP 6-11 like any other receipt.

## 10.2 Release Rules

1.  Funds are released only while a State of Imminent Disaster declared by the City Mayor is in force (Chapter 1, SOP GOV-05), and only for AA measures in the approved PDRA. MODE's AA checklist is derived from the ten sections of IRR Rule IV; it is a working aid, not a list fixed by law.

2.  Disbursement is based on the anticipated extent of damage and disruption in the PDRA (Rule V Sec. 2).

3.  **Tranche rule** (hydrometeorological and geological hazards): disbursements for interventions to be carried out more than 3 days before forecast impact may not exceed 30% of the AA allocation; up to 70% may be disbursed for interventions within 3 days of impact (Figure 6.4).

4.  **Slow-onset hazards** (for example, drought): timelines and rates await the NDRRMC operational guidelines. Until then they are not graduated, but disbursements never exceed the year's AA allocation (ADR-025 item 4).

5.  **No Regrets Principle.** Goods bought for AA continue to reach the intended beneficiaries even if the hazard does not materialise (Rule V Sec. 3). Their distribution is recorded as for any other relief.

6.  Procurement, budget, accounting and audit rules still apply (Rule V Sec. 2). Where the procurement ground is "time is of the essence", the BAC records it under RA 12009 Sec. 35(b) (SOP 6-07).

7.  Unused AA funds accrue to the STF (Rule V Sec. 3).

8.  Third parties engaged for AA are paid on a *quantum meruit* basis from the AA allocation (Rule V Sec. 3).

# 11. Procurement for DRRM

## 11.1 Normal Procurement

DRRM goods, works and services follow the PPMP, the APP and competitive bidding or the appropriate alternative mode under RA 12009 and its IRR. The CDRRMO prepares the PPMP from the LDRRMFIP and writes the technical specifications; the BAC runs the procurement.

## 11.2 Pre-arranged Supply

Before each typhoon season and at least once a year, the CDRRMO and the CGSO, with the BAC Secretariat, keep the following ready (SOP 6-06):

-   a list of suppliers for relief goods, fuel, water, heavy equipment rental, transport, medicines and medical supplies, and generator service, with capacity and a contact position (FIN-F08);

-   framework agreements under RA 12009 Sec. 16 and GPPB Circular No. 07-2026 where the BAC finds them suitable, stating item, maximum quantity, unit price or price basis, delivery time, call-off trigger and validity;

-   standard specifications and price references for recurrent emergency items;

-   agreements usable for anticipatory action, as RA 12287 IRR Rule IV contemplates (B6.6).

## 11.3 Emergency Procurement

Negotiated procurement – emergency cases (RA 12009 Sec. 35(b)) is used only when its ground exists: imminent danger to life or property during a State of Calamity, or time is of the essence because of a calamity and immediate action is needed to prevent loss of life or property or to restore vital public services, infrastructure or utilities. The requesting office writes the ground on FIN-F07 before the purchase, and the BAC and the HoPE confirm it under the RA 12009 IRR. Items are limited to what the emergency requires. Documentary requirements follow GPPB Circular No. 06-2026; posting and reporting follow the IRR and GPPB rules.

# 12. Donations and External Assistance

Donations in cash or in kind and external financial assistance are accepted, recorded and used as follows (SOP 6-08):

1.  One intake point per event, staffed by the CSWDO and the CGSO with the EOC Logistics or Finance and Administration desk.

2.  Acceptance follows SP Res. No. 2024-102 and COA rules; a deed of donation or acknowledgement receipt is issued (FIN-F09).

3.  Cash goes to the City Treasurer and is recorded by the City Accountant under COA Circular No. 2012-002 as a receipt from other sources, kept to its stated purpose.

4.  Goods are inspected, valued, recorded and stored by the CGSO (or the CSWDO for relief items), enter the stock card (SOP 6-11), and are issued against distribution records.

5.  Conditions set by the donor are respected; unsuitable goods are declined politely at intake.

6.  Donations used for AA are not subject to the 30%/70% graduation (IRR Rule V Sec. 2).

7.  Receipts, use and balances are disclosed with the quarterly report.

No personal data of beneficiaries is copied into the donations log; distribution lists stay with the CSWDO (RA 10173).

# 13. Resource Management and Prepositioned Stocks

Chapter 4 coordinates resources during an event: it receives requests, sets priorities and tracks each request from "requested" to "demobilized" (SOP 4-07). It can do so only if the resources exist, are known and can be released lawfully. This section sets how the City keeps relief stocks, warehouses and the resource inventory ready between events (Figure 6.5).

## 13.1 Stock Levels From Scenarios

Stock levels are set from the contingency plan scenarios (Chapter 3, SOP 3-06), not from habit. For each priority hazard the CSWDO and the CDRRMO take the number of families expected to be displaced in the most probable scenario, the number of days before national or provincial augmentation is expected, and the ration per family, and compute the level for each item (Annex 6-D). The NDRRMC Disaster Preparedness Minimum Standards set a floor of 500 food packs and 500 hygiene kits per LGU, each good for a family of five for three days; for a city of 85 barangays the scenario figure is likely to be higher, and the higher figure applies.

**Table 6.11**

*Parameters That Set Prepositioned Stock Levels*

| **Parameter**                | **Meaning**                                                                                                  | **Source**                                            |
|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Families to be served        | Families expected in evacuation centres or home-based in the most probable scenario, by cluster of barangays | Contingency plans (CP Forms); CDRA exposure data      |
| Days of self-reliance        | Days the City must cover before augmentation arrives                                                         | Contingency plans; DSWD Field Office VIII agreements  |

## 13.2 Warehousing, Rotation and Replenishment

-   **Location.** Warehouses stand in low-risk areas (DPMS Vol. 2), checked against the hazard maps in Chapter 3, and are spread so that one flooded road does not cut off all stocks. The 10-Year Plan's seven planned storage facilities are sited using the same check.

-   **Condition.** Dry, secure, ventilated and pest-controlled; stock on pallets; a fire extinguisher and a posted layout; access controlled by the custodian.

-   **Rotation.** First expiry, first out. Items within the rotation window are issued to regular CSWDO programmes, feeding programmes or drills before they expire, so nothing is wasted.

-   **Replenishment.** When the stock of an item reaches its reorder point, the custodian raises a replenishment request against the stock replenishment line, the AA line (on an imminent threat) or the QRF (under a State of Calamity), whichever window is open.

-   **Count.** A monthly spot count by the custodian and an annual physical count with the CGSO and the COA schedule; variances are explained in writing.

## 13.3 The DRRM Resource Inventory

The EOC needs one view of what can be mobilised, whoever owns it (B6.3). The DRRM resource inventory therefore covers four holders: City offices; barangays (BDRRMC equipment and stocks); partners (national agencies, neighbouring LGUs under mutual aid, CSOs); and suppliers and private-sector providers under signed commitments (B6.6). City-owned items are copied from the property records; others are recorded with the agreement that commits them, the contact position, the activation trigger and the date last confirmed. An entry that has not been confirmed in the last twelve months is shown as unconfirmed.

In MODE, the resources table holds each item with category, unit, quantity, owner office, location and serviceability, and resource requests in Chapter 4 link to it. The current schema has no fields for holder type, commitment reference, reorder point or expiry date. Until they are added, these are kept on the LOG forms (section 20) and noted in the resource record; the gap is listed in section 19 for the platform custodian.

## 13.4 Equipment Readiness

Response equipment and vehicles are inventoried, serviceable and on a preventive maintenance schedule (A8.3). The CDRRMO keeps the schedule and the maintenance log for DRRM equipment; serviceability is updated in the inventory after each check, so the EOC does not count equipment that cannot be used. Equipment out of service for more than one operational period is reported to the CDRRMO Head with the repair plan.

# 14. Property and Asset Accountability

DRRM property bought with the LDRRMF, STF, QRF, AA funds or donations is government property. The CGSO records it, issues it to the end user on a Property Acknowledgement Receipt or Inventory Custodian Slip, and includes it in the annual physical count. The CDRRMO:

-   keeps a DRRM asset register linked to the MODE resource inventory, so the same item has one record;

-   returns or transfers property on reassignment or separation of the accountable person;

-   reports loss or damage at once, with the incident record, for relief from accountability under COA rules;

-   applies the same rules to equipment lent to barangays, with a signed receipt and a return date.

Consumables issued in an emergency (fuel, food packs, non-food items) are supported by issue slips, trip tickets and distribution summaries. The CDRRMO file holds aggregate distribution counts only; named lists stay with the CSWDO.

# 15. Risk Financing and Insurance

The City layers its risk finance (Figure 6.6): the QRF and the 70% component for frequent, smaller events; the AA line, the STF and pre-arranged supply for imminent events; insurance for damage to public assets; and national funds, contingent credit and partners for catastrophic events. SOP 6-09 tests that layering each year against a severe credible scenario (a Thelma-type flash flood; a Yolanda-strength typhoon) and records the shortfall and the fallback sources.

# 22. Concluding Notes

The City already sets aside and approves its DRRM fund, and it has bought relief goods quickly when a State of Calamity allowed. The work now is to make funds and supplies reachable at the right moment and to prove what they did: an anticipatory action line before impact, a QRF under its proper trigger after impact, suppliers agreed and stocks set before the season, an inventory the EOC can trust, and records that tell the CDRRMC, the public and COA what each peso and each item did.

Writing these procedures does not implement them. Until the AA line exists, the first stock plan is approved, the inventory reaches the barangays and suppliers, and at least one exercise has tested SOPs 6-04, 6-05 and 6-11 with the Budget, Accounting, Treasury, BAC, CGSO and CSWDO offices, this chapter remains a controlled working draft.
