# 1. Introduction and Assessment-informed Rationale

Chapters 1 to 6 set what the City does before, during and after a disaster and how it pays for it. This chapter asks whether those things are being done, whether they work, and what changes as a result. It is the "Check" and "Act" of the Plan–Do–Check–Act cycle that Chapter 1 (section 9) adopts for the whole Manual.

A procedure that is written but never measured drifts. The Introduction (section 5.4) names the risk: with Mandatory compliance at 32.9%, the working standard can settle at "we responded" rather than "we met the standard". The remedy is a fixed standard, measured the same way every year, with gaps tracked to closure and lessons fed back into plans and the Manual. This chapter supplies those mechanisms.

The IAT v1.7 baseline shows that the City plans and reports, but rarely closes the loop (Figure 7.1). Domain A10 (Partnerships, M&E and Accountability) has an index of **1.86 (Developing)**, with 1 of 4 Mandatory items compliant. Regular reporting to OCD Region VIII and the DILG is in place (A10.3 at 3). The gaps are in the learning loop: no 45-day post-declaration report template (A10.8, score 0, High); no annual M&E report of the LDRRMP (A10.4); after-action reviews that rest on the FGD alone (A10.6, B7.4); no disaster event and loss database (A5.4, score 0); and data-protection arrangements for DRRM data not sighted (A5.5) (Table 7.3).

## 1.1 What the Records Show

-   **A results-based M&E design exists.** The DRRM 10-Year Plan 2023–2032 (Chapter 5 of the plan) bases its monitoring on the UNDRR Ten Essentials for Making Cities Resilient, overlaid with NDRRMP 2020–2030 outcomes, the DILG capacity development pillars, the Sendai Framework targets and the Regional Development Plan of Region VIII (Figure 7.3). It sets a monitoring tool (Form 5D) and five outputs, including an annual progress report each January, a physical and financial report each December and a mid-term report in 2029, reported to the CDRRMC and the Local Development Council.

-   **Mandates to monitor exist.** RA 10121 Sec. 11(b) tasks the CDRRMC to approve, monitor and evaluate the LDRRMP and to review and test it regularly. EO 2024-032A Sec. 2(a) repeats the task. EO 2022-00020 created the CDRRMP Technical Working Group for formulating, implementing, monitoring and evaluating the plan, with thematic focal offices: CENRO for prevention and mitigation, the DILG City Office for preparedness, the CSWDO for response and the CPDO for recovery. EO 2023-0026 tasks the Project TRANSFORM TWG to monitor indicators in the NRC resilience scorecard.

-   **The loop is not yet closed.** No annual results report and no baseline database were found in the document set. The plan itself observes that the City's performance has been assessed mainly through national award systems, without outcome-based M&E linked to sector results.

-   **Event history is scattered.** The 10-Year Plan lists the six most significant disasters; the CDRA lists previous disasters with damage figures; RDANA and PDNA reports exist for 2017 to 2021. The CDRA recommends a disaster event and loss database. Chapter 5 found one damage figure in the plan that is not plausible (Yolanda), which a controlled database would have caught.

-   **The assessment itself is the first full baseline.** The October–November 2026 IAT cycle scored 140 indicators, applied the evidence rule and produced the first Institutional Maturity Index (IMI 1.98). The validation workshop of 1–4 December 2026 will confirm or revise each score. That cycle is the model for the annual cycles this chapter sets.

## 1.2 Design Considerations

1.  **Measure the same way every year.** The IAT, the LDRRMP indicators and the reporting calendar stay stable so that change can be seen; when the instrument changes, the change is versioned and the old results stay readable.

2.  **Evidence before score.** A score of 3 needs an approved document and proof of use; interviews and FGDs alone cap at 2. The same rule applies to M&E claims: an output is "done" only with its means of verification.

3.  **One register.** Findings from the IAT, AARs, exercises, audits, compliance reports and feedback go into one corrective and preventive action (CAPA) register, defined in Chapter 1 (section 9.5, GOV-10) and run under this chapter.

4.  **Closure means effective.** An action is closed when the problem it addressed has not recurred in the next test, exercise or event, not when work has started.

5.  **Separate the scorer from the validator.** Whoever scores an indicator does not validate it; MODE enforces this in the database (ADR-029).

6.  **Privacy by design.** M&E and knowledge products use aggregate counts; personal data stays with the office that collected it, under the Data Privacy Act.

# 2. Purpose and Intended Results

This chapter sets how the City monitors and evaluates its DRRM plan and its DRRM institutions, how it learns from events and exercises, how it tracks corrective action to closure, how it meets its reporting duties, and how it keeps and shares DRRM knowledge and data safely.

Intended results:

1.  The LDRRMP has an approved indicator set with baselines, and the CDRRMC receives an annual results report each January (A10.4).

2.  The IAT is applied every year with the same instrument, evidence rules and separation of duties, and its results feed the action plan and the Manual (MR-12 to MR-15).

3.  Every EOC activation at Blue or Red, every State of Calamity or State of Imminent Disaster, and every exercise is followed by an after-action review whose findings are tracked (A10.6, B7.4).

4.  Every corrective action in the CAPA register has an owner, a due date and verification evidence, and is closed only when shown to be effective.

5.  The 45-day post-declaration report is submitted on time after each State of Imminent Disaster (A10.8), and all other mandated reports follow a calendar.

6.  A disaster event and loss database holds the City's history from 1991 onwards in a standard format (A5.4).

7.  Lessons from Ormoc's own events and from other LGUs and networks are recorded with the changes they produced (A10.9).

8.  DRRM data handling meets the Data Privacy Act: a designated DPO, privacy notices, data-sharing agreements and a breach procedure (A5.5).

# 5. Systems Thinking and the Learning Cycle

The CDRRMO learns in two ways. In single-loop learning it corrects how a procedure was carried out: a SitRep was late, so the cut-off reminder is moved earlier. In double-loop learning it questions the procedure or its assumptions: SitReps are late because the same staff run the EOC and the field, so the roster changes (Argyris and Schön, 1978). The IAT and the AAR find problems of both kinds; the CAPA register records which kind each action is, so that repeated single-loop fixes to the same problem trigger a deeper review.

Figure 7.2 shows the learning system. Evidence arrives from five sources: the annual IAT cycle, LDRRMP monitoring, after-action reviews and exercises, audits and compliance reports, and feedback and peer learning. One register turns it into change in plans, the Manual, MODE and people's capacity. The next cycle measures again, which is how effectiveness is verified.

-   **Against drifting goals.** Standards are set by the IAT rubric and the law, not by last year's performance. The register shows Mandatory gaps first.

-   **Against shifting the burden.** An AAR finding that recurs in two events is escalated from a single-loop fix to a CDRRMC decision on the fundamental solution (staffing, issuance, budget).

-   **Delays are named.** Each action states when its effect can be seen (the next drill, the next season, the next IAT cycle), so the CDRRMC does not abandon a correct fix before it has had time to work.

# 7. The Institutional Assessment Cycle

The IAT measures the CDRRMO and the EOC themselves. The Introduction (sections 5.1 to 5.3) describes the instrument and the baseline. This section sets how it is used every year.

## 7.3 Validation and Separation of Duties

Every score is validated before the results are final. MODE records who set each score; a validation by the same person is refused. A validator either confirms the score or revises it; a revision needs a written note, and the submitted score is kept. Baseline rows loaded from the workbook have no recorded scorer, so the lead assessor does not validate them (ADR-029; Methodology v1.1, section 6.1). The agreement rate between scorers and validators is reported as a calibration signal, not as a performance measure of individuals.

The proposed timing places fieldwork and validation in the third and fourth quarters, so the results reach the January annual report and the budget call that follows.

## 7.5 From Gap to Action to Manual

Each gap routes to an improvement action with a responsible office, target date and budget source, and to the Manual section that addresses it (MR-14). MODE holds actions per indicator and cycle with a status (not started, ongoing, done, dropped) and shows the chain from indicator to master requirement to Manual document in the traceability view. Actions arising from the IAT are copied into the CAPA register so that one register holds all corrective work.

> PLATFORM NOTE. The MODE actions table has no fields for root cause, verification evidence or effectiveness review, which Chapter 1 (Table 1.23) requires of a CAPA entry. Until they are added, the CAPA register is kept on ME-F05 and the MODE action carries the ME-F05 reference. The gap is listed in section 17.

# 11. Knowledge Management

## 11.1 Disaster Event and Loss Database

The City needs one record of every disaster that affected it: what happened, where, to whom and at what cost (A5.4). The database uses the Sendai Framework and DesInventar fields (Annex 7-E), records barangay as the smallest unit using PSGC codes, and holds aggregate counts only. It starts with the events already documented (the 1991 flash flood, Yolanda in 2013, the 2017 earthquake, Urduja in 2017, Ursula in 2019 and Odette in 2021) and is updated after every event from the RDANA, DROMIC, PDNA and final SitRep. Each figure carries its source; implausible figures, such as the Yolanda damage printed in the 10-Year Plan, are flagged until verified (SOP 7-07).

## 11.2 Records and the Vault

DRRM records follow Chapter 1 (GOV-08). Issuances, plans, reports and evidence are kept in the MODE Vault; documents are superseded, never deleted, and each has an owner office and a date. Retention and disposal follow the schedules approved under RA 9470; nothing is disposed of by the CDRRMO on its own. Evidence files attached to IAT scores identify sources by office or position, never by an informant's name (MR-15).

## 11.3 Lessons Register and Manual Revision

A lesson is recorded only when it changed something or when a change has been proposed. The lessons register (ME-F08) links each lesson to its source (AAR, exercise, IAT, peer exchange), the change made (plan, Manual section, form, training, MODE rule) and the CAPA entry. Changes to the Manual are made as new versions in the MODE Library; approved versions are never edited, and only the CDRRMO Head approves a version.

## 11.4 Peer Learning

Ormoc takes part in several networks: the Making Cities Resilient 2030 initiative, the National Resilience Council Resilient LGU program (SP Res. No. 2021-036), the EDC Adopt-a-City partnership and DENR–EDC Project TRANSFORM (EO 2023-0026), and OCD forums. Each exchange is recorded with what was learned and what the City changed as a result (A10.9). Partnerships themselves are managed under Chapter 8.

# 12. Protection of DRRM Data

# 20. Concluding Notes

The City has the pieces of a learning system: a results-based M&E design in its 10-Year Plan, a Council with the duty to monitor, a working group with thematic focal offices, regular reports to OCD and the DILG, and now a full institutional baseline. What it lacks is the loop that ties them together: one register, one calendar, one database, and the habit of closing an action only when it has worked.

Writing these procedures does not implement them. Until the first annual results report has been presented, the first validated IAT cycle has fed the CAPA register, one event or exercise has gone through an AAR to verified closure, and the 45-day report template has been agreed with OCD VIII, this chapter remains a controlled working draft.
