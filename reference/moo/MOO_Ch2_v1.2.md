# 1. Introduction and Rationale

The effectiveness of Ormoc City's disaster risk reduction and management system depends on a permanent, competent and adequately supported institutional workforce. Laws, plans, facilities, information systems and response assets produce results only when responsibilities are assigned to qualified personnel, authority is understood, work is coordinated and continuity is maintained during periods of heightened risk and emergency operations.

Chapter 1 established the legal and governance relationships among the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (CDRRMC), the City Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (CDRRMO), the Emergency Operations Center (EOC), and the Incident Command System (ICS). This chapter translates that governance framework into an organizational and human-resource operating model. It defines the CDRRMO's functional structure, division-level responsibilities, staffing principles, position accountability, workforce development, personnel welfare and arrangements for supporting an activated EOC.

The chapter treats organizational capacity as a system property. It is not measured solely by the number of positions or the existence of an organizational chart. It depends on the alignment of mandates, staffing, competencies, supervision, work processes, information flows, equipment, funding and inter-office relationships. A position is operationally meaningful only when its mandate, authority, expected outputs, reporting relationship, required competencies and performance measures are clear.

![Figure 1: 1. Introduction and Rationale](/library/ch2v12/fig-01.webp)

The October 2026 Institutional Assessment identifies the CDRRMO's organizational structure and human-resource arrangements as a priority improvement area. The A2 domain tests alignment with Joint Memorandum Circular (JMC) No. 2014-1, the permanent status and qualifications of the CDRRMO Head, minimum staffing, position descriptions, 24/7 operational capability, competency-based training and personnel welfare. The EOC B4 domain separately tests the EOC's incident-time organizational structure, pre-designated personnel and alternates, duty rosters, liaison desks, job action sheets and welfare arrangements. These are related but distinct workforce systems.

The assessment is diagnostic. It does not itself establish legal non-compliance, approve a staffing pattern or create positions. All staffing and organizational proposals remain subject to verification of the current legal framework, approved City organizational structure, plantilla, qualification standards, appropriations and applicable Civil Service Commission (CSC) and Department of Budget and Management (DBM) rules.

# 2. Purpose and Objectives

This chapter establishes a clear, legally grounded and implementable organizational and human-resource framework for the CDRRMO and its support to the EOC.

Specifically, it aims to:

1.  Translate the mandates established in Chapter 1 into a functional organizational structure aligned with RA No. 10121 and JMC No. 2014-1.

2.  Define the responsibilities, authority, outputs and interfaces of the CDRRMO Head and its core functional divisions.

3.  Establish principles for staffing establishment, plantilla management, position descriptions, succession and workforce continuity.

4.  Clarify the distinction between permanent CDRRMO positions and personnel assigned or designated to support the EOC during activation.

5.  Provide a systematic approach to competency assessment, training, certification, exercises and professional development.

6.  Establish duty-of-care arrangements for staff and responders, including occupational safety, insurance, protective equipment, rest, mental health and psychosocial support.

7.  Strengthen performance management, supervision, records, accountability and continuous improvement.

8.  Provide operational workflows, RACI assignments, forms and evidence requirements that can be implemented and audited.

# 3. Scope and Application

This chapter applies to the CDRRMO as a permanent office of the City Government and to City personnel formally assigned, designated or mobilized to support its functions. It also establishes the personnel interface with the EOC, without replacing the detailed EOC activation, incident management and operational procedures in subsequent chapters.

# 5. Organizational Design Principles

| **Principle**                                   | **Operational application**                                                                                                         |
|-------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Legality and mandate alignment                  | Every function and position must be traced to a lawful mandate, approved structure or authorized designation.                       |
| Functional clarity                              | Each division and position must have defined responsibilities, deliverables, authority limits and reporting lines.                  |
| Separation of governance and management         | CDRRMC provides statutory oversight; CDRRMO manages the permanent technical and administrative work.                                |
| Separation of permanent and activated functions | The CDRRMO remains operational during normal periods; the EOC is configured and staffed according to activation requirements.       |
| Scalability and interoperability                | Staffing and role assignments must expand or contract according to incident complexity and integrate with ICS and partner agencies. |
| Continuity and redundancy                       | Critical positions require alternates, documented handover and succession arrangements.                                             |
| Competency-based deployment                     | Personnel are assigned according to qualifications, training, demonstrated competence and role requirements.                        |
| Inclusion and duty of care                      | Workforce arrangements must be safe, accessible, gender-responsive and attentive to fatigue and psychosocial needs.                 |
| Evidence and performance                        | Organizational effectiveness must be demonstrated through approved records, work outputs, exercises and review.                     |

![Figure 4: 5. Organizational Design Principles](/library/ch2v12/fig-04.webp)

The organizational model shall be proportionate to Ormoc City's risk profile, geographic and sectoral diversity, service obligations and available resources. It shall not assume that a larger organizational chart automatically produces better performance. The objective is a fit-for-purpose structure with clear accountability and sufficient capability to discharge mandatory functions.

# 6. CDRRMO Organizational Structure and Functional Architecture

The CDRRMO shall be headed by a qualified CDRRMO Head and organized around the three core functional areas identified in JMC No. 2014-1: (a) Administration and Training; (b) Research and Planning; and (c) Operations and Warning. The precise division names, unit configuration, staffing complement and reporting relationships shall follow the City's approved organizational and staffing pattern and applicable national requirements.

The proposed functional architecture below is a functional reference model for MOO purposes. It is not, by itself, an approved organogram or authority to create offices or positions.

# 7. Functional Mandates and Division-Level Responsibilities

## 7.1 CDRRMO Head

The CDRRMO Head provides overall leadership and is accountable for translating statutory mandates, CDRRMC policy direction and approved City plans into coordinated office work. Subject to the City's approved structure and applicable rules, the Head shall:

- Lead preparation and implementation of the CDRRMO annual work and financial plans, staffing proposals and performance commitments.

- Direct and integrate the three core functional divisions and ensure that responsibilities are allocated without duplication.

- Advise the City Mayor and CDRRMC on DRRM policy, institutional readiness, risk-informed priorities and capability gaps.

- Ensure that the CDRRMO performs its statutory and assigned Secretariat functions and maintains reliable governance records.

- Coordinate with CPDO and sector offices to mainstream DRR and CCA into development, land-use, investment and public-service continuity processes.

- Ensure that risk information, preparedness, early warning, response coordination and recovery support are linked through documented processes.

- Oversee readiness arrangements for the EOC, including pre-designated personnel, alternates, role descriptions and coordination with partner agencies.

- Ensure compliance with approved personnel, procurement, financial, records, privacy, occupational safety and reporting requirements.

- Lead performance review, after-action learning and corrective action within the CDRRMO's authority.

**Anticipatory-action focal person (A2.9).** The CDRRMO Head shall designate an anticipatory-action focal person by office order (RA 12287 IRR Rule IV Sec. 1; Rule VII). The focal person, BDRRMC officers, ACDVs and partner CSOs shall attend anticipatory-action training when OCD Region VIII cascades the IRR. This is a new mandatory item and currently scores 0.

## 7.2 Administration and Training Division

The Administration and Training Division provides the administrative and organizational support required for a dependable CDRRMO. It shall perform the following functions, subject to the approved division mandate and coordination with HRMO and other competent offices:

- Prepare and maintain administrative work plans, office records, correspondence, internal directives, schedules and document-control registers.

- Coordinate with HRMO on staffing records, personnel movement, attendance, leave, training nominations, performance documentation and position-description updates.

- Support CDRRMC Secretariat administration when assigned, including notices, agendas, attendance, minutes, resolutions and action monitoring.

- Maintain the CDRRMO's inventory of office equipment, supplies, vehicles, communications assets and administrative resources in coordination with property and supply offices.

- Coordinate training needs assessment, annual training plans, training records, competency matrices and post-training evaluation.

- Maintain staff induction materials, orientation checklists, internal administrative SOPs and handover records.

- Coordinate personnel welfare and administrative support during extended operations, including rest, meals, transport and family-contact arrangements as approved.

- Support budget preparation, procurement planning, records retention and audit readiness in coordination with Budget, Accounting, BAC and Records Management.

The Division shall not assume the statutory personnel authority of HRMO, the financial authority of the Budget and Accounting Offices, or procurement authority assigned to the BAC and authorized procurement officials.

## 7.3 Research and Planning Division

The Research and Planning Division provides the analytical and planning functions that enable the City to understand risk, prioritize interventions and integrate DRRM and climate resilience into development decisions. It shall:

- Coordinate preparation, review and updating of the Local DRRM Plan and relevant contingency, preparedness, mitigation and recovery plans.

- Maintain technical linkages with the CDRA, CLUP, CDP, LCCAP, public service continuity plans and sectoral plans, ensuring that versions, assumptions and data sources are traceable.

- Coordinate with CPDO, GIS/ICT and sector offices on hazard, exposure, vulnerability, capacity and critical-facility information, without displacing their technical mandates.

- Develop and maintain risk-information products, planning assumptions, scenario analyses and evidence briefs for CDRRMC and executive decision-making.

- Support pre-disaster risk assessment and anticipatory-action planning with Operations and Warning and relevant sector offices.

- Prepare planning inputs for annual investment programming, DRRM financing proposals, project screening and resource prioritization.

- Maintain planning indicators, monitoring frameworks, data dictionaries, evidence registers and knowledge products.

- Coordinate technical studies, research partnerships, lessons analysis and integration of climate and disaster risk into local policy.

- Ensure that data collection and sharing comply with privacy, confidentiality, records and information-security requirements.

The Division shall distinguish technical data stewardship from policy approval. CPDO remains the competent office for its statutory and assigned development-planning functions; engineering, health, environment and other technical offices retain their respective professional and regulatory mandates.

## 7.4 Operations and Warning Division

The Operations and Warning Division maintains the CDRRMO's operational readiness and coordinates warning, preparedness and response-support functions in accordance with approved plans and the separate EOC and ICS procedures. It shall:

- Maintain routine hazard monitoring and warning coordination arrangements with PAGASA, PHIVOLCS and other competent sources.

- Translate official warnings and risk information into operational recommendations and warning-action coordination with barangays and sector offices.

- Maintain duty officer arrangements, operational contact directories, readiness checklists and escalation protocols.

- Coordinate preparedness activities, drills, exercises and operational readiness reviews with BDRRMCs and response agencies.

- Support pre-disaster risk assessment, anticipatory action planning, prepositioning and evacuation recommendations under the applicable legal and operational procedures.

- Coordinate response information and resource requests through the activated EOC and designated incident management arrangements.

- Support the CDRRMO's coordination of response clusters and technical working arrangements without assuming the command authority of the Incident Commander or mandates of response agencies.

- Maintain operational logs, warning dissemination records, deployment coordination records and after-action inputs.

- Coordinate operational transition and technical inputs to recovery and rehabilitation functions.

The Division's internal duty officer system shall operate during normal periods and heightened readiness. Its activation and shift arrangements shall be aligned with the EOC procedures in the relevant chapters and shall not be used to imply that the full EOC is continuously activated.

# 8. Staffing Establishment, Plantilla and Workforce Planning

The CDRRMO shall maintain a staffing complement that is lawful, functionally sufficient, competency-based and sustainable. Staffing decisions shall be anchored in the statutory functions of the office, JMC No. 2014-1, the City's approved organizational structure and staffing pattern, CSC qualification standards, DBM requirements and available appropriations.

![Figure 10: 8. Staffing Establishment, Plantilla and Workforce Planning](/library/ch2v12/fig-10.webp)

## 8.1 Staffing Establishment Principles

- Mandatory office functions and minimum positions shall be identified first, before proposing additional or specialized roles.

- Each proposed position shall have a documented justification linking workload, risk exposure, service requirements, outputs and required competencies.

- Positions shall be created, reclassified, filled or redesigned only through the prescribed HRMO, Budget, DBM/CSC and local approval processes.

- Critical functions shall have designated alternates or continuity arrangements to reduce single-person dependency.

- Temporary, project-based, volunteer or partner personnel shall not be treated as substitutes for mandatory plantilla positions where law requires permanent staffing.

- Staffing proposals shall account for 24/7 operational requirements, leave, training, fatigue, field deployment and emergency surge demands.

## 8.2 Workforce and Plantilla Review

The CDRRMO Head, in coordination with HRMO and Budget, shall conduct an annual workforce review. The review shall compare the approved plantilla with required functions, filled and vacant positions, qualifications, workload, duty coverage, training and operational gaps.

| **Review dimension**      | **Minimum information / evidence**                                                                              |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Approved staffing pattern | Latest approved organizational chart, plantilla and relevant DBM/CSC approvals.                                 |
| Position status           | Authorized, filled, vacant, temporarily assigned, detailed or otherwise unavailable.                            |
| Qualification compliance  | Position qualification standards, appointment records and supporting credentials, subject to HRMO verification. |
| Workload and coverage     | Work plans, task distribution, duty rosters, leave coverage, incident deployments and overtime records.         |
| Competency profile        | Required versus demonstrated competencies, training and certification records.                                  |
| Continuity risks          | Single-person dependencies, absence of alternates, succession gaps and critical knowledge held by individuals.  |
| Resource implications     | Personnel services costs, training, PPE, insurance and operational support requirements.                        |

## 8.3 Minimum Staffing and Gap Analysis

A staffing gap analysis shall be prepared against the applicable JMC No. 2014-1 requirements and the current approved staffing pattern. The analysis shall not assume a fixed numerical complement without verifying the current standard and its applicability to Ormoc City. It shall identify: (a) required positions; (b) authorized positions; (c) filled positions; (d) vacancies; (e) qualification gaps; (f) operational coverage gaps; and (g) proposed corrective actions.

The assessment workbook's A2.3 indicator requires evidence of minimum plantilla positions and the ratio of filled to required positions. The CDRRMO and HRMO shall reconcile the indicator against the approved plantilla and current national guidance before using it as a compliance conclusion.

## 8.4 Position Creation and Approval Workflow

1.  CDRRMO Head documents the functional need, workload evidence, proposed duties, competency requirements and expected outputs.

2.  HRMO reviews the proposed position, qualification standards, organizational fit and personnel implications.

3.  The Budget Office reviews personnel-services affordability and funding implications.

4.  City Legal Office reviews legal authority and consistency with existing ordinances and organizational issuances where required.

5.  The proposal is routed through the City's authorized executive and legislative processes and to DBM/CSC or other competent authorities as applicable.

6.  Only after required approvals are secured shall the position be incorporated into the approved staffing pattern and recruitment or appointment process.

## 9.2 Delegation and accountability

Delegation shall be documented and limited to authority that may lawfully be delegated. It shall identify the delegating authority, delegate, scope, duration, conditions, reporting requirements and revocation mechanism. Delegation does not remove the delegating official's accountability for oversight where provided by law.

RACI matrices in this chapter allocate operational work but do not confer appointment authority, statutory decision rights, financial authority or professional licensing. Where a decision is reserved by law or City policy to a particular official or body, that authority prevails over a RACI designation.

# 11. Duty Systems, Availability and Continuity of Personnel

The CDRRMO shall maintain a duty system proportionate to its legal functions, operational requirements and approved staffing. The system shall distinguish routine office hours, duty officer coverage, heightened readiness and full EOC activation. It shall not assume continuous full EOC activation.

## 11.1 Duty roster and handover

A controlled duty roster shall identify primary personnel, alternates, contact details, duty period, supervisor, escalation route and any special competency or authorization required. Handover shall cover current warnings, incidents, pending actions, resource requests, equipment status, communications issues, unresolved decisions and records requiring follow-up.

**Current practice (site visit, October 2026).** The CDRRMO already runs 24-hour duty from the CDRRM Office for three priority areas: City Traffic Monitoring, Public Safety and Emergency Monitoring. To move A2.5 and B4.3 from 2 to 3, the CDRRMO shall: (a) have the White-level OpCen SOP approved; (b) give each monitoring stream a duty post, a task list and a reporting line, including who escalates to the CDRRMO Head and when; and (c) keep signed rosters and handover forms (Annex 2-E) for at least three months as proof of use.

## 11.2 Continuity and succession

- Maintain at least one trained alternate for critical CDRRMO functions and ensure EOC key positions are designated with alternates in accordance with the applicable EOC standard.

- Maintain updated contact and emergency notification lists with access controls.

- Document acting arrangements and succession in accordance with City rules.

- Ensure essential records, credentials and operating instructions are accessible to authorized alternates.

- Include staff absence, facility disruption, ICT outage and simultaneous incidents in continuity exercises.

- Coordinate Public Service Continuity Planning for CDRRMO functions with the City's designated continuity focal office.

## 11.3 Fatigue management

Duty schedules shall provide reasonable rest periods, relief arrangements, meals, hydration and safe transport where required. Supervisors shall monitor fatigue and fitness for duty, particularly during prolonged operations. # 13. Personnel Welfare, Occupational Safety and Psychosocial Support

The City shall apply a duty-of-care approach to CDRRMO personnel and responders. Personnel shall not be assigned to tasks beyond their competence, authorization or safe operating conditions. Safety and welfare arrangements shall be coordinated with HRMO, occupational safety and health focal persons, health services and other competent offices.

## 13.1 Minimum welfare and safety controls

- Documented hazard identification and risk assessment for office, field and emergency assignments.

- Appropriate PPE, safety equipment, communications and task-specific protective measures.

- Pre-deployment briefing, fitness-for-duty checks where appropriate, buddy arrangements and accountability procedures.

- Access to first aid, medical referral, insurance and applicable benefits, subject to law and approved City policy.

- Rest cycles, meals, hydration, sanitation and safe accommodation or transport during extended operations.

- Psychological first aid, psychosocial support, referral pathways and post-incident support, consistent with RA 11036 and applicable protocols.

- Protection against harassment, discrimination, retaliation and unsafe working conditions.

- Family communication and emergency contact arrangements consistent with privacy safeguards.

- Post-deployment check-in, incident reporting, injury/near-miss reporting and learning review.

Nos. 03 and 04 s.2022 (31 March 2022) adopted NDRRMC MC No. 64 s.2021 on the accreditation, mobilization and protection of community disaster volunteers, and approved a risk categorization as the basis for buying insurance for accredited volunteers. No policy or proof of purchase was sighted, and no equivalent arrangement covers regular CDRRMO staff (insurance, hazard pay, PPE issue, MHPSS). The responder welfare policy shall close both gaps.

## 13.2 Inclusion and reasonable accommodation

Personnel arrangements shall respect gender equality, disability inclusion, age-related needs, cultural and language diversity and reasonable accommodation. Field assignments, facilities, training and communications shall be designed to enable qualified personnel to participate safely and effectively.

## 13.3 Responder welfare ownership

# 14. Human Resource Administration, Performance and Conduct

All CDRRMO personnel remain subject to the City's approved personnel administration systems and applicable CSC rules. The CDRRMO Head and division chiefs shall align work plans and individual performance commitments with the CDRRMO mandate, approved office targets and City performance management requirements.

## 14.1 Performance management

- Translate annual office outputs into division and individual commitments.

- Use measurable indicators covering timeliness, quality, compliance, coordination, readiness and service outcomes.

- Conduct regular supervisory discussions and document performance support or corrective measures.

- Use after-action review findings and exercise results to identify training and process improvements.

- Ensure that institutional assessment findings are used for system improvement and not as a substitute for formal individual performance evaluation.

## 14.2 Conduct, confidentiality and information security

Personnel shall comply with applicable codes of conduct, confidentiality obligations, records rules, data privacy and cybersecurity policies. Access to beneficiary information, personnel files, health information, incident intelligence and sensitive operational records shall be role-based and limited to legitimate official purposes.

Volunteers, accredited civil society partners, auxiliary personnel and surge staff may augment capacity where legally permitted and appropriately managed. Their roles, supervision, safety, training, insurance, access to information and limits of authority shall be documented. # 15. Workforce Governance Workflows

## 15.1 Annual workforce planning and staffing review

1.  CDRRMO Head issues the annual workforce review instruction and confirms the review period.

2.  Division chiefs submit workload, outputs, staffing, competency, duty coverage and continuity information.

3.  Administration and Training consolidates the workforce profile and evidence register.

4.  HRMO validates positions, qualification standards, appointment status and personnel implications.

5.  The Budget Office assesses personnel-services and training affordability.

6.  CDRRMO Head approves the office-level workforce gap analysis for submission through the City's authorized process.

7.  Corrective actions are assigned, monitored and reviewed quarterly.

## 15.2 Recruitment, appointment and onboarding interface

1.  CDRRMO identifies an approved vacant position and documents operational requirements.

2.  HRMO confirms vacancy status, qualification standards, recruitment and selection requirements.

3.  Budget confirms funding and personnel-services availability.

4.  Recruitment, selection and appointment proceed through the City's authorized process and CSC rules.

5.  Upon appointment or assignment, the supervisor provides an induction briefing, position description, work plan, records and access orientation, safety briefing and training needs assessment.

6.  Completion of onboarding is documented and retained in the appropriate personnel and office records.

## 15.3 Training needs assessment and development cycle

1.  Map position competencies and assess current proficiency.

2.  Identify gaps using performance, incident, exercise and assessment evidence.

3.  Prioritize training by legal requirement, risk and operational criticality.

4.  Prepare and approve the annual training plan and budget.

5.  Deliver training and maintain verifiable records.

6.  Evaluate application in work performance or exercises and update the competency matrix.

## 15.4 EOC personnel designation and readiness

1.  CDRRMO Head identifies permanent office personnel who may be assigned to EOC roles, in consultation with division chiefs.

2.  Participating City offices and partner agencies nominate qualified primary and alternate liaison personnel.

3.  HRMO and the relevant office head confirm administrative status and availability.

4.  CDRRMO maintains the controlled roster and records relevant training and competencies.

5.  On activation, personnel report under the approved EOC activation and staffing procedure.

6.  After deactivation, personnel return to their normal assignments and submit handover, records and welfare reports as required.

## 15.5 Personnel welfare incident and corrective action

1.  Personnel report injury, exposure, near miss, fatigue concern or psychosocial support need through the designated channel.

2.  Supervisor ensures immediate safety, medical response or referral and notification of the competent office.

3.  HRMO / occupational safety / health focal persons record and assess the incident under applicable rules.

4.  CDRRMO reviews operational implications and identifies corrective actions.

5.  Actions are assigned, tracked and verified for effectiveness without disclosing protected personal information.

# 20. Implementation, Review and Chapter Conclusion

The CDRRMO Head shall lead implementation of this chapter in coordination with HRMO, Budget, the City Legal Office and the relevant City offices. The first implementation cycle shall establish the verified organizational baseline: current legal basis, approved organogram, plantilla, filled and vacant positions, qualification status, position descriptions, duty arrangements, competency profile and welfare controls.

Implementation shall proceed through approved City processes. No provision of this chapter shall be interpreted as independently authorizing the creation of positions, appointment of personnel, payment of additional compensation, transfer of personnel, procurement of equipment or expenditure of public funds.

This chapter shall be reviewed at least annually and whenever there is a material change in law, national guidance, City organizational structure, staffing pattern, risk profile, operational requirements or institutional assessment findings. Revisions shall be controlled, approved by the competent authority and communicated to all affected personnel.

A mature CDRRMO is not defined merely by its formal existence. It is defined by its ability to sustain qualified personnel, clear functional responsibilities, reliable duty coverage, institutional memory, safe working conditions and coordinated performance across normal operations and emergencies. By linking organizational design to workforce planning, competency development, operational readiness and continuous improvement, this chapter provides the human and institutional capacity foundation for the succeeding operational modules.

# 21. Integrated Process Architecture and Process Flows

To operationalize the organizational model, the CDRRMO shall manage workforce and institutional-support activities as controlled end-to-end processes. Each process must identify its trigger, accountable owner, authorized decision-maker, required inputs, control points, outputs, records, service standard, escalation path and review mechanism. The flows below describe the normal administrative pathway; urgent life-safety action during an incident shall follow the applicable emergency procedures and shall not be delayed by routine HR approval steps.

## 21.1 End-to-end Workforce Management Cycle

![Figure 19: 21.1 End-to-end Workforce Management Cycle](/library/ch2v12/fig-19.webp)

A**nnual cycle**: City DRRM priorities and approved plans → workload and capability analysis → review of approved structure and plantilla → identify critical gaps and risks → prepare staffing, succession and learning proposals → HRMO/Finance/legal review → approval by competent authority → recruitment or lawful designation → induction and competency development → performance and welfare monitoring → annual evaluation and corrective action. Any proposal requiring a new or reclassified position, compensation adjustment or additional appropriation must pass the applicable City, CSC and DBM processes before implementation.

| Stage                | Process owner / lead                      | Control / decision gate                                                           | Required evidence                                      |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Analyze demand   | CDRRMO Head with division leads           | Link tasks to LDRRMP, risk profile, service standards and workload                | Workload analysis; risk and service demand register    |
| 2\. Assess supply    | HRMO with CDRRMO                          | Verify authorized plantilla, filled/vacant posts, qualifications and availability | Certified plantilla; staffing and competency inventory |
| 3\. Identify gap     | CDRRMO Head                               | Separate structural, staffing, skill, equipment and process gaps                  | Gap analysis; risk rating; options appraisal           |
| 4\. Develop proposal | CDRRMO / HRMO                             | No assumed position creation; assess alternatives and cost                        | Staffing plan; draft PDFs; cost and funding note       |
| 5\. Review / approve | HRMO, Budget, CLO; Mayor/SP as applicable | Approval follows statutory and administrative authority                           | Review endorsements; approved issuance / appropriation |
| 6\. Implement        | HRMO and CDRRMO                           | Appointment/designation and onboarding comply with rules                          | Appointment; designation; induction record             |
| 7\. Verify / improve | CDRRMO Head / HRMO                        | Test service coverage, competence, safety and outcomes                            | KPI dashboard; CAPA; annual review                     |

## 21.2 Process flow – Organizational and Plantilla Change

![Figure 20: 21.2 Process flow – Organizational and Plantilla Change](/library/ch2v12/fig-20.webp)

**Trigger**: new mandate, material workload change, assessment finding, vacancy pattern or approved restructuring priority.  
CDRRMO documents need → HRMO validates organizational and position requirements → Budget assesses fiscal space and recurring cost → City Legal reviews legal authority and instrument → competent authority approves or rejects → HRMO processes authorized staffing action → CDRRMO updates organogram, role assignments and MOO → implementation is monitored and reviewed.  
HOLD POINT: no position may be treated as created, funded or fillable until the required approval, appropriation and plantilla action are complete.

## 21.3 Process flow – Recruitment, Appointment and Onboarding Interface

![Figure 21: 21.3 Process flow – Recruitment, Appointment and Onboarding Interface](/library/ch2v12/fig-21.webp)

Vacancy / approved staffing need confirmed → HRMO verifies authorized vacant plantilla and qualification standards → CDRRMO validates technical competency profile → HRMO applies approved Merit Selection Plan and recruitment procedures → selection and recommendation → appointing authority acts → HRMO completes appointment documentation and assumption → CDRRMO conducts role-specific induction and safety briefing → supervisor sets performance outputs and learning plan → probationary / periodic performance review as applicable. CDRRMO does not conduct appointments or waive civil-service requirements.

## 21.4 Process flow – Competency and Learning Cycle

![Figure 22: 21.4 Process flow – Competency and Learning Cycle](/library/ch2v12/fig-22.webp)

Position competency profile → individual competency assessment → consolidate competency gaps → prioritize by life-safety, statutory, operational and service risk → approve annual learning plan and resources → deliver training / coaching / exercise → assess demonstrated competence (not attendance alone) → update individual records and duty eligibility → reassess and close gaps. Critical assignments must be limited to personnel who meet the approved competency and safety prerequisites, with supervised development pathways for trainees.

**21.5 Process flow – duty roster, handover and operational augmentation**

![Figure 23: 21.5 Process flow – duty roster, handover and operational augmentation](/library/ch2v12/fig-23.webp)

Forecast duty demand and availability → supervisor prepares roster from trained and available personnel → division head verifies coverage, alternates and fatigue controls → CDRRMO Head approves roster under delegated authority → personnel acknowledge assignment → outgoing/incoming duty officers complete structured handover → exceptions or unfilled shifts escalated immediately → EOC activation or surge request is processed under the relevant EOC chapter → duty records reconciled after shift → gaps entered into CAPA. This chapter establishes personnel readiness; activation levels, EOC configuration and incident command remain in Chapters 4 and 6.

## 21.6 Process flow – Personnel Safety, Welfare and Incident Reporting

![Figure 24: 21.6 Process flow – Personnel Safety, Welfare and Incident Reporting](/library/ch2v12/fig-24.webp)

Unsafe condition / injury / exposure / fatigue / psychosocial concern identified → make area safe and obtain immediate medical or protective assistance → notify supervisor and designated safety focal person → preserve incident facts and protect privacy → refer to HRMO / OSH / health provider / MHPSS provider as appropriate → assess fitness for duty and accommodation → investigate systemic causes without prejudging personnel accountability → implement corrective action → verify effectiveness and close record. Emergency response and medical care take precedence over administrative reporting.

## 21.7 Process flow – Performance Management and Corrective Action

![Figure 25: 21.7 Process flow – Performance Management and Corrective Action](/library/ch2v12/fig-25.webp)

Approved office and individual outputs → periodic coaching and evidence capture → performance review under the City's approved SPMS → identify performance or process gap → distinguish individual competency issue from systemic constraint → agree improvement action and support → monitor milestones → evaluate results → recognize good performance or initiate appropriate administrative process where warranted → feed systemic lessons into workforce planning and MOO revision.
