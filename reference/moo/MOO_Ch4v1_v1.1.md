# 1. Introduction and Assessment-informed Rationale

Early warning and emergency response are not isolated communication or rescue activities. They are a coordinated chain that connects authoritative hazard monitoring, impact interpretation, risk communication, local decision-making, preparedness actions, incident command, EOC coordination, response services and transition to recovery. The effectiveness of this chain depends on whether information reaches the right decision-maker in time, whether decisions are made by the legally authorised person or body, and whether actions are carried out through clearly assigned institutions and documented procedures.

Chapters 1–3 establish the institutional, workforce and risk-intelligence foundations for this chapter. Chapter 1 defines the mandates and decision rights of the City Mayor, CDRRMC, CDRRMO, EOC and Incident Command System (ICS). Chapter 2 establishes the permanent CDRRMO workforce and the personnel arrangements that support an activated EOC. Chapter 3 establishes the risk-information base, scenario and contingency planning, inclusive preparedness and continuity arrangements. Chapter 4 translates those foundations into an operational system for warning, readiness, activation, incident coordination and response support.

The October 2026 institutional assessment and the Chapter 1–3 drafts indicate that the City has operational practices and important response capabilities, but that the institutionalisation of the warning-to-action chain requires further documentation, approval, exercise and evidence. The CDRRMO's normal duty and monitoring functions, the developing 24-hour OpCen, scenario-specific preparedness and the proposed EOC structure must function as one coherent system without confusing permanent office management, EOC coordination and field command.

This chapter applies systems thinking and Total Quality Management (TQM). It considers warning and response as an end-to-end process with feedback loops: warning receipt and verification; impact and exposure analysis; decision and authorisation; dissemination and acknowledgement; protective action; operational monitoring; review; and improvement. A warning successfully transmitted is not necessarily a warning understood or acted upon. An activation order is not evidence of adequate staffing. A response activity is not complete until its outcome, outstanding needs and handover are recorded.

# 2. Purpose and Intended Results

This chapter establishes the City's minimum operating arrangements for receiving and verifying early warnings, translating risk information into timely action, activating the EOC, coordinating incident support and documenting response performance. It does not create an independent command system or transfer the legal powers of the Mayor, CDRRMC, Sangguniang Panlungsod, technical agencies or Incident Commander.

The intended institutional results are:

- An authoritative, traceable warning-receipt and verification process linked to approved hazard-specific contingency plans.

- A locally approved warning-to-action matrix with clear decision authority, lead/support offices, target groups, dissemination channels and acknowledgement requirements.

- A documented White–Blue–Red activation and deactivation process, including the transition from the existing OpCen duty arrangement to an activated EOC.

- A scenario-based EOC structure that scales according to incident complexity while retaining a stable reporting architecture and clear relationship with ICS/IMT.

- Operational arrangements for incident objectives, operational periods, tasking, coordination meetings, escalation and handover, consistent with the applicable ICS doctrine and local issuances.

- Coordinated and accessible warning and protective-action arrangements for barangays and at-risk groups, including persons with disabilities, older persons, children, pregnant/lactating women and other groups identified in the risk assessment.

- Documented response coordination for evacuation, search and rescue, emergency medical and public health support, protection, shelter/CCCM, WASH, logistics, utilities and other sector functions according to their mandates.

- Consistent incident records, logbook entries, SitReps, decision records, barangay reports and resource status information, with privacy and data-integrity controls.

- Exercise-tested procedures, after-action reviews and corrective actions that demonstrate institutional use rather than documentary completion alone.

## 6.2 Warning Receipt, Verification and Escalation

1.  Receive the official product or report through an approved source channel. Record the issue time shown by the source and the time received by the City duty officer. Preserve the original product or a traceable reference to it.

2.  Check issuing agency, product identifier, authenticity, validity period, geographic coverage, hazard type and update/cancellation status. Where the source is unclear or a product appears superseded, contact the issuing agency or its designated channel.

3.  Enter the information in the warning/monitoring log and identify the likely affected barangays, exposed population, critical facilities, lifelines and essential services using the current approved risk-information base.

4.  Compare the product with the relevant approved contingency plan and warning-action matrix. Identify the actions that may be initiated by the duty officer, the actions requiring EOC Manager/CDRRMO Head recommendation and the actions requiring Mayor/CDRRMC or other lawful authority.

5.  Escalate material changes, imminent impacts, conflicting information, suspected failure of a lifeline or a report of casualties/large displacement through the duty roster and approved escalation directory. Do not delay urgent life-safety notifications while routine records are being completed.

6.  Issue an internal warning brief to the CDRRMO Head and relevant decision-makers. State the source, issue/receipt times, hazard, affected area, forecast/observation, expected impacts, lead time, confidence/uncertainty, recommended options and decision required.

7.  Update the log when a new bulletin supersedes or cancels a prior product. Notify recipients of material changes and record which earlier message/version is superseded.

The regional reference for the matrix is Table 2 of the DILG VIII reporting SOP (RDRRMC VIII Resolution No. 12-006, s. 2023), which lists the conditions and triggers for raising the alert status by hazard. Annex 4-C summarises it. Those triggers become City triggers only when the approved EOC Executive Order or a hazard-specific contingency plan adopts them; until then the matrix cites them as the regional reference.

## 6.4 Risk Communication and Warning Dissemination

The designated warning/public information focal person shall coordinate dissemination in accordance with approved City communication and approval arrangements. Technical warnings must retain their official meaning. Any City message that translates a warning into local protective action must identify the authorised issuer, action, affected area, effective time, practical instruction, support information and the channel for clarification.

- Use more than one communication channel where practicable, with backup arrangements for power, mobile network or internet disruption.

- Use clear, concise and action-oriented language. Avoid unexplained technical terms and ambiguous expressions such as “prepare” where a specific action is intended.

- Provide appropriate formats and relay support for people with hearing, visual, cognitive, mobility or language barriers. Coordinate with barangay officials, CSOs and relevant sector offices for local accessibility and comprehension.

- Identify the official source and time of the warning and state whether the information is preliminary, updated, cancelled or superseded.

- Distinguish an advisory, recommendation, evacuation instruction and legally authorised order. Do not imply that a CDRRMO or EOC communication has authority it does not possess.

- Record failed delivery and non-acknowledgement. Escalate to alternate channels and local relays. A sent message alone is not evidence that the intended audience received and understood it.

# 8. EOC Activation, Scaling and Deactivation

## 8.1 Purpose and Activation Principles

The EOC provides the City's central mechanism for operational coordination during emergencies and heightened incidents, as established by the valid local EOC issuance. Activation brings together the functions and personnel needed to maintain a shared operational picture, support incident command, coordinate response clusters, prioritise resource requests and elevate decisions. Activation does not itself confer statutory powers or change the employment and administrative relationships of designated staff.

- Activation shall follow approved triggers, authority, notification and documentation in the EOC EO and contingency plans.

- The EOC is scalable. Only functions and cluster desks required by the scenario and incident complexity are staffed, while the common organisational structure remains recognisable.

- The EOC Manager manages EOC coordination. The Incident Commander/IMT directs on-scene operations.

- The CDRRMO continues its permanent office functions while supporting the activated EOC.

- If the EOC facility is unsafe or unavailable, the approved alternate arrangement shall be used. Do not invent an alternate EOC site in the Manual.

# 20. Conclusion

Chapter 4 establishes the operational chain that connects authoritative warnings and verified local observations with timely decisions, risk communication, EOC activation, ICS-supported incident management, emergency response coordination and transition. Its central control is clarity: the source of information is identifiable; technical meaning is preserved; each decision is made by the competent authority; each task has a responsible office; the EOC supports rather than commands field operations; and each action can be traced through an operational record.

The chapter is not implemented merely because these procedures are written. Institutionalisation requires valid local EOC and ICS issuances, hazard-specific warning-action matrices, current source and contact registers, named staff and alternates, accessible communication channels, exercised EOC/ICS interfaces, reliable reporting, secure and complete records, and corrective actions verified by the responsible offices. Until these controls are approved, tested and evidenced, this chapter remains a controlled working draft.
