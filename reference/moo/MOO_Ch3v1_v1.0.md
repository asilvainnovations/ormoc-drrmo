# 1. Introduction and Assessment-informed Rationale

This chapter establishes the City's operating system for understanding disaster and climate risk, translating risk evidence into land-use and development decisions, preparing for priority hazard scenarios, maintaining essential public services during disruption, and strengthening preparedness from city to barangay level. It treats risk intelligence as a continuous institutional process—not a one-time mapping or report-writing exercise.

**Figure 3.1**

*From Static Risk Assets to a Continuous Risk-Intelligence System*

![Figure 1: 1. Introduction and Assessment-informed Rationale](/library/ch3v10/fig-01.webp)

*Note.* Conceptual illustration prepared for this chapter from the IAT findings (A5.1, A5.2, A5.7).

The chapter is based on the October 2026 Ormoc CDRRMO–EOC Institutional Assessment Tool (IAT) workbook, the governance and chapter architecture established in Chapter 1, and the process ownership and documented-control approach established in Chapter 2. The IAT is a diagnostic instrument. Its scores are not legal certification and do not independently establish that a plan, map, database or process is current, approved, complete or operational.

The assessment indicates that Ormoc has an existing CDRA, hazard information, a Local DRRM Plan and contingency-planning activity. The critical issue is the conversion of these assets into a coherent, maintained and decision-used system: complete multi-hazard coverage; technically validated exposure and vulnerability data; spatial integration into CLUP and zoning; risk screening of development proposals; tested hazard-specific contingency arrangements; and continuity arrangements for essential city services.

# 2. Purpose and Intended Results

The purpose of this chapter is to standardize how Ormoc City identifies, assesses, communicates, plans for and periodically reviews disaster and climate risks. It shall produce the following institutional results:

- An authoritative, version-controlled city risk information base, with source provenance, scale, date, limitations and responsible custodian for each dataset.

- A technically defensible CDRA that follows the applicable DHSUD methodology and is spatially explicit, multi-hazard, multi-sectoral and usable for land-use and development decisions.

- An approved and monitored LDRRMP aligned with the NDRRMP and integrated with the CLUP, CDP, LCCAP, ELA, LDIP, AIP and annual budget processes through CPDO-led planning mechanisms.

- Hazard-specific contingency plans with clear scenario assumptions, objectives, roles, triggers, resource arrangements, information flows and exercise evidence.

- A Public Service Continuity Plan based on an essential-functions inventory, business impact analysis, dependencies, continuity strategies, recovery priorities and tested arrangements.

- A quality-assured system for reviewing barangay DRRM plans, including a verified barangay master list and documented feedback and follow-up.

- Evidence that gender, age, disability, child protection, Indigenous Peoples' rights and other contextually relevant inclusion requirements shape risk assessment, planning and service arrangements.

# 7. Risk Information Governance and Authoritative Hazard Data

The CDRRMO shall maintain a City Risk Information Register and a controlled geospatial repository. It shall not independently re-author or downgrade authoritative hazard products.

Source agencies retain responsibility for their technical products; the City records provenance, communicates limitations and determines how approved information is used in local planning.

| **Data class**                                                                     | **Preferred source / custodian**                                            | **Required metadata and validation**                                                                                                     |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

| Ground shaking, fault, liquefaction, tsunami, volcanic hazards                     | DOST-PHIVOLCS / GeoRiskPH                                                   | Map/report title, scale, date, model assumptions, legend, hazard intensity, limitations and whether site-specific assessment is required |

No single hazard portal or map layer should be treated as a complete site-specific engineering determination. Where a decision requires parcel-level, geotechnical, structural or other specialist assessment, the City shall obtain the assessment from the competent agency or licensed professional under the applicable regulatory process.

# 8. Hazard Information Acquisition, Validation and Mapping

The mapping process is controlled by source provenance and fitness-for-purpose. The CDRRMO GIS/risk-information custodian shall maintain the layer catalogue; the originating agency remains the technical source. A map shall not be published as an official City planning layer until its source, date, scale, coordinate system, legend, geographic coverage and limitations have been checked and the relevant technical office has signed the validation record.

**Figure 3.4**

*Authoritative Hazard Data and Provenance*

![Figure 4: 8. Hazard Information Acquisition, Validation and Mapping](/library/ch3v10/fig-04.webp)

*Note.* Source agencies remain the technical owners of their products (Section 7).

1.  Register the request and define the decision purpose, hazard, geographic extent, map scale and intended users. Reject requests that do not specify whether the output is for screening, planning, public information or site-specific technical review.

2.  Acquire the current official dataset from the responsible agency or its designated portal. Retain the original download, accompanying metadata, readme, legends, technical notes and correspondence; record access date and version.

3.  Check completeness and integrity: file opens; geometry is valid; attributes and classes are present; units and coordinate reference system are known; coverage includes the required City/barangay extent; and metadata identifies scale and date.

4.  Compare the product with other authoritative layers and local evidence without averaging or arbitrarily choosing conflicting values. Log discrepancies, identify which agency owns the disputed technical interpretation, and request clarification or a revised product.

5.  Transform or reproject a working copy only when necessary, document the original and target coordinate reference systems, transformation method and software/version, and preserve the unmodified source file.

6.  Overlay the hazard layer with the authoritative barangay boundary, exposure units and critical assets. Record the spatial operation, date, data versions, treatment of no-data areas and any assumptions.

7.  Submit the draft map to the relevant technical office and source agency for validation where needed. The review must check scale suitability, legend, interpretation, local anomalies, uncertainty and whether the layer is suitable for the stated decision.

8.  Approve the map for a defined use category: internal screening, public information, CLUP/CDP planning layer, or regulatory use. Regulatory use requires the appropriate CPDO/Zoning/Engineering/legal and formal adoption route.

9.  Publish the approved version with map title, date, source, scale, legend, limitations, contact and version number. Archive superseded versions and notify users when a material revision occurs.

**Decision control**: If source data conflict, are outdated, have inadequate scale, or leave an unmapped area, the map custodian shall mark the limitation and refer the issue to the source agency and relevant technical office. The CDRRMO shall not fill a gap by drawing an unverified hazard boundary. The output is a controlled map package: source files, metadata, validation record, map layout, approval/use classification, publication record and revision entry.

# 9. Climate and Disaster Risk Assessment (CDRA)

Ormoc shall conduct and update its CDRA using the applicable DHSUD Enhanced CDRA Policy and current official CDRA training and technical materials. **10. Exposure, Vulnerability, Loss and Impact-chain Analysis**

The exposure database shall identify what is located in hazard-affected areas; vulnerability analysis shall explain why and how exposed elements may be harmed; capacity analysis shall identify resources and adaptive capabilities that reduce impacts.

The analysis must not treat exposure as synonymous with risk. It must include direct and, where data permit, indirect and cascading impacts.

Personal and sensitive personal information shall not be placed in public-facing GIS layers or general-purpose map exports. Use aggregated or appropriately de-identified information for planning and publication. Household-level targeting data must be held by authorized custodians under a documented data-sharing and access-control arrangement consistent with RA 10173.

# 11. Critical Facilities, Lifelines and Cascading-Failure Analysis

The existing CDRA facility and lifeline inventory is the starting point, not the completed dependency analysis. The IAT identifies that facilities and lifelines have been listed but interdependencies and failure chains have not yet been mapped or used to set protection and restoration priorities.

1.  Confirm the asset universe and unique asset identifier with each owner/operator. At minimum include EOC, hospitals, health centres, evacuation centres, schools used for authorized emergency functions, bridges, major roads, port, water supply, wastewater, power, telecommunications, fuel and key government facilities.

2.  For each asset, record location, owner/operator, service function, capacity, service population/area, operating hours, condition, hazard exposure, vulnerability, backup arrangements, access routes, critical inputs and restoration dependencies.

3.  Identify upstream and downstream dependencies. Examples to test—not presume—include power loss affecting water pumping, telecommunications and hospital operations; road/bridge disruption isolating evacuation centres; fuel disruption affecting generators, ambulances and water delivery; and EOC communications failure affecting coordination.

4.  Construct directed dependency maps and scenario-based failure chains. For each chain, identify initiating hazard, asset failure, service consequence, affected population, duration, cascading assets and alternative arrangements.

5.  Validate each dependency with the asset owner/operator and affected service providers. Record disputed or unknown links as data gaps; do not convert assumptions into confirmed dependencies.

6.  Prioritize risk treatment and continuity options using consequence, likelihood, service criticality, redundancy, restoration time and vulnerable-population impact. Use approved criteria and document any local weighting adopted.

7.  Integrate priority actions into asset management, capital investment, contingency plans and PSCP. Maintain a restoration-priority list with accountable owner, target, funding route and evidence of completion.

# 12. Risk-Informed Planning and Investment Mainstreaming

# 15. Barangay DRRM Planning and Quality Assurance

RA 10121 establishes a BDRRMC in every barangay and assigns local DRRM planning and coordination functions. **The current denominator is 85 barangays** (PSA Philippine Standard Geographic Code, fourth quarter 2022): the 28 numbered Poblacion barangays were merged into South, East and West, and Barangay 29 became North. The CLUP 2017–2027 (110 barangays: 69 rural, 41 urban, 16 coastal), the DRRM 10-Year Plan and parts of the CDRA still use the former configuration. CPDO, the City Secretary and the DILG City Local Government Operations Office shall file the merger instrument and plebiscite record in the Vault and keep a crosswalk from the 110 former barangays to the 85 current ones, so that older counts (for example the 46 critical flood barangays) can be restated.

1.  Maintain one master register of barangays with official name, geographic identifier, legal status, boundary reference, population reference year and responsible BDRRMC contact.

2.  Request the current BDRRMP and approval evidence from each barangay using the current DILG/OCD-prescribed template and review tool. Record the document version, coverage period, approval date and evidence source.

3.  Conduct a completeness and quality review covering risk profile, priority hazards, exposed and vulnerable groups, early warning, evacuation, response arrangements, resources, annual work programme, budget, inclusion and monitoring.

4.  Return each plan with specific, actionable comments linked to the applicable quality criteria; identify mandatory corrections separately from recommended improvements.

5.  Verify that comments have been addressed and record the disposition. A submitted plan is not counted as quality-assured merely because it has been received.

6.  Consolidate status by barangay: not submitted, submitted, under review, returned for revision, technically accepted, approved, tested, and due for update.

7.  Provide technical assistance and schedule plan-testing support. Link barangay plans with City hazard scenarios, evacuation arrangements, warning protocols and resource requests without removing barangay ownership.

8.  Report gaps and overdue actions to the CDRRMC and DILG through the applicable channel; maintain a follow-up register.

# 21. Conclusion

Chapter 3 establishes a risk-intelligence and preparedness system that connects authoritative hazard information, method-compliant CDRA, exposure and vulnerability analysis, critical infrastructure dependencies, development planning, contingency planning, service continuity and barangay preparedness. Its central control is traceability: every important risk finding must have a source, a technical owner, a decision use, a responsible office and a record showing whether the required action was completed.

The chapter is not considered implemented merely because it is approved or included in the Manual of Operations. Institutionalization requires current datasets, signed technical validation, adopted plans, completed screening records, exercised contingency and continuity arrangements, verified barangay coverage, documented corrective actions and periodic evidence-based review.
