# 1. Introduction and Assessment-informed Rationale

Money is part of the warning-to-action chain. A warning that cannot be matched by funds, supplies and a lawful way to buy them does not turn into protective action. RA 10121 gives the City a dedicated fund for disaster risk reduction and management; RA 12287 now allows part of it to be spent before a hazard strikes. Both depend on plans approved in advance, procedures that the Budget, Accounting, Treasury and BAC offices recognise, and records that stand up to audit.

## 1.2 Design considerations

1. **Two clocks.** Normal budgeting runs on an annual calendar. Emergency spending runs in hours. Both must lead to the same records.
2. **Three funding windows.** Before impact (anticipatory action funds under RA 12287), during and after impact (QRF under a State of Calamity), and the rest of the year (the 70% component and the Special Trust Fund). Each window has its own legal trigger; this chapter keeps them apart.
3. **Pre-arrangement is the speed.** Emergency procurement is fastest when the supplier list, framework agreements, specifications and price references exist before the event.
4. **Evidence by design.** Every SOP ends in a form that an auditor can follow from appropriation to delivery and use.
5. **No invented thresholds.** Where national guidelines are still pending (NDRRMC operational guidelines on AA triggers; DBM–COA AA fund guidelines), the chapter states the gap and holds the parameter in a rule that can change without rewriting the procedure.

# 2. Purpose and Intended Results

This chapter sets the City's procedures for planning, releasing, spending, recording and reporting DRRM funds, for buying what DRRM needs in normal times and in emergencies, and for accounting for money, goods and donations entrusted to DRRM.

Intended results:

1. The LDRRMF is computed on the correct base, split 70/30, and programmed through an LDRRMFIP that is consistent with the LDRRMP, the CDP and the AIP.
2. An anticipatory action line of up to 10% of the 70% component is programmed, with Budget, Accounting, Treasury and BAC steps that allow immediate release under a State of Imminent Disaster and observe the 30%/70% tranche rule.
3. The QRF is used only within its legal trigger and purpose, and its use is reported.
4. Unexpended balances are tracked by year of origin and used within five years.
5. Emergency procurement is pre-arranged and every emergency purchase can show its legal ground.
6. Donations and external assistance are received, recorded, kept and used as the donor and the law require, and are disclosed.
7. Utilisation is reported quarterly to the CDRRMC and posted under the Full Disclosure Policy.
8. COA observations on DRRM funds are answered and closed.
9. The City knows how far its funds cover a severe credible event, and what it would draw on beyond them.

# 5. Systems-Thinking and TQM Principles for DRRM Finance

- **One chain from plan to proof.** LDRRMP target → LDRRMFIP line → AIP/budget → PPMP/APP → obligation → procurement → delivery → use → report → audit. A break anywhere shows up as unspent money or unsupported spending.
- **Speed through preparation.** The fastest emergency purchase is the one whose supplier, specification and price were agreed before the event.
- **Separation of duties.** The office that requests is not the office that certifies, pays or awards. MODE records each step by position.
- **Feedback loops.** Quarterly utilisation, the STF ageing ledger and COA observations feed the next LDRRMFIP.
- **Prevention over correction.** Pre-audit checklists (Annex 5-B) prevent the most common COA findings: non-DRRM charges, QRF use without a declaration, missing utilisation reports and incomplete supporting documents.

# 6. LDRRMF Planning and Allocation

## 6.1 Computing the fund

The LDRRMF is not less than 5% of **estimated revenue from regular sources** (RA 10121 Sec. 21). For a city this means the NTA share plus regular local revenue (local taxes, fees and charges, and other recurring income), as defined by the Budget Office under DBM rules. It excludes one-time receipts, grants and borrowings.

The CY2026 computation used the NTA alone (Res. No. 05, s. 2025). The City Budget Office confirms the base each year on FIN-F01 and keeps the computation with the budget. If the base was understated, the difference is appropriated through a supplemental budget or set right in the next budget, as the Budget Office and SP decide.

## 6.2 The 70/30 split and the AA line

| Component | Share | Use | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick Response Fund | 30% of the LDRRMF | Standby fund for relief and recovery | State of Calamity declared by the SP (JMC 2013-1) |
| Prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, rehabilitation and recovery | 70% of the LDRRMF | PPAs in the LDRRMFIP | Normal budget execution |
| — of which anticipatory action line | No more than 10% of the 70% | AA measures under RA 12287 | State of Imminent Disaster in force |

**Worked example (illustration only, CY2026 figures).** 70% component PHP 97,969,018.98 → maximum AA line PHP 9,796,901.90. For a hydrometeorological or geological hazard, at most PHP 2,939,070.57 (30%) may be disbursed for interventions more than 3 days before forecast impact; up to PHP 6,857,831.33 (70%) for interventions within 3 days. The CY2026 plan has no AA line, so these figures apply only once a plan includes one.

## 6.3 The LDRRMF Investment Plan

The LDRRMFIP is the annual plan that turns the LDRRMP into funded lines. Each line carries: the LDRRMP and CDP PPA code; thematic area (prevention and mitigation, preparedness, response, rehabilitation and recovery); fund source (70%, QRF, STF by year of origin, other); amount; implementing office; output and target; procurement mode; and quarter of implementation. Three lines are always shown on their own: the **AA line** (A4.6), the **EOC operations and maintenance line** (B7.5), and **insurance premiums** for DRRM assets (A4.7).

## 6.4 Placement in the City budget calendar

| Period | Budget calendar event | Chapter 5 action |
|---|---|---|
| January–February | Prior-year closing; COA audit fieldwork | STF ageing update (SOP 5-03); Q4 utilisation report (SOP 5-02) |
| April–May | COA Annual Audit Report | Audit response plan (SOP 5-10) |
| By mid-year | Budget call; LDC/LFC deliberations | LDRRMFIP drafting (SOP 5-01); adequacy test (SOP 5-09); supplier list refresh (SOP 5-06) |

Exact dates follow the Local Budget Calendar and the City Budget Office's budget call each year.

# 7. Budget Execution, Utilisation and Disclosure

Execution follows the City's normal controls: allotment, obligation request certified by the Budget Officer, procurement, inspection and acceptance, disbursement voucher certified by the Accountant, payment by the Treasurer. The CDRRMO's role is to keep the LDRRMFIP line visible through each step.

**Utilisation measures (computed quarterly, by component):**

- Obligation rate = obligations ÷ allotment for the year.
- Disbursement rate = disbursements ÷ obligations.
- PPA completion = completed PPAs ÷ PPAs due by the quarter.

The IAT target is at least 80% utilisation of the current-year 70% component (A4.4). PPAs below plan by the end of Q3 are flagged for realignment, with CDRRMC approval and SP concurrence where the law requires it. The quarterly report (FIN-F03, carried in MODE as BUD-F02) goes to the CDRRMC and is posted under the Full Disclosure Policy.

# 8. Special Trust Fund Management

Unexpended LDRRMF accrues to the STF and may be used only for DRRM within five years from the year it accrued; after that it reverts to the general fund (RA 10121 Sec. 21). The STF ledger (FIN-F04) keeps each balance by **year of origin** and shows its deadline, so the oldest money is programmed first. STF balances are also a lawful AA source under RA 12287 IRR Rule V Sec. 1(b), and they are not subject to the 30%/70% graduation.

Programming STF balances follows the same approvals as the 2024 and 2025 Trust Fund plans: CDRRMC approval, then SP adoption or concurrence on City Accountant advice (Res. No. 08, s. 2025).

# 9. Quick Response Fund

The QRF is a standby fund for relief and recovery in areas stricken by a disaster. Under JMC 2013-1 it is used when the City is under a **State of Calamity** declared by the SP (Chapter 1, SOP GOV-04). COA has found LGUs using the QRF without that declaration; this chapter does not allow it. A State of Imminent Disaster under RA 12287 does not open the QRF: anticipatory action is funded from the AA line and other lawful sources (IAT A4.6).

Unused QRF at year-end accrues to the STF. The QRF balance is reported in every quarterly report and after each use (FIN-F05).

# 10. Anticipatory Action Financing under RA 12287

## 10.1 Sources and their rules

| Source | Legal basis | 30%/70% graduation | Approval route |
|---|---|---|---|
| AA line in the LDRRMFIP (≤ 10% of the 70%) | IRR Rule V Sec. 1(b) | Yes, for hydrometeorological and geological hazards | Released under the State of Imminent Disaster per the LDRRMFIP line |
| STF balances within their five-year period | IRR Rule V Sec. 1(b)(i) | No | CDRRMC programming; SP concurrence where required |
| Donations, partners, ODA, other LGUs | IRR Rule V Sec. 1(b)(ii) | No | Per donor terms and SP Res. 2024-102 |
| NDRRM Fund augmentation | IRR Rule V Sec. 1(c) | Per national rules | Request through OCD; President's approval on NDRRMC recommendation |

## 10.2 Release rules

1. Funds are released only while a State of Imminent Disaster declared by the City Mayor is in force (Chapter 1, SOP GOV-05) and only for AA measures listed in the approved PDRA and the 29-item IRR Rule IV checklist.
2. Disbursement is based on the anticipated extent of damage and disruption in the PDRA (Rule V Sec. 2).
3. **Tranche rule** (hydrometeorological and geological hazards): disbursements for interventions to be carried out more than 3 days before forecast impact may not exceed 30% of the AA allocation; up to 70% may be disbursed for interventions within 3 days of impact.
4. **Slow-onset hazards** (e.g., drought): timelines and rates await the NDRRMC operational guidelines. Until then, they are not graduated, but disbursements never exceed the year's AA allocation (ADR-025 item 4).
5. **No Regrets Principle.** Goods bought for AA continue to reach the intended beneficiaries even if the hazard does not materialise (Rule V Sec. 3). Their distribution is recorded as for any other relief.
6. Procurement, budgeting, accounting and auditing rules still apply (Rule V Sec. 2). Where the procurement ground is "time is of the essence", the BAC records it under RA 12009 Sec. 35(b) (SOP 5-07).
7. Unused AA funds accrue to the STF (Rule V Sec. 3).
8. Third parties engaged for AA are paid on a *quantum meruit* basis from the AA allocation (Rule V Sec. 3).

MODE applies rules 3 and 4 from the `aa_rules` table, so a change in national guidelines is a parameter change, not a code change (ADR-025).

## 10.3 Getting the AA line into the plan

The CY2026 plan predates the IRR. Two routes are open, for the City to choose:

- **Amend the current or next-year LDRRMFIP** by CDRRMC resolution and SP action, realigning within the 70% component to create the AA line (precedent: Res. No. 08, s. 2025), or
- **Include it in the FY2028 LDRRMFIP** at the next budget call, under CDP PPA 3.15.

Either way, the line names the AA measures it funds (by hazard and sector, from the contingency plans and the IRR Rule IV checklist), the implementing offices and the procurement mode for each item.

# 11. Procurement for DRRM

## 11.1 Normal procurement

DRRM goods, works and services follow the PPMP → APP → competitive bidding or the appropriate alternative mode, under RA 12009 and its IRR. The CDRRMO prepares the PPMP from the LDRRMFIP and the technical specifications; the BAC runs the procurement.

## 11.2 Pre-arranged supply

Before each typhoon season and at least once a year, the CDRRMO and CGSO, with the BAC Secretariat, keep (SOP 5-06):

- a list of suppliers for relief goods, fuel, water, heavy equipment rental, transport, medicines and medical supplies, and generator service, with capacity and contact position (FIN-F08);
- framework agreements under RA 12009 Sec. 16 where the BAC finds them suitable, stating item, quantity range, unit price or price basis, delivery time, activation trigger and validity;
- standard specifications and price references for recurrent emergency items;
- agreements usable for AA, as RA 12287 IRR Rule IV contemplates (IAT B6.6).

## 11.3 Emergency procurement

Negotiated procurement – emergency cases (RA 12009 Sec. 35(b)) is used only when its ground exists: imminent danger to life or property during a State of Calamity, or time is of the essence because of a calamity and immediate action is needed to prevent loss of life or property or to restore vital public services, infrastructure or utilities. The ground is written on FIN-F07 before the purchase, by the requesting office, and confirmed by the BAC and the HoPE under the RA 12009 IRR. Items are limited to what the emergency requires. Posting and reporting follow the IRR and GPPB rules.

# 12. Donations and External Assistance

Donations in cash or in kind and external financial assistance are accepted, recorded and used as follows (SOP 5-08):

1. One intake point per event, staffed by CSWDO and CGSO with the EOC Logistics or Finance/Administration desk.
2. Acceptance follows SP Res. 2024-102 and COA rules; a deed of donation or acknowledgement receipt is issued (FIN-F09).
3. Cash goes to the City Treasurer and is recorded by the City Accountant under COA Circular No. 2012-002 as a receipt from other sources, kept to its stated purpose.
4. Goods are inspected, valued, recorded and stored by CGSO (or CSWDO for relief items), then issued against distribution records.
5. Conditions set by the donor are respected; unsuitable goods are declined politely at intake.
6. Donations used for AA are not subject to the 30%/70% graduation (IRR Rule V Sec. 2).
7. Receipts, use and balances are disclosed with the quarterly report.

No personal data of beneficiaries is copied into the donations log; distribution lists stay with the CSWDO (RA 10173).

# 13. Resource and Property Accountability

DRRM property bought with the LDRRMF, STF, QRF, AA funds or donations is government property. CGSO records it, issues it to the end user on a Property Acknowledgement Receipt or Inventory Custodian Slip, and includes it in the annual physical count. The CDRRMO:

- keeps a DRRM asset register linked to the EOC resource inventory in MODE (Chapter 6), so the same item has one record;
- returns or transfers property on reassignment or separation of the accountable person;
- reports loss or damage at once, with the incident record, for relief from accountability under COA rules;
- applies the same rules to equipment lent to barangays, with a signed receipt.

Consumables issued in an emergency (fuel, food packs, NFIs) are supported by issue slips, trip tickets and distribution summaries; Chapter 6 sets the logistics procedure.

# 14. Risk Financing and Insurance

The City layers its risk finance: the QRF and the 70% component for frequent, smaller events; the STF and AA funds for imminent events; insurance for damage to public assets; and national funds, contingent credit and partners for catastrophic events. SOP 5-09 tests that layering each year against a severe credible scenario (a Thelma-type flash flood; a Yolanda-strength typhoon) and records the shortfall and the fallback sources.

The CDRA reports that only 15% of critical point facilities are insured and no lifeline utility is insured. Under RA 656, public property is insured with GSIS. CDP PPA 1.3 and 1.2.3 programme insurance of buildings, equipment and early warning devices. The EOC, evacuation centres and DRRM equipment are listed first.

- **Chapter 1** declares the State of Calamity (GOV-04) and the State of Imminent Disaster (GOV-05) that open the QRF and AA funds; the CDRRMC approves plans.
- **Chapter 2** staffs the Administration and Research and Planning roles that run this chapter (its sec. 2.16.2 budget cycle is carried out through SOPs 5-01 and 5-02).
- **Chapter 3** sets the LDRRMP and contingency plans that the LDRRMFIP funds and the adequacy test uses.
- **Chapter 4** raises resource requests (SOP 4-07, EOC-F06); expenditure follows this chapter.
- **Chapter 6** runs logistics, warehousing and the resource inventory; this chapter keeps the money and property records.
- **Chapter 7** keeps corrective actions and performance reviews.
- **Chapter 8** finances recovery beyond the QRF.

# 21. Conclusion

The City already sets aside and approves its DRRM fund. The work now is to make it reachable at the right moment: an anticipatory action line before impact, a QRF under its proper trigger after impact, suppliers agreed before the season, and records that tell the CDRRMC, the public and COA what each peso did.
