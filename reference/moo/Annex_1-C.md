**AN EXECUTIVE ORDER ESTABLISHING THE ORMOC CITY EMERGENCY OPERATIONS CENTER, PROVIDING FOR ITS ORGANIZATION, ACTIVATION LEVELS, OPERATING ARRANGEMENTS, INFORMATION MANAGEMENT, RESOURCE COORDINATION AND INTERFACE WITH THE INCIDENT COMMAND SYSTEM**

### Section 1. Establishment

The Ormoc City Emergency Operations Center (EOC) is hereby established as the City's principal facility and coordination mechanism for emergency monitoring, information management, inter-office coordination, resource support, decision support and operational reporting.

The EOC shall be maintained and managed by the CDRRMO under the supervision of the City Mayor and in accordance with the CDRRMC's policies and the Manual of Operations.

### Section 2. Purpose

The EOC shall:

a. Maintain situational awareness of hazards, threats, incidents and emerging risks;
b. Receive, validate, consolidate and disseminate operational information;
c. Support early warning and public information coordination;
d. Facilitate coordination among City Government offices, barangays, national agencies, utilities, CSOs and private sector partners;
e. Track resource availability, requests, allocation and deployment;
f. Support strategic decision-making by the City Mayor and CDRRMC;
g. Maintain a common operating picture;
h. Support the activation and coordination of response arrangements; and
i. Maintain operational records, reports and lessons learned.

### Section 3. Institutional Relationship

The EOC shall be:

1. Operationally managed by the CDRRMO;
2. Available to support the CDRRMC's policy and coordination functions;
3. Activated through the procedures established in the MOO;
4. Linked to barangay-level monitoring and reporting arrangements;
5. Connected to relevant national and regional warning and coordination systems; and
6. Interoperable with the Incident Command System when activated.

The EOC shall not be construed as:

- A separate local government department;
- A substitute for the CDRRMC;
- A substitute for the CDRRMO;
- A field command post unless expressly designated for a specific incident and appropriately organized; or
- An authority empowered to override the Incident Commander or the lawful command structure of responding agencies.

### Section 4. EOC Activation Levels

The EOC shall operate under the following activation levels. The exact criteria and triggers shall be detailed in the EOC MOO and hazard-specific SOPs.

| Level | Operating condition | Minimum staffing and function |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Level 0 – Routine Monitoring | No imminent threat or major incident; routine monitoring | Duty officer, monitoring and communications personnel, scheduled reporting |
| Level 1 – Enhanced Monitoring | Elevated threat, weather disturbance, warning advisory or developing incident | Expanded monitoring, focal-person notification, situation updates and readiness checks |
| Level 2 – Partial Activation | Significant incident or credible threat requiring multi-office coordination | Core EOC team, operations and planning functions, resource tracking and regular situation reports |
| Level 3 – Full Activation | Major emergency, multiple affected areas, significant resource demand or City-level coordination requirement | Full EOC staffing, executive coordination, structured information and resource management, continuous operational reporting |

The activation level shall be determined by the authorized official under the approved activation protocol, based on verified information, hazard-specific thresholds and the scale and complexity of the situation.

The EOC activation level shall not automatically determine the activation level of an ICS organization. The EOC and ICS may be activated at different scales depending on incident needs.

### Section 5. Authority to Activate

The City Mayor shall have the authority to direct EOC activation, consistent with law and the City's approved protocols.

The City DRRM Officer or designated duty officer may initiate or recommend activation based on verified hazard information, official warnings, field reports or other credible evidence.

In urgent situations, the designated duty officer may initiate immediate notification and minimum readiness measures under the approved protocol, subject to prompt confirmation by the authorized official.

The activation protocol shall specify who may approve activation, who may act in the absence of the primary authority, and how the decision shall be recorded.

### Section 6. EOC Functional Organization

The EOC shall be organized around the following functions, scaled according to activation level:

1. EOC Management / Coordination – oversees EOC operations, internal coordination and reporting to the authorized city leadership;
2. Operations – consolidates field information, tracks operational activities and coordinates with response agencies and barangays;
3. Planning and Situation Analysis – maintains the common operating picture, situation reports, projections and planning products;
4. Logistics – coordinates resource availability, requests, procurement or deployment referrals, facilities, transport and support services;
5. Administration and Finance – supports documentation of expenditures, personnel time, administrative requirements and financial records;
6. Public Information and Risk Communication – coordinates validated public information, warning messages, media releases and community advisories; and
7. Safety and Liaison – supports personnel safety coordination and liaison with partner agencies, as appropriate to the scale of operations.

The EOC functional organization may be adapted to the incident, but assigned functions and reporting relationships shall be documented.

### Section 7. EOC Manager

The City DRRM Officer shall designate an EOC Manager for each activation, subject to the approval arrangements in the MOO, and corresponding Contingency Plan.

The EOC Manager shall:

a. Direct the internal functioning of the EOC;
b. Ensure that assigned functional positions are staffed;
c. Maintain coordination with the City Mayor, CDRRMC and Incident Commander, as appropriate;
d. Ensure that information is validated and properly disseminated;
e. Maintain the common operating picture and action tracker;
f. Coordinate resource requests and inter-office support;
g. Ensure the preparation and submission of situation reports; and
h. Ensure the orderly deactivation and archiving of EOC records.

The EOC Manager is responsible for EOC coordination and administration and does not automatically become the Incident Commander.

### Section 8. Incident Command System Interface

Where an incident requires an ICS structure, the designated Incident Commander shall exercise incident-level command and control in accordance with applicable ICS rules and the activated incident organization.

The EOC shall:

- Receive and consolidate situation reports from the Incident Command Post;
- Facilitate resource requests and inter-agency coordination;
- Provide policy and strategic information to the City Mayor and CDRRMC;
- Maintain citywide situational awareness;
- Coordinate support from offices not directly assigned to the incident organization; and
- Avoid issuing conflicting tactical instructions to field teams.

The Incident Commander shall communicate operational priorities, resource requirements and incident developments through the established reporting channel.

### Section 9. Barangay and Community Reporting Interface

The EOC shall maintain a designated reporting and communications arrangement with all barangays.

Each barangay shall identify authorized focal persons and alternates for:

a. Hazard monitoring and local observations;
b. Receipt and acknowledgment of warnings;
c. Reporting of incidents and affected populations;
d. Evacuation and protective action updates;
e. Requests for assistance and resources; and
f. Confirmation of service restoration and return conditions.

The CDRRMO shall establish backup communication methods for barangays with weak or disrupted telecommunications.

### Section 10. Information Management and Common Operating Picture

The EOC shall maintain a Common Operating Picture (COP) based on verified and time-stamped information.

At a minimum, the COP shall contain:

- Incident and hazard status;
- Geographic location and affected areas;
- Official warning and advisory status;
- Population and facilities potentially or actually affected;
- Evacuation status and displacement information;
- Critical infrastructure and lifeline status;
- Response actions and responsible agencies;
- Resource availability, requests and deployment status;
- Access constraints and urgent needs; and
- Key decisions, outstanding actions and next reporting deadlines.

Information shall be classified as verified, provisional or unverified. Unverified information shall not be presented as confirmed fact in public advisories or executive situation reports.

### Section 11. Communications and Public Information

The EOC shall coordinate with authorized public information offices and relevant technical agencies to ensure that public advisories are accurate, timely, consistent and accessible.

Warnings shall use appropriate languages and formats for affected communities, including Cebuano/Ormocanon and accessible formats where needed.

A warning message shall, at minimum, identify:

1. The hazard and affected location;
2. The expected timing and level of threat;
3. The population or sector at risk;
4. The required protective action;
5. The responsible source or issuing authority;
6. The time of issuance and next update; and
7. Available assistance or reporting channels.

### Section 12. Resource Management

The EOC shall maintain a resource status and tracking system covering personnel, equipment, vehicles, supplies, facilities and other available response resources.

All requests, approvals, dispatches, transfers, receipts and returns shall be documented.

The EOC shall coordinate resource support but shall not bypass the procurement, accounting, property, budget and audit controls applicable to the City Government.

### Section 13. Duty Roster and Continuous Operations

The CDRRMO shall maintain an approved EOC duty roster and alternate roster.

The roster shall specify:

- Primary and alternate personnel;
- Duty schedules;
- Reporting and handover requirements;
- Contact details and escalation channels;
- Minimum staffing per activation level;
- Rest and relief arrangements; and
- Access to EOC facilities and equipment.

### Section 14. Training, Exercises and Evaluation

The City shall conduct periodic EOC orientation, functional exercises, communications tests and full-scale or multi-agency exercises as appropriate.

After each activation or exercise, the CDRRMO shall conduct an After-Action Review and prepare an Improvement Plan identifying corrective actions, owners, deadlines and verification requirements.

### Section 15. Initial Implementation Requirements

Within sixty (60) days from effectivity, the CDRRMO shall submit for approval:

1. The EOC organization chart and functional position descriptions;
2. EOC activation and deactivation SOP;
3. EOC duty roster and succession arrangements;
4. Communications and contact directory;
5. Common Operating Picture and situation report templates;
6. Resource request and tracking forms;
7. EOC–ICS interface protocol;
8. Barangay reporting and last-mile communication protocol; and
9. Minimum equipment, facility and continuity requirements.

### Section 16. Effectivity

This Executive Order shall take effect immediately upon signing and shall be disseminated to all concerned offices and partner institutions.
