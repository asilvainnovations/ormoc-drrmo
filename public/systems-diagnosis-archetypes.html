1. How to Read These Diagrams: Six Core Building Blocks

Systems thinking looks past one-off emergencies to see the hidden circles of cause and effect driving daily work. Here is what each symbol in the interactive diagrams below means in plain language.

Causal Loop Diagrams (CLDs)

A visual map showing how actions and conditions feed back into one another in closed circles, rather than ending in a straight line.

In Ormoc Short-term storm fixes affect whether permanent ordinances get passed later.

Polarities (+ and −)

+ (Same): When the first item grows, the second grows too. − (Opposite): When the first grows, the second shrinks.

In Ormoc More staff overload (−) cuts 24/7 readiness; clear SOPs (−) cut field confusion.

Reinforcing Loops (R)

A self-multiplying cycle where every turn pushes things further in the same direction—either building momentum or worsening a crisis.

In Ormoc Command confusion delays support, which makes the incident even harder to manage.

Balancing Loops (B)

A stabilizing cycle—like a thermostat—that reacts to a gap or problem and pulls the system back toward control or a limit.

In Ormoc Emergency Executive Orders quickly calm an immediate typhoon disruption, then stop.

Delays ( || )

Parallel hash bars across an arrow mark a waiting period before an action pays off—causing people to give up or overreact too early.

In Ormoc Drafting, reviewing, and passing an enabling City Ordinance takes months, not hours.

Systems Archetypes

Well-known combinations of R and B loops that explain why hardworking teams see the same headaches return year after year.

In Ormoc Four archetypes explain 41.8% compliance despite active typhoon response.
